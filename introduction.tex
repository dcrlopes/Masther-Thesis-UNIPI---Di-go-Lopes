\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

\section{Context and motivation}
\label{sec:intro-context}

Small modular reactors (SMRs) have taken a growing place in nuclear
engineering over recent years. Two forces drive this growth.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The rising electricity demand of data centres and other new
        large loads.

  \item The search for shorter construction times and lower capital
        costs than those of large nuclear power plants.
\end{enumerate}

Within the SMR family, pressurised-water designs of naval heritage form
one of the most technologically mature branches. Compact pressurised
water reactors (PWRs) have propelled naval vessels since the beginning
of nuclear power history, so their design base is long and well
exercised.

In Brazil, the Navy's nuclear propulsion programme is anchored by
LABGENE (\emph{Laborat\'orio de Gera\c{c}\~ao de Energia
Nucleoel\'etrica}), a land-based prototype plant. Its openly disclosed
characteristics define the reference envelope of the present
work~\cite{dados_abertos_2026}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item A pressurised water reactor of 48\,MW thermal power.

  \item Low-enriched uranium (LEU) fuel, below 20\,wt\% $^{235}$U.

  \item A refuelling interval of the order of five years.
\end{itemize}

A deliberate methodological choice governs everything that follows:
\textbf{the study is built exclusively from open sources}.

Every geometric dimension, material specification and operating
parameter of the reference core either appears in the compiled
open-data dossier of the LABGENE and SNCA
programmes~\cite{dados_abertos_2026} or is reconstructed from
international open literature on comparable reactors, notably the
NuScale-class fuel design~\cite{nuscale_htp2_2017}.

Where a required quantity is not openly available, it is estimated from
published proportions, and each estimate is documented as an explicit
assumption.

This provenance discipline serves two purposes.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It keeps the work fully publishable.

  \item It turns the thesis into a demonstration of how far modern
        open-source tools and open data can carry a realistic
        naval-core design study.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Problem statement}
\label{sec:intro-problem}

A long refuelling interval and a flat power distribution pull a PWR
core design in opposite directions.

Reaching a multi-year cycle under the LEU ceiling demands high fuel
enrichment and therefore high beginning-of-life (BOL) reactivity, which
must be suppressed by burnable absorbers and by leakage.

At the same time the radial peaking factor $F_{\Delta H}$, the
thermal-margin quantity of first interest in this work, is degraded by
exactly the enrichment zoning and the absorber placement that the
cycle-length objective motivates.

The design problem is therefore genuinely multi-objective. Its
evaluation function, the coupled neutron transport and depletion
calculation, is computationally expensive and, in Monte Carlo form,
\emph{stochastic}.

The thesis addresses the resulting question: how should a
surrogate-assisted multi-objective optimisation (MOO) campaign be
constructed so that its Pareto front is trustworthy when each objective
evaluation costs hours of computing time and carries statistical noise?

\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:intro-objectives}

The general objective is to develop, execute and critically evaluate a
surrogate-assisted multi-objective optimisation of a LABGENE-class
48\,MWth Small Modular Reactor (SMR) core, built in the open-source
Monte Carlo code OpenMC~\cite{romano_openmc_2015} from open data.

The optimisation maximises the cycle length in effective full-power
days (EFPD) and minimises the radial peaking factor $F_{\Delta H}$
under licensing-motivated constraints.

The cycle length measures how long the core sustains full-power
operation before it loses the reactivity needed to remain critical. Its
formal definition, through the conversion between burnup and
irradiation time, is given in Section~\ref{sec:bg-depletion}.

The radial peaking factor measures how unevenly power is distributed
among the fuel rods. It compares the most heavily loaded rod against
the average of the evaluation domain, so a value close to unity
indicates a flat radial power distribution and a large value indicates
a hot rod that consumes thermal margin. Its formal definition is given
in Section~\ref{sec:bg-peaking}, and the numerical estimator actually
used, together with its evaluation domain, in
Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}.

The two objectives conflict, for the physical reasons outlined in
Section~\ref{sec:intro-problem}. The purpose of the optimisation is
therefore to map the trade-off between them rather than to find a
single best design.

The specific objectives, in the order the work addresses them, are the
following.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Construct a parametric OpenMC model of the reference core at
        pin, assembly and 32-assembly core fidelity, and verify its
        internal geometric and neutronic consistency.

  \item Formulate the optimisation over five design variables, namely
        inner enrichment, outer enrichment, gadolinia (Gd$_2$O$_3$)
        loading, pin pitch and reflector thickness, subject to five
        constraints that include a geometric vessel-fit condition and
        bounds on beginning-of-life (BOL) reactivity.

  \item Couple a Gaussian-process (GP) surrogate to the Non-dominated
        Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) in an active-learning
        loop governed by empirical stopping rules.

  \item Impose an accident-tolerant-fuel (ATF) discharge-burnup limit
        and quantify its reshaping of the Pareto front.

  \item Characterise the statistical behaviour of the Monte Carlo
        peaking estimator, both its noise \emph{and} its bias, and
        develop the selection methodology that identifies true optima
        beyond that noise.

  \item Extract decision-relevant structure from the campaign archive,
        specifically:
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item the feasibility envelopes of the design space,
          \item the variable importance ranked by decision perspective,
          \item a small set of physically distinct candidate designs
                for high-fidelity confirmation.
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro-contributions}

Beyond the reference design study, the work contributes four
methodological results that generalise to surrogate-assisted
optimisation with expensive, stochastic, reduced-order evaluations.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item An exact post-processing transform that imposes a
        discharge-burnup licensing limit on a completed campaign
        without re-evaluation.

  \item A quantified demonstration that a maximum-over-cells Monte
        Carlo objective is noisy \emph{and} biased upward. The bias
        exceeded the Pareto-front spread and inverted its ranking. A
        two-stage noise-margin subset-selection procedure that
        resolves true optima accompanies the demonstration.

  \item A measured proxy-fidelity result. The single-assembly peaking
        proxy correlates with the true core quantity globally, with a
        Spearman coefficient $\rho_S = +0.890$ over the full archive,
        yet scrambles the ordering in the near-optimal set. It carries
        a design-dependent gap of $1.75$--$1.91\times$ anti-correlated
        with reflector thickness, and it turned an inactive constraint
        into one violated by every design. The remedy adopted,
        evaluating the true core quantity inside the loop at
        $\approx3\%$ cost, reframes proxy validation as a rank-fidelity
        requirement.

  \item The reactivity-feasibility structure of the design space.
        Volume-weighted enrichment governs the envelope, the fixed
        twelve-pin gadolinia pattern bounds absorber authority at
        $\Delta k \approx 0.05$, and the measured conflict between
        core-level peaking and the reactivity limit identifies absorber
        \emph{inventory}, that is the poisoned-pin count, as the
        missing design freedom.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:intro-scope}

The optimisation workhorse is a two-dimensional model. Depletion is
computed on the reflective single assembly, leakage-corrected through a
calibrated reactivity target. From Campaign 4 onward, the
beginning-of-life peaking objective is evaluated on the full
32-assembly core.

Three classes of physics are outside the model.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Thermal-hydraulic feedback.

  \item Transient safety parameters.

  \item Axial effects.
\end{enumerate}

The core is a fresh, uniform, single-batch loading with no
loading-pattern freedom. These boundaries are stated where they bear on
results and are collected in the concluding chapter.

\section{Thesis outline}
\label{sec:intro-outline}

Chapter~\ref{ch:litreview} reviews the SMR landscape, burnable-absorber
practice, surrogate-assisted optimisation, noisy objectives and
multi-fidelity methods. Chapter~\ref{ch:background} assembles the
theory the methodology rests on and hosts the formal definitions of the
quantities used throughout the thesis. Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}
presents the model, the optimisation formulation and the statistical
machinery. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports Campaigns 1--3, the
core-level validation that redirected the work, the running Campaign 4
and the Campaign-5 proposal, with open items flagged explicitly. The
final chapter draws conclusions and future work.
