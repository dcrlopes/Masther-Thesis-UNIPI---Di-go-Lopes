\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results}

\section{Campaigns 1 and 2: workflow verification and the estimator}
\label{sec:res-c1c2}

The first two campaigns were executed on the cloud instance of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-diagnostics}, at the transport settings of
Table~\ref{tab:fidelity}, with 72 and 84 evaluations and 1008 and 2184
transport solves. Campaign 1 ran a fixed depletion schedule of 14
states with four constraints, and Campaign 2 the adaptive schedule of
10 to 28 states with five, the vessel-fit constraint having entered
between them.

\subsection{Campaign 1: two structural findings}
\label{sec:res-c1}

Campaign 1 sampled the full design space of
Table~\ref{tab:design-space} at the low-fidelity setting of
$4\,000\times60$ (20 inactive) and produced 46
feasible designs of 72, the 26 rejections being 25 violations of the
upper reactivity bound and one of the peaking constraint. It is retained in
the record for two structural findings rather than for its front.

The first is geometric. All four designs of its Pareto front carry
reflector thicknesses between 23.99 and \SI{24.99}{cm}, while the
analytic vessel envelope admits at most \SI{19.5}{cm} at the most
favourable pitch, so every nominally optimal design exceeds the vessel
budget by at least \SI{4.5}{cm}. The optimiser exploited the one
physical requirement that the constraint set did not encode, and the
vessel-fit constraint $g_\mathrm{geom}$ of
Equation~\eqref{eq:renv} has been enforced ever since.

The second emerged only from the recovered archive. The depletion
horizon maps onto a cycle-length limit through the constant specific
power.

\begin{equation}
  t_\mathrm{hor} = \frac{B_\mathrm{hor}}{P_\mathrm{spec}}
  \label{eq:depletion-horizon}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $t_\mathrm{hor}$ is the maximum reportable cycle length
in EFPD, $B_\mathrm{hor}$ the depletion horizon in MWd/kgHM and
$P_\mathrm{spec}$ the specific power of
Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}. Campaign 1 ran with the depletion
schedule that the evaluator applies by default, which stops at a fixed
horizon. The horizon is not recorded in the archive, but 39 of the 72
evaluations report the same cycle length, \SI{3555.9}{EFPD}, and
Equation~\eqref{eq:depletion-horizon} converts that value back to a
horizon of \SI{35.5}{MWd/kgHM}.

Those 39 depletions stopped before their assembly reached the
end-of-cycle target, so their cycle length is right-censored, the
recorded value being a lower bound identical for every censored design. The cycle-length
objective therefore carries no information over that majority of the
archive, and the non-dominated sorting of those designs is decided by
the peaking factor alone.

\subsection{Campaign 2: the horizon test and the estimator}
\label{sec:res-c2}

Campaign 2 corrected both defects, added the tabulated $k$-target
calibration and the adaptive depletion schedule, raised the horizon
from 35.5 to \SI{100}{MWd/kgHM} and completed 84 evaluations. It
returned 44 feasible designs of 84, the 40 rejections being 39
violations of the upper reactivity bound of 1.35 and one of the peaking
constraint.

Raising the horizon tested whether the censoring was an artefact of the
stopping value or a property of the design space, and the answer is the
second. The censored fraction moved from \SI{54.2}{\%} to
\SI{59.5}{\%}, and all 50 censored
designs again report the identical converted horizon of
\SI{10016.7}{EFPD}, the closest resolved design ending within
\SI{0.08}{\%} of it. Tripling the horizon therefore did not resolve the
objective, because the degeneracy is generated by the design space
itself, in which enrichments near the limit combined with thick
reflectors sustain criticality well beyond any operationally meaningful
cycle. Such cycle lengths exceed the reported refuelling interval by a
factor of five to ten (Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-mission}) and measure a
reactivity inventory that the controllability constraint later shows to be
beyond the regulating banks. The remedy adopted from Campaign 3 is the
discharge limit of Section~\ref{sec:meth-atf}, which replaces the
arbitrary horizon by a design-basis bound.

Figure~\ref{fig:c2-space} shows both facts on the archive. The left
panel is the objective plane of the campaign, with 50 of the 84 designs
stacked on the horizon, and the feasible non-dominated set reduced to
six designs of which four sit on that line. The right panel plots the
design vectors of every phase in parallel coordinates, each variable
normalised to its own bounds. The design of experiments fills the box,
while every infill iteration proposes the same structure: enrichments
in the upper half of their range, the pin pitch at its lower bound and
the reflector thickness split between designs at the upper bound and
designs at the lower one. The optimiser is therefore not exploring the
box, it is pressing on the two variables that buy reactivity, which is
what the degenerate objective rewards.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/th_c2_space.pdf}
  \caption{Campaign 2: objective plane with the depletion horizon, and the design vectors of each phase.}
  \label{fig:c2-space}
\end{figure}

Campaign 2 also exposed the estimator pathology. The stored
single-seed peaking values
carried a bias larger than the front spread they ranked, and resolving
the candidates at high fidelity re-ordered them: the stored fourth
became the true first, the apparent winner fell to seventh, and the
pair separated at $|t| = 12.8$. The ordering is therefore not preserved
between the two fidelities. Replicating the three lowest-peaking
front designs at the same $4\,000\times60$ setting with five seeds each
gives a pooled seed-to-seed standard deviation of $0.015 \pm 0.003$,
against a bias of $-0.07$ relative to the reference setting. The treatment that this finding forced,
per-design seeding and the fidelity study, is specified in
Section~\ref{sec:meth-fidelity}.

That treatment was calibrated on the same designs. Repeating the
Campaign 2 front and the Campaign 3 candidates at four transport
settings with independent seeds gives the noise and the bias of the
assembly estimator at each of them (Table~\ref{tab:fid-ladder}).
Between $16\,000$ and $256\,000$ particles the noise follows the
$1/\sqrt{N}$ law of independent histories, at a fitted exponent of
$-0.48$. The step from $4\,000\times60$ to $16\,000\times120$
multiplies the active histories by nine and, on the one design solved
at all three settings, lowers the bias from $+0.055$ to $+0.0074$, by
\SI{87}{\%}, and the relative error of the hottest-pin tally from 3.6
to \SI{1.1}{\%}. Each further factor of four in histories halves the
noise at 2.5 to 3.7 times the wall time, which is why
$16\,000\times120$ was adopted for Campaigns 3 and 5. The bias
reduction rests on the one and two designs also solved at the reference
setting, and the reference itself retains a residual bias of about
$+0.005$, so the bias column is relative to it.

\section{Campaign 3: design of experiments and feasibility envelope}
\label{sec:res-c3doe}

Campaign 3 ran 72 evaluations at $16\,000\times120$ with per-design
seeds and the discharge-burnup limit of Section~\ref{sec:meth-atf}
applied inside the loop, at \SI{75}{MWd/kgHM} in this campaign. Its
36-point Latin hypercube returns 11 feasible designs, \SI{31}{\%}, and
the upper reactivity bound is responsible for 21 of the 25 rejections.

Figure~\ref{fig:c3-space} shows the campaign from two sides. The left
panel is the archive in the plane the campaign optimises, where
discharge burnup and cycle length are proportional at constant specific
power, so the horizontal axis is the cycle-length objective read in the
unit the limit acts on. The right panel plots the design vectors of
every phase in parallel coordinates, each variable normalised to its own
bounds.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/th_c3_space.pdf}
  \caption{Campaign 3: objective plane with the in-loop burnup limit, and the design vectors of each phase.}
  \label{fig:c3-space}
\end{figure}

Three features of the plane carry into the rest of the campaign. Half
the archive, 36 of 72 designs, is feasible, and the infeasible designs
are rejected by the reactivity bound rather than by the peaking limit,
which no design of this campaign violates. Fifty-two designs reach the
limit at \SI{75}{MWd/kgHM} and stack on that line, where the
cycle-length objective is degenerate and dominance is decided by the
peaking factor alone. The feasible non-dominated set is therefore
short, three designs at 51.7, 52.3 and \SI{75.0}{MWd/kgHM} with peaking
factors of 1.1266, 1.1336 and 1.1397, and the design at the limit is
the one the campaign reports as the burnup-limited strategy.

The right panel shows where the loop went once it had learned that
boundary. The design of experiments fills the box, while both infill
blocks leave its corners and concentrate: the pin pitch moves into the
upper half of its range, with a normalised mean of 0.66 and 0.73 against
0.34 in the design of experiments, and the outer enrichment narrows to
4.8 to \SI{11.2}{wt\%} against the 2.0 to \SI{19.0}{wt\%} the design of
experiments sampled. The reflector thickness stays in the lower half
throughout, which is the one variable the campaign does not press
against its bound.

Feasibility itself is governed by the volume-weighted enrichment acting
through the reactivity bound, with the pin pitch as the second lever,
since over-moderation reaches criticality at low enrichment. The
gadolinia fraction is the weakest of the three at the fixed twelve-rod
pattern of this campaign, where it buys
$\Delta k \approx 0.05$ to $0.06$ over its full range, which is
\SI{2.7}{wt\%} of enrichment headroom, against the layouts of up to 40
poisoned rods per assembly studied for soluble-boron-free
cores~\cite{rr_smr_atf_2025,ismr_sbf_2024}. That comparison is what
Campaign 5 acts on when it admits the rod count as a design variable.

\section{Campaign 3: convergence and the resolved front}
\label{sec:res-c3front}

Campaign 3 is the first campaign run under the statistical treatment of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-fidelity}, with a per-design seed and the
high-fidelity search setting of $16\,000\times120$ chosen from the
fidelity study, and with the discharge-burnup limit of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-atf} applied inside the loop. It therefore
answers two questions that Campaigns 1 and 2 left open: whether the
loop converges under the stopping rule of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-loop-stop}, and whether a front resolved at high
fidelity survives the estimator bias that re-ordered the Campaign 2
front.

Figure~\ref{fig:hv} answers the first. The hypervolume rises by
\SI{11.9}{\%} and \SI{1.7}{\%} in the first two iterations of Block 1
and then returns three consecutive exact zeros, so the stopping rule is
met with margin. The two blocks behave differently, and the difference
is the signature of convergence seen from inside the loop. Block 1
returns 18 feasible designs of 18, against 11 of 36 in the design of
experiments, because the surrogate has learned the reactivity boundary,
and only six of its designs stop at the discharge-burnup limit, so the
block walks into the region below it where a trade-off exists. Block 2 returns
7 feasible designs and all 18 at the limit, probing the boundary of a
front that is already stable.

  \centering

  \caption{Campaign 3: hypervolume history and the outcome of each phase.}

\end{figure}

The second question is answered by re-solving the finalists at
$256\,000$ particles with eight seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:c3-resolved} and Figure~\ref{fig:plateau}). The stored
peaking values fall by 0.003 to 0.020, by an amount that depends on the
design, and the re-ordering of Campaign 2 therefore repeats here. What
the campaign resolves is not a winner but a plateau: four designs that
are statistically identical in $F_{\Delta H}$, from 1.1212 to 1.1244,
spanning 2904 to \SI{5902}{EFPD}, with the burnup-limited strategy at
\SI{7513}{EFPD} led by C3-45, which is itself not separated from the
plateau once the bias is corrected, at $|t| = 2.6$.

Two engineering strategies share that plateau, and both are shaped by
the same scarce resource, the \SI{90}{cm} vessel radius of

forward, because the peaking factor of this campaign is still the
assembly value, which Section~\ref{sec:res-corevalidation} shows to
rank designs differently from the core.

\section{Core-level validation: the model discrepancy}
\label{sec:res-corevalidation}

Rebuilding the finalists as full cores at beginning of life measures
what the reflective assembly cannot see
(Table~\ref{tab:core-finalists}).

  \begin{tabular}{lccccc}

    & $F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}$ & $F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}$
      (95\% CI) & seeds & ratio & EFPD \\
    \midrule
    C3-46 & 1.1397 & $2.013\pm0.018$ & 8 & 1.77 & 7\,513 \\

    C3-53 & 1.1445 & $2.114\pm0.018$ & 8 & 1.85 & 5\,902 \\
    C3-18 & 1.1270 & $2.126\pm0.018$ & 3 & 1.89 & 2\,904 \\
    C3-51 & 1.1266 & $2.153\pm0.015$ & 3 & 1.91 & 5\,180 \\
    C3-49 & 1.1336 & $2.159\pm0.032$ & 3 & 1.90 & 5\,239 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}

Three facts. The model discrepancy is a factor \textbf{1.75--1.91},
anti-correlated with reflector thickness ($r=-0.96$ over the six
finalists): thin reflectors leak, depress the periphery, and drive
core-wide peaking, an effect invisible to an infinite lattice. The ranking
\emph{inverts}: over the six finalists solved at core level the Spearman
rank correlation between the bias-corrected assembly value and the core
value is $-0.94$, and the assembly-best design, C3-49 at
\num{1.1212}, is core-worst at \num{2.159}. The burnup-limited
pair (C3-46 and C3-45, tied at $|t|=0.54$) beats the plateau at
$|t|\approx7$--$8$, collapsing the front to the burnup-limited strategy. And the
peaking constraint, inactive everywhere at assembly level, is now
\textbf{violated by every design}: the best sits at
$2.013\pm0.018$ against the 2.0 limit.

Rescoring the \emph{entire} feasible archive (36 designs,
Fig.~\ref{fig:asmcore}) generalises the finding. The proxy is globally
informative, with $\rho=+0.890$, yet scrambles the ordering exactly where
the optimiser operates. The designs it mis-ranks move by three to nine places, with ranks taken
among
the 36 feasible Campaign 3 designs.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{images/th_asm_vs_core}
  \caption{Every feasible Campaign 3 design rescored at core level.}
  \label{fig:asmcore}
\end{figure}

The bridge model (Eq.~\ref{eq:bridge}) fitted on all 36 designs,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{F^\mathrm{core}}{F^\mathrm{asm}}
  = 1.400 \;-\; 0.0045\,t_\mathrm{refl} \;+\; 0.343\,p,
  \qquad R^2 = 0.746,
  \label{eq:bridge-fit}
\end{equation}
confirms reflector and pitch as the corrupting variables, and its
residual quarter is the argument for measuring rather than correcting.
The fit describes the uniform loading of Campaign~3 only. On the zoned
core that the evaluator builds from Campaign~6 the ratio is smaller and
no longer depends on the geometry, as
Section~\ref{sec:res-proxy-gap-zoned} reports.

Closing the leakage-corrected target of Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget}
on the same finalists, that is comparing the target the campaign used
with the core eigenvalue measured on each design, gives residuals of
$-91$ to \SI{-429}{pcm}, most negative for the thick reflectors, which
validates the cycle-length proxy at about the \SI{2}{\%} level.

\textbf{The methodological statement of the thesis follows:} a
surrogate-assisted optimisation on a reduced-order proxy must validate
the proxy's \emph{rank fidelity} near the optimum, not merely its
statistical convergence, because correlation weakens exactly where the
optimiser converges, and it did so here twice (estimator bias in
Campaign 2, geometric proxy in Campaign 3).

\section{Campaign 4: the core-level objective}
\label{sec:res-c4}

Campaign 4 moves $F_{\Delta H}$ and $g_\mathrm{peak}$ to core level at
$\approx3\%$ evaluation cost (Eq.~\ref{eq:corecost}) and is complete:
a 36-point DOE plus three infill iterations of six evaluations each,
54 designs in total, \textbf{zero feasible}
(Table~\ref{tab:c4-doe}, Fig.~\ref{fig:c4traj}). With an empty feasible set
the hypervolume is undefined and is reported as zero, so convergence is
judged on the reduction of the constraint violation. On the core basis
the peaking limit is the constraint that binds: 47 of the 54 designs
violate it, against 40 that violate the upper reactivity bound. Campaign 5
repeats the pattern more sharply, with no feasible design of 60, 57
violations of the peaking limit, 31 of the bound and 2 of the floor.
That is the finding the radial zoning of Campaign 6 addresses.

The infill ran under constraint-violation ranking throughout, since no
design was ever feasible, and its trajectory is the central measurement
of the campaign. The optimiser collapsed the reactivity spread of the
design of experiments by a factor of 200 and then walked
$k_\mathrm{BOL}$ down through its limit. Iteration 2 reached designs
feasible on peaking, $F_{\Delta H} = 1.947$ to 2.010, that missed the
reactivity limit by $+0.0055$ to $+0.0067$, and iteration 3 satisfied
the reactivity limit at $k_\mathrm{BOL} = 1.3494$ to 1.3507 and lost
peaking feasibility in exchange. Within this five-variable space the
two constraints are therefore tangent and not overlapping: each is
satisfiable alone, their intersection was never reached, and the
residual gap at the point of closest approach is
$\Delta k \approx 0.006$.

Every design of iterations 2 and 3 pins three of the five variables at
their bounds, the gadolinia fraction at \SI{8.0}{wt\%}, the pitch at
\SI{1.150}{cm} and the reflector at the vessel-budget bound of
Equation~\eqref{eq:renv}, leaving the enrichment as the only degree of
freedom, which the optimiser walks from 11.82 to \SI{11.50}{wt\%}. The
constrained optimum sits at a vertex of the design box, and the
optimiser is in effect requesting more absorber than the concentration
bound permits. The resolution, the absorber inventory as a design
variable, follows in Section~\ref{sec:res-campaign5}. Over the 54
designs the volume-weighted enrichment governs the core peaking factor,
with $r = -0.59$, while the gadolinia fraction is peaking-neutral at
$r = -0.16$, which is why the conflict is structural.

Two limits bound these results. The designs at \SI{1.150}{cm} carry the
guide-tube clipping caveat of Section~\ref{sec:res-c7-limits}, and 46
of the 54 designs reach the discharge-burnup limit, so the objective plane
degenerates and the campaign is effectively a constrained minimisation
of the core peaking factor. The infill also exposed two defects of the
framework, an inconsistency of \SI{0.02}{cm} between the analytic
vessel constraint and the geometry builder, corrected as recorded in
Section~\ref{sec:meth-geom}, and a failure of the optimiser on an empty
feasible set.

\section{Campaigns 5 to 7: inventory, acquisition and controllability}
\label{sec:res-c5-c7}
%% labels of the material summarised here, kept so that the
%% cross-references of the other chapters still resolve
\label{sec:res-campaign5}\label{eq:pins-needed}\label{tab:c5}\label{fig:c5pins}\label{sec:res-c5-search}\label{eq:violation-normalised}\label{fig:c5traj}\label{fig:c5lobes}\label{sec:res-c5-corrected}\label{fig:c5objcorr}\label{tab:c5-champions}\label{eq:kmin-selfcons}\label{sec:res-c5-kbasis}\label{fig:c5kbasis}\label{tab:c5-sweep}\label{sec:c5_confirmation}\label{tab:c5_confirmation}\label{fig:restart_defect}\label{tab:restart_experiment}


Three campaigns separate the core-level objective of Campaign 4 from the
candidate campaign of Campaign 8. Each made one change to the problem or to
the loop, and each is reported here by that change and its outcome.

Campaign 5 admitted the number of poisoned pins as a design variable,
between 12 and 40 rods in octant-symmetric patterns. The prediction from
Campaign 4 was that about two further pins would close the residual gap to
feasibility. A single-design test confirmed it: feasibility was reached at
14 rods, with $k_\mathrm{BOL} = 1.3456$ and a measured absorber worth of
about \SI{500}{pcm} per rod. The minimum total constraint violation of the
archive fell from 0.0077 to 0.0012. Two further corrections were established
in the same campaign, the normalisation of the constraint violation by each
limit, and the move of the upper reactivity bound to the core basis, which admits
the same designs as the assembly bound at 1.40. Re-scored on the core
basis, the Campaign 4 and Campaign 5 archives hold seven and two
feasible designs, against none on the assembly basis at the bound of
1.35. The two bases do not differ by a constant: across the design space the leakage gap between
them varies between about \num{4300} and \SI{8060}{pcm} and grows with
the reflector thickness and the pitch, so a fixed assembly-basis bound
is inconsistently strict in a way that depends on two of the design
variables themselves.

Campaign 5 also exposed a defect in the restarted depletion, described
with its correction in Section~\ref{sec:restart_defect}. It halved the
effective depletion horizon of the affected campaigns: archive index 7,
for example, depleted in nine blocks and lost one step of
\SI{4.0}{MWd/kgHM} at each of the eight restarts, so its recorded
horizon of \SI{75.0}{MWd/kgHM} corresponds to \SI{43.0}{MWd/kgHM} of
physical burnup and its recorded \num{7513} effective full power days
to \num{4307}.

The peripheral multiplier of Section~\ref{sec:meth-zoning-box} was set
on two Campaign 5 designs. Radial zoning at $m_P = 1.150$ lowered their
radial peaking factor from 2.026 and 2.043 under uniform loading to
1.561, and $m_P = 1.250$ lowered it by a further 0.06 to 0.09, to 1.501
and 1.473. The larger multiplier was rejected because it lowers the
upper enrichment bound of Equation~\eqref{eq:leu-box} in the same
proportion, from \SI{17.174}{wt\%} to \SI{15.800}{wt\%}, and the
enrichment limit was already active: under a zoned map at
$m_P = 1.075$, 10 of the 60 Campaign 5 designs exceeded it.

Campaign 6 applied the corrected basis, the evaluator-level radial zoning and
the raised depletion horizon, and used its own 114 evaluations as a test bench
for the acquisition function. The raised horizon leaves one design of the
42-point design of experiments censored, and that design is infeasible on the
upper reactivity bound in any case. Each of its four infill blocks changed exactly
one element of the acquisition, so the behaviour of each element is
attributable:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The bare uncertainty ranking and the batch diversity left the
        hypervolume at the value of the design of experiments, 1288.09.

  \item The feasibility margin raised it to 1580.05, and the convergence
        block closed at 1601.51.
\end{itemize}

The acquisition of Section~\ref{sec:meth-acq} is therefore the state reached
at the end of Campaign 6, and it is the state used in every later campaign.

The search setting on the surrogate was fixed on the same campaign. The
Gaussian processes were fitted once on the 42 designs of the Campaign 6
design of experiments and held fixed, and the NSGA-II search was repeated
for six combinations of population size and number of generations, with
eight seeds each (Table~\ref{tab:nsga-sens}). The seed-to-seed standard
deviation of the hypervolume at the former setting of $60\times80$,
12.72, is about thirty per cent of the range spanned by all six
settings, against 0.59 at the adopted $300\times400$.

The axial leakage factor of Section~\ref{sec:meth-lax} was measured on
the same archive, on six Campaign 6 designs spanning the excess
reactivity of the design space. It is constant over them, and its mean
of \num{1.0289} is the value applied from Campaign 8.

Campaign 7 replaced the upper reactivity bound, a proxy for controllable excess
reactivity, by a measurement. Every evaluation solves the beginning-of-life
core a second time with the sixteen regulating-bank assemblies inserted, and
the resulting eigenvalue enters the constraint set as the controllability
constraint of Section~\ref{sec:meth-ctrl}. The campaign ran 60 evaluations
without a failed case.

The bound derived from the eight Campaign 6 designs, 1.126, rejected
fourteen designs whose own measured bank worth showed them
controllable, which is the bias of a minimum taken over a small sample.
From Campaign 8 the measured constraint therefore decides feasibility and
the derived value is kept only as a outer bound.

Two findings of Campaign 7 bound what the earlier archives can support:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Four designs reached the active batches with the Shannon entropy of
        the fission source not yet converged. None is on a front, and all are
        excluded from every candidate list.

  \item Twenty-five of the 60 designs sit below a pitch of \SI{1.224}{cm},
        where the lattice clips the guide-tube wall and replaces part of it
        by moderator. The defect reaches back to every earlier design at such
        a pitch. Fixing the pitch at \SI{1.26}{cm} from Campaign 8 removes it
        prospectively, and the affected results carry the caveat.
\end{enumerate}

Fixing the pitch costs part of this front. Five of the six designs of
the controllability-constraint front, and 14 of the 60 designs of the campaign,
cannot be built at \SI{1.26}{cm}, because their low peaking relies on
thick reflectors that fit only at a narrow pitch. The peaking floor
expected of Campaign 8 under the fixed pitch is therefore near 1.60,
against the lower values this campaign reports.

\section{Campaign 8: the candidate campaign}
\label{sec:res-c8}

Campaign 8 is the last campaign of the original formulation, and its
front is the set of candidate cores under that formulation. It fixes what the earlier campaigns established and
measures what they could not. It ran 60 evaluations on wks720 in
\SI{21.02}{h}, every one of them completing without error, and every
cycle length it reports refers for the first time in this work to a
finite-height core, through the axially corrected target of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ktarget-3d}.

\paragraph{The finite-height target}
\label{sec:res-c8-target}

The axially corrected target of Section~\ref{sec:meth-lax} is what
makes these cycle lengths finite-height values, and its cost is
measured here. Averaged over the seven reflector nodes of the campaign
box, the two-dimensional target is 1.05256 and the three-dimensional
one 1.08298, the latter running from 1.0839 at \SI{2.0}{cm} to 1.0820
at \SI{5.66}{cm}. Across the whole design space the target therefore
moves by about \SI{200}{pcm}, roughly 0.3 MWd/kgHM or \SI{34}{EFPD}, so
the reflector thickness has ceased to be a reactivity variable in this
campaign and acts on the radial power shape alone.

The axial factor itself raises the target by about \SI{3040}{pcm}
relative to the two-dimensional campaigns, so the depleting assembly
stops earlier, as Figure~\ref{fig:ktarget-3d-shift} shows on design
C8-29, whose end of cycle moves from 35.5 to \SI{30.7}{MWd/kgHM}, and
on the ten designs whose depletion history crosses the target. Their
end-of-cycle slopes are 552 to \SI{756}{pcm} per MWd/kgHM, median 652,
and the correction shortens their cycle by 403 to \SI{552}{EFPD},
median 468, by more for the longer cycles, because the reactivity slope
of the assembly flattens as the fuel depletes.

\paragraph{Specification}
\label{sec:res-c8-intent}

The design space, the fixed quantities and the transport settings are

\SI{13000}{pcm} for four banks, and it holds four of the nineteen
feasible designs.

\paragraph{Surrogate, front and search of Campaign 8.}
\label{sec:res-c8-cv}\label{sec:res-c8-stability}\label{sec:res-c8-valgrid}
The cycle length is the best predicted quantity and the peaking factor
the worst, and the predictive intervals of the objectives are too
narrow, with a standardised residual of 1.26 to 1.63 against 1 for a
calibrated predictor. The two constraint eigenvalues are predicted with
a rank correlation above 0.96 and a two-sigma coverage of 0.97, above
the nominal 0.954, so the feasibility margin applied during the
campaign was at least as wide as intended. The front of eight designs
was still growing when the campaign stopped, since C8-59 joined it at
the last evaluation, and under the Monte Carlo noise of the transport
solves the three lowest-peaking members, C8-47, C8-42 and C8-23, hold
their place in 0.73 to 0.80 of 5000 replicates, so their order is not
resolved. On the two-dimensional subspace the surrogate-assisted search
recovered a front of eight members with 20 evaluations against 35 for
the exhaustive enumeration, with a hypervolume larger by
\SI{8.6}{\%} because it places designs between the grid nodes, and its
residual gap to the enumerated front is the additive epsilon indicator
of \SI{328}{EFPD} at the short-cycle end.

The two fronts are not identical. The inverted generational distance of
Equation~\eqref{eq:val-igd} is 0.137 in the normalised objective space,
against 0.243 for the enumerated point that the search approximates
worst, and the recovered front reproduces five of the eight enumerated
points strictly and six within a tolerance of 0.02. Of the eleven
members of the union of the two fronts, eight come from the search and
three from the grid, and the search dominates 14 of the 17 feasible
grid nodes. The search therefore covers most of the enumerated front
and extends it between the nodes, at 20 evaluations against 35, and the
hypervolume ratio above one measures that extension rather than a
complete recovery.

and it can only be held with the four regulating banks inserted deep
at power.


The two requirements pull in opposite directions because a longer
cycle is bought with reactivity that is burned later, and that
reactivity must be held at the start of life. With the soluble boron
and the gadolinia already present in every eigenvalue of this work,
whatever the burnup has not yet consumed is held by the rods, so the
excess that lengthens the cycle is the same excess that the
regulating banks must hold when inserted. The consequence for the formulation is
stated in Section~\ref{sec:concl-reform}: the cycle length becomes a
constraint at the required value and the two-bank eigenvalue an
objective. That is a stronger use of the two-bank reading than a
constraint would be, since a constraint would only bound the margin
whereas an objective maximises it.

\paragraph{Cycle length against the reported refuelling interval}
\label{sec:res-c8-mission}

The reported refuelling interval of five years is 1461 effective full
power days at a capacity factor of 0.8 and 913 at 0.5. Every design on the front exceeds the former, the shortest by
a third and the longest by a factor of four, and design C8-53 in the
two-bank subset meets it at 1142 EFPD with ring enrichments of 2.5,
3.1 and \SI{4.0}{wt\%}. The reported enrichment figures locate on the
same axis. The 6 to \SI{8}{wt\%} stated in 2014 corresponds to designs
31 and 21, at 3381 and 4221 EFPD, and the \SI{19}{wt\%} reported in
2025 is above the search box and, on the evidence of the axial study,
above what the regulating banks of this core can hold. The comparison
is conservative on two counts. Every cycle length is a lower bound
for a core operated with boron letdown
(Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-post-limits}), and the burnup dependence of
the leakage factor lengthens the six longest cycles by 11 to
\SI{56}{d} (Section~\ref{sec:res-kt-burnup}).

The consequence for the design problem is stated as a finding. The
objective of maximising cycle length pushes every design far beyond
the reported requirement and, in doing so, onto the control boundary.
Cycle length is not the binding requirement of this core. The binding
requirements are controllability and peaking, and the cycle length
belongs among the constraints, at the required value. Campaign 9 poses
the problem that way (Section~\ref{sec:res-c9}).

\paragraph{The pre-registered expectations, scored}
\label{sec:res-c8-hyp}

Eight expectations were written before the design of experiments
closed. Six held, among them the two-bank worth at a ratio of 0.473 to
0.511 of the four-bank worth, a design-of-experiments feasibility of 9
of 36 and no censored design. One was not testable, because no
uncorrected Campaign 8 run exists. One failed: the peaking floor was
expected to rise to about 1.60 through a thicker reflector, and the
campaign reached 1.510 through low enrichment instead.

\paragraph{Cost}
\label{sec:res-c8-cost}

The campaign cost \SI{1261}{s} per evaluation against \SI{949}{s} in
Campaign 7, and the archive records every transport phase separately:
assembly solve \SI{11}{s}, core solve \SI{107}{s}, depletion
\SI{925}{s} and the two rodded solves \num{100} and \SI{102}{s}, which
leaves \SI{16}{s} of non-transport overhead. The depletion dominates
because the cycles are long, at 14.6 transport states per design
against 13.0 in Campaign 7. The measured particle rate of the 64
threads of the workstation, \num{5734} per second, equals that of the
32 cores of the cloud instance, so the difference in wall time is the
third core solve and the longer depletions rather than the host.

\subsection{Limitations and the candidate list}
\label{sec:res-c8-limits}

Five limitations bound the claims of this section.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Five designs, C8-15, C8-24, C8-26, C8-28 and C8-48, reached the
        active batches with the fission-source entropy not yet
        converged, and none is on the front or in the two-bank subset.

  \item The statistical error on $F_{\Delta H}$ was not measured during
        the campaign, and adjacent front designs differ by 0.008 to
        0.051, so the ordering of the three lowest, C8-47, C8-42 and
        C8-23, is not resolved.

  \item C8-54 and C8-11 fail the controllability constraint by 17 and
        \SI{63}{pcm}, within the resolution of one core solve, so they
        are marginal rather than rejected and are re-scored with seed
        replicates.

  \item The controllability constraint tests hold-down at power with
        \SI{1000}{ppm} present and the model temperatures. It is not a
        shutdown-margin demonstration, which needs the cold state, all
        banks minus the highest-worth assembly and the boron worth of
        Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-boron}.

  \item The convergence claim is one block short of the rule of
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-loop}: the gain fell below one per cent
        in the last two blocks of four, so the third consecutive
        observation is missing and the front is reported as the best
        available rather than as converged.
\end{enumerate}

The campaign therefore returns two candidate lists, which answer
different questions. For the longest cycle at a stated peaking it
returns the front of Table~\ref{tab:c8-front}, whose three regions are
represented by C8-47 at the short-cycle floor, C8-23 at the knee and
C8-21 at the long-cycle boundary. For a core that operates like a
PWR, with the regulating banks withdrawn at power
and the refuelling interval met, it returns the two-bank subset of
Table~\ref{tab:c8-two}, represented by C8-53, which meets five years at
a capacity factor of 0.8, and by C8-31 at the enrichment reported in
2014. The three-dimensional confirmation of
Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-closures} decides between the two lists.

The candidate of the Campaign 8 formulation is design C8-47, for the
reasons set out in Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-decision} after the
post-analysis has been reported. Campaign 9 then searches the same
design space under the reformulated problem (Section~\ref{sec:res-c9}).

\label{sec:res-c8-post}

The archive of Campaign 8 fixes the front at 1000\,ppm of soluble boron

feasible designs that satisfy the two-bank reading of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-ctrl}.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A boron sweep at 0, 500, 1000 and 1500\,ppm in three rod

\paragraph{Which constraint binds}
\label{sec:res-c8-nesting}

In Campaign 7 the derived bound $k_{\max}$ rejected fourteen
controllable designs and the controllability constraint rejected none that the
bound accepted. Campaign 8 inverts this. Of the 41 infeasible designs,
six fail $k_{\min}$, 23 fail both $k_{\max}$ and the controllability constraint, and
twelve fail the controllability constraint alone. No design fails $k_{\max}$ alone.
The three-dimensionally derived bound of 1.166 is now redundant,
and the controllability constraint is the binding constraint.

A linear fit across the archive gives
$k_\mathrm{RE} = 0.875\,k_\mathrm{core} - 0.017$ with a
residual standard deviation of 1059\,pcm, so the controllability constraint acts
as an effective bound near $k_\mathrm{core} = 1.151$, with a spread
that the bank worth of each design sets. For the two-bank state the fit
is $k_\mathrm{RE12} = 0.930\,k_\mathrm{core}$ with a residual of
603\,pcm, and the effective bound is $k_\mathrm{core} = 1.064$.

\paragraph{Three-dimensional closures}
\label{sec:res-c8-3d}

The axial factor of the rodded core differs from that of the unrodded
core. With all rods out the three-dimensional core model gives
$L_\mathrm{ax} = k_\mathrm{2D} / k_\mathrm{3D}$ between 1.0286 and
1.0311 at 1000\,ppm over the eleven confirmed designs, against the 1.0282
to 1.0298 of the water-slab axial study of Section~\ref{sec:meth-lax}.
With RE1 + RE2 inserted it is between 1.0219 and 1.0252, and with the
four regulating banks inserted between 1.0126 and 1.0177. The reason is
the rod stack.
The two-dimensional constraint inserts B$_4$C over the whole fuel height,
whereas the benchmark rod leaves the bottom 12.04\,cm of the fuel
unrodded, carries 30.48\,cm of Ag-In-Cd above it and B$_4$C over the
remaining 77.48\,cm. The three-dimensional rodded core is therefore
less rodded than the two-dimensional one, and part of the axial leakage
gain is spent on the weaker stack, in proportion to the number of rods
inserted. The two-dimensional controllability constraint remains conservative, by
\num{1272} to \SI{2047}{pcm} for the four-bank state and by \num{2062}
to \SI{2671}{pcm} for the two-bank state, rather than by the 2900 to
3100\,pcm that the unrodded axial factor would suggest. Both ranges are
the differences of the margin columns of
Table~\ref{tab:c8-post-3d1000} over the eleven confirmed designs. The
two-dimensional eigenvalues of the confirmation reproduce the archive
within $-53$ to $+69$\,pcm over the thirty-three comparisons.

The unrodded axial factor splits by enrichment. The six designs at or
below \SI{5.90}{wt\%} give 1.0301 to 1.0311 and the five at or above
\SI{6.59}{wt\%} give 1.0286 to 1.0291. The campaign value 1.0289 is
therefore right for the long-cycle designs and 120 to 220\,pcm low for
the low-peaking end of the front.

\paragraph{Limitations of the post-analysis}
\label{sec:res-c8-post-limits}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every margin is a beginning-of-life value on fresh, xenon-free
        materials. The gadolinia burns out during the first part of the
        cycle and $k_\infty$ may rise before it falls. The minimum
        hold-down margin over the cycle may then sit at that maximum. The
        depletion histories of the eleven designs are on disk and the
        maximum can be located without new transport.
        The operating maximum was located from those histories in
        Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-closures}, where the hump of $-1723$ to
        $+4955$\,pcm on the core is applied to the margins before the
        candidate decision.

  \item The cycle lengths of the archive are those of a core held at
        1000\,ppm for the whole cycle, because the depleting assembly
        carries the operating point of the core solve. Both a boron
        letdown cycle and a boron-free cycle reach end of cycle with the
        absorber removed, so every reported cycle length is a lower bound.
        The bias is larger for the low-enrichment designs, whose boron
        worth is higher.
        \openpoint{Deplete design C8-47 at 0\,ppm with the same schedule, and
        recompute the reference target at 0\,ppm with one assembly and
        one core solve, to measure the bias on the boron-free candidate.}

  \item that condition is necessary and not
        sufficient for boron-free operation. It does not cover the
        cold-shutdown margin with the highest-worth rod stuck out, which
        needs the shutdown bank positions and a temperature-dependent
        water density that the model does not have, and it does not cover
        the power-shape penalty of operating with regulating banks
        partly inserted through the cycle.

  \item The boron sweep is a single-seed study. Its eigenvalues are
        confirmed by the two-seed zero-boron solves of
        Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-boron} and by the
        archive at 1000\,ppm, but its rodded peaking factors carry the
        0.028 seed noise of Table~\ref{tab:c8-post-sigma} and are not
        used for any conclusion.

  \item Discharge burnups of 50 to 58\,MWd/kgHM core average, as reached
        by designs C8-44, C8-59 and C8-1, combine with the 1.15 periphery
        multiplier and the radial peaking into peak-pin burnups that the
        constraints do not bound.

\paragraph{Candidate decision}
\label{sec:res-c8-decision}

Design C8-47 is the candidate carried forward, because it satisfies the
three readings of the post-analysis at once. It is the low-peaking
floor of the feasible front, at 1941\,EFPD, $F_{\Delta H} = 1.510$ and
4.39\,wt\% assembly enrichment. It is controllable with the first two
regulating banks at 1000\,ppm on the three-dimensional core, with a
margin of 1330\,pcm that survives the gadolinium hump because its
beginning of life is its most reactive state
(Table~\ref{tab:c8-swing}). And it satisfies the necessary condition
for boron-free operation of that condition,
holding 3069\,pcm under the four regulating banks at 0\,ppm, one of
only two front members that do, the other being its twin 42 at
2841\,pcm, whose two-bank status the seed noise does not resolve.

Its cycle length is the shortest on the front, which is a cost against
the rest of the front and not a shortfall against the reference
envelope, since \SI{1941}{EFPD} is 6.6 calendar years at a capacity
factor of 0.8. The same design leads all three readings because each of
them rewards a small beginning-of-life excess, and the low-enrichment
end of the front carries the smallest excess that still meets the
mission.

Two alternatives are recorded for a decision that weighs cycle length
more heavily. With boron, design C8-21 at the knee of the front gives
4221\,EFPD at $F_{\Delta H} = 1.592$ and 7.81\,wt\%, controllable by
the four banks only and not boron-free. Under two-bank control, design
31 gives 3381\,EFPD at $F_{\Delta H} = 1.650$ and 6.59\,wt\%, the
enrichment reported for the prototype in 2014, holding 5856\,pcm with
two banks on the three-dimensional core at beginning of life and
8684\,pcm boron-free under four banks, but its hump of 4955\,pcm
removes the two-bank margin at the operating maximum
(Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-closures}).

Design C8-47 is the candidate of the Campaign 8 formulation. Scored on the
objectives of Campaign 9 it is dominated by members of the Campaign 9
front, as Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-compare} shows.

for C8-43 and C8-51. The floor
of 1826\,EFPD was adopted for Campaign 9, and the boron worth was
measured on every design there instead of estimated.


\section{Campaign 9: the reformulated objective set}
\label{sec:res-c9}

Campaign 8 closed with a question. The controllability constraint, and
not the cycle length, decided which designs survived, and the
retrospective of Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-retro} showed that an objective
set built around the soluble boron requirement would select a different
part of the archive. Campaign 9 runs that formulation instead of
inferring it.

The campaign changes the formulation of Section~\ref{sec:meth-c9} and
keeps everything else. The design space, the transport fidelity, the
depletion schedule, the leakage-corrected target and the zoned loading map
of Section~\ref{sec:meth-zoning} are those of Campaign 8, so a difference
in outcome is attributable to the reformulation. Design indices from this
section onward carry the prefix of their campaign: C9-47 and C8-47 are
different designs that happen to share an archive index.

\paragraph{Measurement and cost}
\label{sec:res-c9-formulation}

The objectives are $F_{\Delta H}$ and the critical boron concentration at
the operating maximum, Equations~\eqref{eq:c9-problem}
and~\eqref{eq:c9-cmax}. The controllability constraint is applied at the operating
maximum through $g_\mathrm{ctrl,peak}$, and the guards that clamp
the objective at \SI{0}{ppm} and clip it at \SI{6000}{ppm} are those of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-c9}. The boron worth is measured in every evaluation
and is not taken from the power law of
Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-retro}.

That measurement also tests the correlation it replaces. Against the
sixty measured worths the power law fitted on eleven Campaign 8 designs
deviates by \SI{3.5}{\%} in root mean square, with a bias of
\SI{-1.2}{\%}. Below \SI{5.6}{wt\%}, which contains every feasible design,
the bias is \SI{-0.3}{\%} and the scatter \SI{2.6}{\%}, which is about
\SI{45}{ppm} at \SI{1700}{ppm} against a front spanning \SI{502}{ppm},
so inside the converged region the correlation would have been
adequate. Above that enrichment the law overestimates the worth by
\SI{3.6}{\%}, and those are the designs whose concentration reaches the
clip, so a correlation-driven campaign would have compounded an
extrapolated worth with an extrapolated root.

The campaign ran 60 evaluations in \SI{18.65}{h} on the reference
workstation, and the boron measurement took \SI{2.71}{h} of that,
\SI{14.5}{\%}, at one or two extra core solves per design, against the
\SI{3}{\%} increment that moved the peaking objective to core level in
Campaign 4. Depletion at assembly level takes \SI{10.47}{h}, \SI{56}{\%}
of the campaign, and the three core solves of the constraints and of the
reference concentration take \SI{5.28}{h} together, so the new
measurement is not the expensive part of an evaluation. The adaptive
choice of the second solve is the reason: ninety boron solves served
sixty designs, since $\rho(c)$ already changes sign over the interval
from 1000 to \SI{2000}{ppm} whenever the design is near criticality at
the reference concentration, and the designs that
needed a second solve are the high-enrichment ones that the constraint
set rejects in any case.

\subsection{The feasible set and the Pareto front}
\label{sec:res-c9-front}

Of the 60 designs evaluated, 22 satisfy every constraint, and 20 of those
need less boron than the limit of C9-47, \SI{2897}{ppm} at
\SI{12.8}{MPa} in three dimensions (Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-ceiling}).
That limit belongs to one lattice and serves here only as a reference
line, because Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-ceiling} shows that every design has
its own. Thirteen of the 22 pass the two-bank constraint of Campaign 8, a
two-dimensional reading at \SI{1000}{ppm}. The Pareto front holds five
designs, listed in Table~\ref{tab:c9-front}. Every concentration in the
table is interpolated between measured points, and the last column is the
two-bank reading at the operating maximum, recorded and not constrained.
The cycle lengths of the table are single evaluations whose statistical
error is \SI{29.1}{d}, measured in
Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-dep-check}. Two members, C9-40 and C9-44, are not
demonstrably above the mission floor once that error is taken into
account. Both remain on the front, which is defined in
$(F_{\Delta H}, c_\mathrm{max})$ and is unaffected, and their
cycle-length feasibility is declared uncertain.

\paragraph{Controllability measured where the design operates.}
The two-bank reading carried through the campaign is evaluated on a
two-dimensional core at a fixed \SI{1000}{ppm}, which is neither the
geometry nor the concentration of the operating state. The front was
therefore re-solved on the three-dimensional core model of Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-3d},
at each design's own critical boron, in three states: all rods out, all
rods in, and the first two regulating banks inserted.

At their own critical concentration the five front designs give an
unrodded multiplication factor of 0.97014 to 0.97111 on the three-dimensional core model.
With the first two banks inserted the factor falls to 0.90453 to 0.90713,
a worth of 7263 to \SI{7495}{pcm}, and with all four banks to 0.83819 to
0.84231, a worth of 15\,746 to \SI{16245}{pcm}. Three readings follow:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every front member is held subcritical by the first two banks alone,
        with 9228 to \SI{9545}{pcm} of margin against the 0.99 constraint of
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-core-objective}. The two-bank capability of
        these designs is roughly nine times what the constraint demands.

  \item The two-dimensional constraint overstates the bank worth by 4 to
        \SI{7}{\%}. The same two banks are worth 7651 to \SI{7812}{pcm} in
        two dimensions against 7263 to \SI{7495}{pcm} on the three-dimensional core model,
        because the axial reflector and the withdrawn rods change the flux shape
        the banks see. The constraint is therefore optimistic, and it remains
        usable because the margin it tests is an order of magnitude larger
        than the error.

  \item The spread across the front is small. The two-bank worth varies by
        \SI{3}{\%} over designs whose gadolinia loading varies by a factor of
        1.7, so the absorber loading has little influence on the bank worth
        at the concentrations these designs run at.
\end{enumerate}

The same measurement on the thirteen two-bank designs at \SI{1000}{ppm}
gives a four-bank worth of 15\,283 to \SI{16285}{pcm}. In the unrodded
state it also gives the axial factor, the ratio of the two-dimensional to
the three-dimensional multiplication factor, of 1.0298 to 1.0312,
consistent with the 1.0289 of Campaign 6 and with the 1.0304 of the
withdrawn-rod study of Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-3d}. This factor is measured at

The relaxation cascade behaves as expected at convergence. It deepened
from one halving to three and remained there, so the surrogate front
had few designs left that were new at the required separation. This
agrees with the flat hypervolume.

The margin count does not. No candidate passed the feasibility margin
of Equation~\eqref{eq:feas-margin} in any iteration, against 25 to 59 of
300 in Campaign 8 and 160 to 266 of 300 in Campaign 6, and an offline
replay of the acquisition on the final archive reproduces that result
with the cycle-length constraint binding on every candidate. The cause
is in the formulation of Equation~\eqref{eq:c9-problem}: a lower
$c_\mathrm{max}$ requires less excess reactivity, which shortens the
cycle, so the front sits on the cycle-length floor, the constraint is
active at the optimum, and NSGA-II places its candidates on the
predicted floor. To clear a margin of $\kappa = 1.5$ posterior standard
deviations there, a candidate would need a predicted cycle of about
\SI{1868}{d}, some \SI{42}{d} above the floor at the median, and such a
design carries more excess reactivity, needs more boron and is
dominated on the surrogate.

Two consequences follow. With no margin-feasible candidate, every batch
came from the fallback ordering, which followed the posterior standard
deviation of the cycle length, least uncertain first, at a Spearman
correlation of 0.92 in the replay against 0.10 for the exploration
criterion of Equation~\eqref{eq:acq-score}. The batches were therefore
selected by confidence in the cycle length rather than by exploration.
They were also placed on the floor itself, the median infill design
exceeding it by \SI{14}{d}, so 17 of the 36 infill designs fell below
it, ten of them by less than \SI{35}{d}, about one posterior standard
deviation of the constraint.

The finding does not change the archived results. Every design on the
front is a measured OpenMC evaluation, and C9-47 exceeds the floor by
\SI{121}{d}. It changes how the front is read. The margin was
introduced to stop evaluations being spent on designs of uncertain
feasibility (Section~\ref{sec:res-c6-margin}), and in this campaign it
could not act. The clustering of the feasible designs inside a
\SI{0.3}{wt\%} enrichment band is consistent with a ranking that
prefers well-predicted designs. Convergence produces the same pattern,
and the archive cannot separate the two. A formulation of the margin
that remains active at the boundary is listed in
Section~\ref{sec:concl-future}.

\paragraph{Scoring under the Campaign 8 objectives}

The Campaign 9 archive was also scored under the Campaign 8

\paragraph{Comparison with the Campaign 8 candidates}
\label{sec:res-c9-compare}

The two campaigns do not converge on the same designs. C8-47 is at a
normalised design-space distance of 0.401 from the nearest Campaign 9 front
member, and C8-13 at 0.722, both far outside the minimum infill separation
of 0.14. Much of that distance is the reflector, 4.29 and \SI{3.15}{cm}
against 5.65 to \SI{5.66}{cm} on the Campaign 9 front. Inside the Campaign 8
box the reflector is worth only about \SI{52}{pcm} per centimetre through
the target (Equation~\eqref{eq:ktarget-c8-fit}). At the mission floor, where
every design sits near the minimum reactivity the cycle needs, that small
gain lowers the enrichment required, and the enrichment determines the
boron demand, which is consistent with a boron objective pushing every front
member to the reflector bound.

Placed on the Campaign 9 objectives, both Campaign 8 designs that the
retrospective of Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-retro} selected at the same floor
are dominated. Both have a negative hump, so their concentration at the
operating maximum is the beginning-of-life value.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item C8-47, at $F_{\Delta H} = 1.510$, \SI{2327}{ppm} and 1941\,EFPD, is
        dominated by C9-35, C9-40 and C9-34, which need 419 to
        \SI{602}{ppm} less boron at a peaking factor lower by 0.014 to
        0.020. The peaking differences are within about one and a half seed
        standard deviations of Campaign 8, so the dominance rests on the
        boron demand.

  \item C8-13, at $F_{\Delta H} = 1.632$, \SI{1697}{ppm} and 2383\,EFPD, is
        dominated by C9-47 and C9-44 on both objectives, by 291 and
        \SI{201}{ppm} and by 0.087 and 0.093 in peaking.
\end{enumerate}

C8-53 needs \SI{1312}{ppm}, less than any member of the Campaign 9 front,
but its cycle of 1142\,EFPD does not meet the floor. The comparison is
indicative, because the Campaign 8 concentrations come from the
post-analysis, a measured root for C8-47 and a linear estimate for C8-13,
while the Campaign 9 concentrations come from the probe solves of the
evaluator.

Both pairs point the same way. At matched inventory the design with the
higher concentration in each poisoned pin shows no resolved hump, while its
partner shows a hump of 1093 and \SI{1349}{pcm}. The enrichment differs by
0.14 and \SI{0.24}{wt\%} within the pairs, and in both cases the difference
acts against the observed effect, since the design without the hump carries
the higher enrichment, so the effect is if anything understated.

The second pair reverses the ranking. At beginning of life C9-32 needs less
boron, 2121 against \SI{2154}{ppm}. At the operating maximum C9-51 needs
less, 2154 against \SI{2333}{ppm}. At matched inventory and identical
reflector, which of the two needs less boron depends on the state at which
the question is asked, which is the clearest single demonstration in this
work that the objective has to be defined at the operating maximum.

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
The hump of Equation~\eqref{eq:c7-hump} is the difference between the
reactivity at the mid-cycle maximum and at beginning of life, and the
gadolinium suppresses both terms. It is governed by a race between
gadolinium burnout, which raises the multiplication factor, and fuel
depletion with fission-product build-up, which lowers it.

A dilute gadolinia pin is nearly transparent to thermal neutrons through
its volume, so it burns out quickly and uniformly, within the first few
\si{MWd\per kg} of burnup and before appreciable fuel depletion. Almost the
whole poison worth returns as a rise above the beginning-of-life point, and
the hump is large. A concentrated pin shields its own interior and burns
inward from the surface over many \si{MWd\per kg}, by which time fuel
depletion has removed a comparable amount of reactivity, so the two effects
cancel and the multiplication factor does not climb above its initial
value.

The grouped statistic supports the same reading. Of the 16 designs in the
band, the seven with a resolved hump carry a mean gadolinia weight fraction
of \SI{1.67}{wt\%}, against \SI{3.52}{wt\%} for the nine without, and a
one-sided Mann-Whitney test gives $U = 10$ and $p = 0.012$. That test is
used in preference to the rank correlation because it is insensitive to the
nine ties at the noise floor.

The mechanism is read across the enrichment band.

Three things happen as the gadolinia rises. The beginning-of-life
concentration falls, because the poison holds down reactivity that boron
would otherwise have to hold. The hump disappears above roughly
\SI{2.5}{wt\%}. The two quantities therefore converge, and above that
loading the objective equals its beginning-of-life value. One design of
the band sits above the limit of C9-47, C9-12 at \SI{3003}{ppm}, the
most dilute of the sixteen at \SI{0.14}{wt\%}, whose beginning-of-life
requirement of \SI{2482}{ppm} is below that line, so the hump alone
carries it above.

The lattice scan of Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-ceiling} makes the case stronger
than the reference line allows. The limit of C9-12 is \SI{2091}{ppm}, not
\SI{2897}{ppm}, because the same dilution that raises its demand also lowers
what it can tolerate. Judged against its own lattice the design fails by
\SI{912}{ppm} instead of \SI{106}{ppm}, and even its beginning-of-life
requirement is \SI{391}{ppm} too high. An objective evaluated at beginning
of life would therefore also have rejected this design, but only once its
own limit was known.

Figure~\ref{fig:c9-hump} isolates the hump over the whole feasible set,
against the gadolinia content and against the boron penalty it carries,
with the \SI{400}{pcm} noise floor marked. The hump reaches
\SI{3430}{pcm} at negligible loading and falls below that floor above
\SI{2.5}{wt\%}. Only two feasible designs above that loading retain a
resolved hump, \SI{1093}{pcm} at \SI{3.15}{wt\%} and \SI{512}{pcm} at
\SI{4.10}{wt\%}, and both carry their gadolinia on 20 rods instead of
16, which is the matched comparison of Table~\ref{tab:c9-pairs} seen
from the other side.

The right panel plots the penalty the hump carries into the objective
against the hump itself, and confirms that it enters as
Equation~\eqref{eq:c9-cmax} defines. It is proportional to the hump with
a slope of \SI{0.153}{ppm} per pcm, an implied boron worth of
\SI{6.5}{pcm} per ppm, against \SI{6.35}{pcm} per ppm measured as the mean
over the eight designs that carry a hump. The agreement is a consistency
check on the implementation, and it is reported because it is the only
direct verification that the hump term and the measured worth are combined
correctly.

The mechanism is inferred from two matched pairs and a grouped test. A
designed set of four or five assemblies at fixed gadolinia inventory
spanning 12 to 40 poisoned pins, one depletion each, about one hour of
machine time, would settle it, and the multiplication-factor histories of a
humped and an unhumped front design, already written by
\texttt{validate\_ktarget\_burnup.py}, would show the burnout timescale
directly at no additional cost.

\subsection{The peaking objective under fidelity and dimensionality}
\label{sec:res-c9-peaking}

The five front designs were re-solved with all rods out at
\num{150000} particles and 200 batches with 80 inactive, two seeds, on the
two-dimensional core and on the three-dimensional core model of
Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-3d}. Three quantities can be separated from those
solves, together with the core-level fidelity study of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-fid-ladder}: the noise and bias of the estimator,
the effect of raising the transport statistics, and the effect of the
third dimension.

noisy bins is biased upward (Section~\ref{sec:bg-mc}), and raising the
statistics lowers the estimate. The shift is about a quarter of the width
of the whole front on that objective.

\paragraph{Consequence for the front.}
With the two-dimensional confirmation values the non-dominated set of these
five designs in $(F_{\Delta H}, c_\mathrm{max})$ is C9-34, C9-35, C9-44 and
C9-47, since C9-34 then dominates C9-40. With the three-dimensional values
it becomes C9-34, C9-40 and C9-47. The three changes are of unequal
standing.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item C9-40 enters the front because its hot channel factor falls by
        0.030 between the two models, at 5.0 standard deviations of the
        six-seed comparison. This change is measured.

  \item C9-44 leaves the front because C9-47 improves by 0.026 while C9-44
        does not move, so in three dimensions C9-47 has both the lower
        peaking, 1.501 against 1.513, and the lower boron requirement, 1406
        against \SI{1496}{ppm}. This change is measured.

  \item C9-35 leaves the front because it becomes dominated. Against C9-34
        the dominance is not resolved, because both designs reach a peaking
        of 1.487 and the boron requirement alone separates them, 1725
        against \SI{1908}{ppm}. Against C9-40 it is resolved at about two
        standard deviations, with a peaking of 1.460 against 1.487 and a
        boron requirement of 1768 against \SI{1908}{ppm}, so this change
        stands or falls with the shift of C9-40 in the first item.
\end{enumerate}

A design-dependent shift of this size is the case in which a
three-dimensional objective reorders a front instead of translating it, so
a calibrated correction applied to the two-dimensional value would not
recover the same answer, as the zoned model discrepancy of
Section~\ref{sec:res-proxy-gap-zoned} found for the assembly value.

One caution applies to this five-design comparison. C9-40, the design
that drives the front change, has a two-seed spread of 0.017 in the
two-dimensional solve against 0.001 to 0.004 for the other four, so its
own convergence is the least well established of the set.

\paragraph{The comparison over the whole archive.}
The comparison with all rods out was extended to all sixty designs, one
solve per design in each model with two seeds. The three-dimensional hot
channel factor is lower than the two-dimensional one for almost every
design, by 0.023 on average with a standard deviation of 0.017, a ratio of
0.9851 with a relative spread of \SI{1.10}{\%}. The correction is therefore
not a constant, and the rank correlation between the two orderings is 0.941,
with a largest rank shift of 14 places out of 60.

Recomputed over the 22 feasible designs, the non-dominated set is C9-34,
C9-35, C9-44, C9-47 and C9-58 with the two-dimensional factor, and C9-12,
C9-34, C9-40 and C9-47 with the three-dimensional one. Three observations
bound what that change means:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item C9-47 is on both fronts. It is selected whichever factor is used, so
        the reordering does not reach the design the campaign selects.

  \item The newcomer is inoperable. C9-12 fails the limit of its own
        lattice by \SI{912}{ppm} (Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-ceiling}), so it
        cannot be a candidate whatever its peaking.

  \item Part of the change comes from fidelity. The two-dimensional values of
        this comparison are solved at \num{150000} particles over 200
        batches, while the archive values are solved at \num{100000} over
        170. Over the 41 designs common to both, the higher-fidelity value is
        lower by 0.016 on average with a standard deviation of 0.017,
        reaching 0.068 on C9-42 and 0.037 on C9-58. A hot channel factor is
        a maximum over channels, and a maximum over noisier estimates is
        biased upward (Section~\ref{sec:bg-mc}), so the campaign-fidelity
        value reads high. C9-58 enters the two-dimensional front of this
        comparison largely through that shift.
\end{enumerate}

The quantities that matter are of the same size. The peaking spread across
the whole front is 0.055, the dimensional shift 0.023, the fidelity shift
0.016, and the leave-one-out error of the surrogate on the feasible subset
0.039 (Table~\ref{tab:c9-cv}). A campaign that optimised this objective more
finely could not resolve differences of this size, which argues against a
further campaign on the peaking objective more strongly than the front
change does on its own. The three-dimensional peaking of C9-47 is therefore
reported as a verified value, 1.501 against 1.527 in two dimensions, both
well inside the 1.65 constraint.

\subsection{The MTC boron limit of each candidate lattice}
\label{sec:res-c9-ceiling}

Campaign 8 measured one MTC boron limit, on the
lattice of C8-47, and Campaign 9 inherited it as a single reference line.
That inheritance does not hold. The coefficient responds to a change in
moderator density, and the response depends on the absorber loading of the
lattice, which is a design variable. Eight lattices were therefore scanned
on the three-dimensional core model at \SI{12.8}{MPa}: every candidate on either peaking
front of Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-peaking}, and C9-57 as a diagnostic design.

\paragraph{Method of the MTC scans.}
Each Campaign 9 lattice was scanned with the method of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-mtc},
sixteen solves per design. The window at \SI{12.8}{MPa} is the one of
Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-mtc}, so the Campaign 8 and Campaign 9 limits
can be compared directly. Three of the eight scans have $\chi^2/\nu$
above two, the signature of a mild curvature of the coefficient in
boron, and their uncertainties are inflated accordingly.

\paragraph{The MTC boron limit of C9-47.}
The best-ranked design was measured in two and in three dimensions at
both pressures. It gives $2466 \pm \SI{106}{ppm}$ and
$2897 \pm \SI{99}{ppm}$ at \SI{12.8}{MPa}, and $2669 \pm \SI{135}{ppm}$
and $3266 \pm \SI{75}{ppm}$ at \SI{15.5}{MPa}, all from the three-point
fit described above. The move from two to three dimensions raises the
limit by $430 \pm \SI{145}{ppm}$ and $597 \pm \SI{154}{ppm}$. On
C8-47 the same shift was \SI{584}{ppm}, so the axial leakage feedback
of Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-3d} reproduces on an independent lattice,
and a limit computed in two dimensions is conservative by roughly
that amount at a quarter of the cost.

demand of the same design, and the uncertainties are those of the

The limit rises with the gadolinia loading at both pressures, and two
measures of that loading were tested:

\begin{itemize}
  \item At \SI{12.8}{MPa} the Spearman coefficient is 1.000 against the
        inventory and 0.976 against the Gd$_2$O$_3$ weight fraction.

  \item At \SI{15.5}{MPa} it is 0.900 against the inventory and 1.000
        against the weight fraction.

  \item The number of gadolinia pins alone shows no correlation at
        either pressure, with coefficients of $-0.07$ and $-0.10$.
\end{itemize}

The two measures rank every lattice identically except C9-34 and C9-44.
Their limits differ by \SI{106(146)}{ppm} at \SI{12.8}{MPa}, in favour
of C9-44, and by \SI{161(133)}{ppm} at \SI{15.5}{MPa}, in favour of
C9-34, so this pair is not ordered by either measurement. The present
data therefore do not distinguish the inventory from the weight fraction
as the governing variable.

An unweighted logarithmic fit in the inventory gives the relation below.

\begin{equation}
  c_\mathrm{MTC}(I) = a \ln I + b
  \label{eq:mtc-ceiling-fit}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $c_\mathrm{MTC}$ is the boron concentration at which the
        moderator temperature coefficient crosses zero, in ppm.

  \item $I$ is the gadolinia inventory, the Gd$_2$O$_3$ weight fraction
        in wt\% multiplied by the number of gadolinia pins as built,
        dimensionless.

  \item $a$ is the slope, \SI{287}{ppm} at \SI{12.8}{MPa} and
        \SI{226}{ppm} at \SI{15.5}{MPa}.

  \item $b$ is the intercept, \SI{1595}{ppm} at \SI{12.8}{MPa} and
        \SI{2134}{ppm} at \SI{15.5}{MPa}.
\end{itemize}

The root mean square residual of this fit is \SI{55}{ppm} at
\SI{12.8}{MPa} and \SI{96}{ppm} at \SI{15.5}{MPa}. A straight line in
the weight fraction gives \SI{68}{ppm} and \SI{48}{ppm}. Neither form is
preferred at both pressures.

The pressure shift is consistent with a single value. Over the five
lattices measured twice its weighted mean is \SI{322(50)}{ppm}, with
$\chi^2 = 5.7$ on four degrees of freedom ($p = 0.22$), and no trend
with inventory is resolved.

The verdicts do not depend on the pressure. The binding pressure is
\SI{12.8}{MPa} for every lattice, where C9-12 fails by \SI{912}{ppm},
C9-58 clears by \SI{120}{ppm} and the three front designs measured at
both pressures clear by 1027 to \SI{1491}{ppm}.

The limit and the demand also move in opposite directions with the
same variable. Across the eight lattices at \SI{12.8}{MPa} the limit
spans \SI{806}{ppm}, while the operating margin spans \SI{2403}{ppm},
from \SI{-912}{ppm} to \SI{+1491}{ppm}. The demand does most of the work
and the limit reinforces it, so a design is either comfortably
operable or clearly not, with little middle ground.

\paragraph{The fidelity of the two sides of the comparison.}
The limit is measured on the three-dimensional core model, while the demand is a
two-dimensional core quantity, so the comparison in this section mixes two
fidelities. The rodded confirmation of Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-front}
measures the size of that mismatch. At each design's own critical
concentration the unrodded factor falls from 1.0000 in two dimensions to
0.9704 on the three-dimensional core model, a reactivity difference of 3001 to
\SI{3028}{pcm} across the five front designs, which at their measured boron
worths is 445 to \SI{459}{ppm}.

The three-dimensional demand is therefore about \SI{450}{ppm} below the value
in Table~\ref{tab:c9-front}, and the two-dimensional limit is about
\SI{430}{ppm} below the three-dimensional one. Both errors point the same
way, so every margin quoted here is conservative by roughly \SI{900}{ppm}.
The direction of the conclusions is unaffected. The comparison is stated as
it was measured and is not corrected, because a correction would mix a
measured quantity with an inferred one, and a campaign that relied on this
comparison would have to measure both sides on the same model.

\paragraph{Verdicts of the eight lattices.}
Of the seven candidates on either peaking front, six clear their own
limit and one does not. C9-47, C9-44, C9-34, C9-40 and C9-35 clear it
by 710 to \SI{1491}{ppm}, at 3.7 standard deviations or more, so those
verdicts are not in question. C9-58 clears by \SI{120}{ppm}, at 2.3
standard deviations, and further sampling will not change that, because
the margin itself is the result: the design operates at \SI{95}{\%} of
what its lattice tolerates, against \SI{49}{\%} for C9-47, so its
operability is not established in the sense the other five are. C9-12
fails by \SI{912}{ppm}, at 13.2 standard deviations, and it is the one
design that entered the front only through the three-dimensional
peaking of Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-peaking}.

The diagnostic design C9-57 fails by \SI{193}{ppm} at 1.1 standard
deviations, within the fidelity mismatch of about \SI{450}{ppm}
described above, so its verdict is undecided and is reported as such.
It marks where the combined effect of demand and limit places the
boundary, at roughly \SI{1}{wt\%} of gadolinia in this design space.
C9-12 is the only verdict the mismatch cannot overturn, since it still
fails by roughly \SI{460}{ppm} once both sides are put on the
three-dimensional core. The eight lattices also confound the inventory
with the reflector thickness, which is at its bound for seven of them,
so the logarithmic law should not be extrapolated outside the converged
region of this campaign.

\paragraph{Status of the post-analysis}
\label{sec:res-c9-post}

The post-analysis of Campaign 9 mirrors that of Campaign 8 and is scripted
in \texttt{run\_c9\_post.sh}. The three-dimensional peaking of the front and
of the whole archive with all rods out is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-peaking}, the rodded three-dimensional confirmation
of the front in Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-front}, and the MTC boron limit of
eight candidate lattices in Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-ceiling}.

The drifts combine by inverse-variance weighting into a mean of
$-62 \pm 54$\,pcm, with $\chi^2/\nu = 0.26$ over the five designs. The mean is
consistent with zero at 1.2 standard deviations and with the $-13 \pm 30$\,pcm
of the Campaign 8 designs, Equation~\eqref{eq:kt-comp}. The leakage factor
fitted at beginning of life therefore holds to the end of cycle, which is
what justifies the depletion at assembly level.

The residuals behave differently. All five are positive, from $+15$ to
$+98$\,pcm with a mean of $+59$\,pcm, so the measured factor is above the
tabulated target on every design. Five residuals of the same sign are a
systematic offset in the k-target table and are stated separately from the
drift. The offset is small, about one part in two thousand in the factor,
and it shortens the reported cycle by 2 to 17 days, below one per cent.

C9-34 is the design for which the correction is largest relative to its
margin. Its cycle length of \SI{1841}{d} becomes \SI{1824}{d} once the
end-of-cycle leakage factor is applied, that is \SI{2}{d} below the mission
floor. The correction itself carries an uncertainty of about \SI{15}{d},
propagated from the \SI{105}{pcm} uncertainty of its drift, so the shortfall
is \SI{2+-15}{d} and is not resolved. It is also an order of magnitude
smaller than the \SI{37}{d} uncertainty of the cycle-length proxy at this
mission. The design is therefore retained on the front, with the same caveat
that applies to C9-44, that its compliance with the mission is not
established by this campaign.

Corrections are not applied retroactively to individual designs, because
the front is the set the constraint evaluation produced. A correction
applied to one design and not to the other fifty-nine would not correspond
to any consistent procedure.

The boron worth of the five front designs was measured in a separate
unrodded sweep at 0, 1000, 1500 and \SI{2500}{ppm}, with the solver path
of the campaign evaluator. The differential worth between 0 and
\SI{1000}{ppm} is 6.83 to \SI{7.10}{pcm} per ppm, and it falls by 3 to 7
per cent up to \SI{2500}{ppm}. The critical concentrations interpolated
from the sweep, 1336 to \SI{1920}{ppm}, reproduce the $c_\mathrm{BOL}$ of
the campaign within $-5$ to \SI{+12}{ppm}, and the unrodded eigenvalues
at \SI{1000}{ppm} agree with the archive within \SI{60}{pcm}. The boron
demand of Table~\ref{tab:c9-front} is therefore confirmed on the front.

\subsection{Axial peaking as a candidate objective}
\label{sec:res-c10-gate}

A third objective was considered for a further campaign, the axial
peaking factor $F_z$ of the three-dimensional core. It was tested on
the Campaign 9 archive, all 60 designs with the axial mesh and the 22
feasible ones again with a second seed, against two thresholds fixed in
advance: a signal-to-noise ratio of at least 2, the spread across
designs divided by the seed-to-seed standard deviation, and a
leave-one-out $R^2$ of at least 0.5.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $F_z$ runs from 1.4263 to 1.4504 over the archive, a span of
        \SI{1.67}{\%}, against \SI{23.2}{\%} for the three-dimensional
        $F_{\Delta H}$ on the same solves. With a seed-to-seed standard
        deviation of 0.0032, the signal-to-noise ratio of one solve is
        1.19. Over the feasible region alone it falls to 0.76, and two
        seeds raise it only to 1.05.

  \item The surrogate reaches a leave-one-out $R^2$ of 0.513 against
        0.824 for the three-dimensional $F_{\Delta H}$, so it sits on
        the threshold while the established objective is above it.

  \item Ranking the archive by the product $F_{\Delta H} F_z$ reproduces
        the ranking by $F_{\Delta H}$ alone, with a Spearman coefficient
        of 0.996 and a largest rank shift of six places.
\end{itemize}

The axial shape of this core is therefore set by the geometry, which is
the same for every design, and what little it varies follows the
enrichment, with a rank correlation of $-0.709$. An objective whose
spread is of the order of its own noise cannot order a front, so the
axial peaking factor was rejected and the campaign was not run. The
axial power shape is reported as a diagnostic of the confirmed designs
in Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-front}.

\subsection{Replicas and fidelity of the depletion on the front}
\label{sec:res-c9-dep-check}

The cycle length, the gadolinium hump and, through the hump, the boron
objective all come from one assembly depletion per design at
$4\,000\times60$ with 20 inactive batches. Three statements of this work
rest on that single solve, and none of them had been measured.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item That the exploration tier and the production tier give the same
        cycle length, which the two-tier strategy of
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-fidelity} assumes.

  \item That the statistical error of the cycle length is small against
        the margins the front reports.

  \item That the front members clear the mission floor of \SI{1826}{d}
        by the margins recorded in the archive.
\end{enumerate}

Each front design was therefore depleted again four times at the campaign
fidelity with independent seeds, and once at the Campaign 5 production
fidelity of $16\,000\times120$ with 30 inactive batches. C9-40 and C9-44,
whose margins turned out to be at the mission floor, received four
further replicas each, so they carry nine samples against five for the
others. Only the depletion was repeated. The peaking factor, the
reactivity window and the boron points are beginning-of-life core solves
at $100\,000\times170$ and are unchanged, so the boron objective was
recomputed from the replicated hump with the archived boron points,
exactly as the campaign computes it.

mission floor in units of that standard error.

\paragraph{The two fidelities agree.}
The difference between the production tier and the mean of the campaign
tier is \SI{-2.2}{d} with a standard error of \SI{4.9}{d} over the five
designs, $t = -0.45$. Eight times more histories do not move the cycle
length, so the inter-fidelity shift that
Section~\ref{sec:meth-fid-settings} requires before the cycle lengths of
Campaigns 6 to 9 may be compared with those of Campaigns 3 to 5 is
measured here and is compatible with zero on this lattice family. The
exploration tier is adequate for the cycle-length axis, which is the
assumption the two-tier strategy rests on.

The replicas also measure the hump itself. Pooled over the five designs
its seed-to-seed standard deviation is \SI{217}{pcm} on 28 degrees of
freedom, so the \SI{400}{pcm} floor below which
Equation~\eqref{eq:c9-cmax} sets the hump to zero is twice that
standard deviation.

\paragraph{The boron objective is stable, for a structural reason.}
Three of the five designs return the archived $c_\mathrm{max}$ in every
replica, with zero scatter. Their gadolinium hump is more than the
\SI{400}{pcm} noise floor of Equation~\eqref{eq:c9-cmax} away from that
floor, in either direction, so the floor clips it to the same value in
every replica and $c_\mathrm{max}$ reduces to the beginning-of-life
boron, which comes from core solves that were not repeated. Only C9-44,
whose hump is \SI{1004}{pcm}, and C9-40, whose hump is \SI{-58}{pcm} and
therefore within one replica spread of the floor, show any scatter, and
it is \SI{34}{ppm} for both. The non-dominated set in
$(F_{\Delta H}, c_\mathrm{max})$ is the same five designs under the
archive, under the replica means and at production fidelity.

\paragraph{Two cycle-length margins do not survive the noise.}
The pooled seed standard deviation of the cycle length is \SI{29.1}{d},
with 23 degrees of freedom and a 95 per cent range of 22.6 to
\SI{40.8}{d}. It separates the front into three groups.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item C9-47 and C9-35 clear the floor by 136 and \SI{104}{d}, that is
        10.4 and 8.0 standard errors. Their feasibility in cycle length
        is established.

  \item C9-34 clears it by \SI{29}{d}, 2.2 standard errors, so it is
        probably feasible.

  \item C9-40 and C9-44 both give \SI{-6}{d} over nine samples, less than
        one standard error on the infeasible side, and their production
        solves give $-4$ and \SI{-21}{d}. Their cycle length is
        indistinguishable from the floor, and the archived margins of
        $+44$ and \SI{+32}{d} were single samples of a quantity whose
        standard deviation is \SI{29.1}{d}.
\end{enumerate}

This is the constraint-side counterpart of the acquisition behaviour
reported in Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-convergence}. The search drives the
front onto the mission floor, so about half of the designs that land
there are above it only by the statistical error of one evaluation.
Feasibility at an active constraint cannot be read from a single
evaluation, and the recommendation of this work therefore rests on
C9-47, whose margin is 10.4 standard errors.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:res-c9-limits}

Seven limitations qualify the result of this campaign.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Boundary optimum:} every front member carries the
        largest reflector the vessel admits, a bound the optimiser
        reached in the first infill iteration and never left, so the
        front is conditional on the vessel envelope and the clearance
        of Section~\ref{sec:meth-c8-space}.

  \item \textbf{Cycle-length margin:} two front members are not
        demonstrably above the mission floor. The seed replicas of
        Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-dep-check} measure a statistical error of
        \SI{29.1}{d} on the cycle length, and over nine samples each
        C9-40 and C9-44 fall \SI{6}{d} below the floor of \SI{1826}{d},
        inside one standard error of \SI{9.7}{d}. Both stay on the front,
        which is defined in $(F_{\Delta H}, c_\mathrm{max})$ and is
        stable under replicas and at production fidelity, and no
        cycle-length feasibility is claimed for them. Separating a
        \SI{6}{d} margin from zero at two standard errors would need
        about ninety replicas per design, which is not a sensible use
        of the remaining budget.

  \item \textbf{MTC boron limit:} eight lattices were scanned at
        \SI{12.8}{MPa} and five of them also at \SI{15.5}{MPa}, so the
        limit of C9-35, C9-40 and C9-57 at the higher pressure is
        inferred from the measured shift of $322 \pm \SI{50}{ppm}$
        rather than measured. C9-58 clears its own limit by only
        \SI{120}{ppm}, so its operability is not established.

  \item \textbf{Front stability:} only C9-47 and C9-34 survive every
        resample of Table~\ref{tab:c9-stability}, and the apparent
        quality of the front is optimistic by \SI{2.8}{\%} in
        hypervolume through selection alone.

  \item \textbf{Peaking objective:} it is evaluated in two dimensions
        at campaign fidelity. Raising the statistics lowers it by
        $0.013 \pm 0.004$, the three-dimensional model shifts it by a
        design-dependent amount, and substituting that value changes
        the non-dominated set (Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-peaking}), so
        Table~\ref{tab:c9-front} is a set of comparable designs rather
        than an ordered ranking.

  \item \textbf{Constant boron:} the depletion runs at \SI{1000}{ppm},
        so every cycle length is a lower bound for a core operated with
        boron letdown, and the critical concentration is measured at
        two states of the cycle only.

  \item \textbf{Acquisition margin:} with the cycle-length constraint
        active on the front, no candidate passed the feasibility margin
        and every batch came from the fallback ordering
        (Table~\ref{tab:c9-acq-health}), so the claim that the batches
        were selected for exploration does not carry over from
        Campaigns 6 and 8.
\end{enumerate}

The third and the fifth are the two that could change a conclusion, and
both are measured: the limit of eight lattices in
Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-ceiling} and the two- and three-dimensional
peaking of all sixty designs in Section~\ref{sec:res-c9-peaking}.
Neither displaces the selected design.
