\section{Campaigns 1 and 2: the shakedown and the estimator}
\label{sec:res-c1c2}

Campaign 1 (72 evaluations) is retained for one lesson: every design on
its Pareto front violated the vessel-fit condition once checked
analytically. Geometry self-consistency has been enforced inside the
optimiser ever since (Eq.~\ref{eq:renv}).

Campaign 2 (84 evaluations, 44 feasible, $4\,000\times60$ fidelity)
located the optimal region and exposed the estimator pathology
(Table~\ref{tab:c2-summary}): the stored single-seed peaking values
carried a bias \emph{larger than the front spread they ranked}, and
resolving the candidates at high fidelity inverted the ranking --- the
stored 4th became the true 1st, and the apparent winner fell to 7th,
separated at $|t|=12.8$.

\section{Campaign 3: design of experiments and feasibility envelope}
\label{sec:res-c3doe}

Campaign 3 (72 evaluations at $16\,000\times120$, per-design seeds, ATF
limit in-loop) opens with a 36-point Latin hypercube: 11 feasible
(31\%), with the upper reactivity bound responsible for 21 of 25
rejections. Regression over the archive gives the feasibility envelope
($R^2 = 0.92$--$0.94$, stable from $n=36$ to $n=72$):
\begin{equation}
  k_\mathrm{BOL} \;=\; 0.894 \;+\; 0.215\,\ln e_\mathrm{vw}
  \;-\; 0.0066\,\mathrm{gd},
  \qquad
  e_\mathrm{vw} = 0.72\,e_\mathrm{out} + 0.28\,e_\mathrm{in}.
  \label{eq:envelope}
\end{equation}

Three readings of Eq.~\eqref{eq:envelope} organise the design space.
Volume-weighted enrichment governs: the 72/28 zone split makes
$e_\mathrm{out}$ dominant by construction. Gadolinium at the fixed
twelve-pin pattern buys only $\Delta k \approx 0.05$--$0.06$ over its
full range --- 2.7\,wt\% of enrichment headroom --- against a
literature practice of 20--40 poisoned
pins~\cite{rr_smr_atf_2025,ismr_sbf_2024}. And pitch emerges as the
second feasibility lever (logistic coefficient $+1.67$ at $n=72$,
behind only $e_\mathrm{out}$): over-moderation reaches criticality at
low enrichment. The envelope figure generated from the archive
(\texttt{figs/envelope\_b2}) belongs here.

emerge: a thick-reflector, high-enrichment capped design pressed
against the vessel ($g_\mathrm{geom}=-0.4$\,cm), and an over-moderated
cluster (pitch 1.41, enrichment $\sim$5.3\,wt\%, thin reflector) ---
both shaped by the same scarce resource, the 90\,cm vessel radius
(Eq.~\ref{eq:renv}).

\section{Core-level validation: the proxy gap}
\label{sec:res-corevalidation}

Rebuilding the finalists as full cores (BOL, 8 seeds) measures what the
reflective assembly cannot see (Table~\ref{tab:core-finalists}).

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Finalists at core level ($100\,000\times$ up to 230 batches,
  8 seeds). The capped pair ties and dominates; \emph{no design meets
  the 2.0 limit}.}
  \label{tab:core-finalists}
  \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
    \toprule
    & $F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}$ & $F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}$
      (95\% CI) & ratio & EFPD \\
    \midrule
    idx46 (cand3) & 1.1397 & $2.013\pm0.018$ & 1.77 & 7\,513 \\
    idx45 (cand7) & 1.1522 & $2.020\pm0.019$ & 1.75 & 7\,513 \\
    idx53 (cand5) & 1.1445 & $2.114\pm0.018$ & 1.85 & 5\,902 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Three facts. The proxy gap is a factor \textbf{1.75--1.91},
anti-correlated with reflector thickness ($r=-0.96$ over the six
finalists): thin reflectors leak, depress the periphery, and drive
core-wide peaking --- invisible to an infinite lattice. The ranking
\emph{inverts}: the assembly-best design is core-worst, and the capped
pair (idx46/idx45, tied at $|t|=0.54$) beats the plateau at
$|t|\approx7$--$8$, collapsing the front to the capped strategy. And the
peaking constraint, inactive everywhere at assembly level, is now
\textbf{violated by every design}: the best sits at
$2.013\pm0.018$ against the 2.0 limit.

Rescoring the \emph{entire} feasible archive (36 designs,
Fig.~\ref{fig:asmcore}) generalises the finding:

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{th_asm_vs_core}
  \caption{Every feasible Campaign-3 design rescored at core level. The
  proxy is globally informative ($\rho=+0.890$) but scrambles the
  ordering exactly where the optimiser operates.}
  \label{fig:asmcore}
\end{figure}

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{The proxy in numbers, and the designs it mis-ranked.}
  \label{tab:proxy-numbers}
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    Spearman $\rho$ (assembly vs core, $n=36$) & $+0.890$ \\
    Assembly ranks 1 / 2 / 3 at core level & 11 / 9 / 12 \\
    Largest gains (``discarded gems'') & d47: \#14$\to$\#4;
      d43: \#9$\to$\#2; d42: \#11$\to$\#5 \\
    Core-level Pareto front & single design (d46), violating
      $F\le2.0$ \\
    Designs meeting $F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}\le2.0$ & 0 of 36 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The bridge model (Eq.~\ref{eq:bridge}) fitted on all 36 designs,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{F^\mathrm{core}}{F^\mathrm{asm}}
  = 1.400 \;-\; 0.0045\,t_\mathrm{refl} \;+\; 0.343\,p,
  \qquad R^2 = 0.746,
  \label{eq:bridge-fit}
\end{equation}
confirms reflector and pitch as the corrupting variables --- and its
residual quarter is the argument for measuring rather than correcting.
Route-B closure on the finalists gives residuals of $-91$ to
$-429$\,pcm (most negative for thick reflectors), validating the
cycle-length proxy at the $\sim$2\% level, and the core-scale entropy
sentinel caught one case converging at batch 94 against a 40-batch
cutoff --- the reason the production core solves carry 60--120 inactive
batches.

\textbf{The methodological statement of the thesis follows:} a
surrogate-assisted optimisation on a reduced-order proxy must validate
the proxy's \emph{rank fidelity} near the optimum, not merely its
statistical convergence --- correlation weakens exactly where the
optimiser converges, and it did so here twice (estimator bias in
Campaign 2, geometric proxy in Campaign 3).

\section{Campaign 4: the core-level objective}
\label{sec:res-c4}

Campaign 4 moves $F_{\Delta H}$ and $g_\mathrm{peak}$ to core level at
$\approx3\%$ evaluation cost (Eq.~\ref{eq:corecost}). Its 36-point DOE
is complete (Table~\ref{tab:c4-doe}, Fig.~\ref{fig:c4conflict}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{th_c4_conflict}
  \caption{Campaign 4. (a) The binding constraint flips when the same
  limit is evaluated at the right level. (b) The structural conflict:
  low core peaking demands the high enrichment that the reactivity
  limit forbids.}
  \label{fig:c4conflict}
\end{figure}

The best design (core $F_{\Delta H}=1.9215$, the only value below 2.0
in the DOE) fails only $g_{k\max}$, by $+0.0455$ --- and closing that
gap by concentration alone is impossible:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\mathrm{gd} = \frac{0.0455}{0.0066} \approx 6.9~\mathrm{wt\%}
  \;\Rightarrow\; 8.7~\mathrm{wt\%} > 8.0~\mathrm{(bound)}.
  \label{eq:gd-gap}
\end{equation}
Low core peaking requires high enrichment (it flattens the radial
gradient); the reactivity limit forbids it; the twelve-pin absorber
cannot bridge the two. The missing lever is absorber \emph{inventory}.
