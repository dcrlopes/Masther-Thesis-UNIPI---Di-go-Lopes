\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

\section{Design basis and technological conservatism}
\label{sec:design-basis}

The reference small modular reactor is conceived under a deliberate
constraint of \emph{technological conservatism}: the core must rest on
nuclear-fuel technology already industrialised in Brazil. The work then
characterises a plausibly deployable naval core, not a green-field
concept, and departures from established domestic practice are admitted
only where physically necessary to meet the mission objective --- a long
refuelling interval under low-enriched uranium (LEU).

The element carried over unchanged from national production is the fuel
rod. UO$_2$ ceramic pellets, Zircaloy-4 cladding, and the rod-fabrication
route operated by Ind\'ustrias Nucleares do Brasil (INB) at the
F\'abrica de Combust\'iveis Nucleares in Resende --- which supplies
reload fuel for Angra~1 and~2~\cite{inb_fcn_resende} --- are adopted
without modification.

The reference core departs from the $16\times16$ lattice of the Angra
units~\cite{angra_fuel_1_2} by adopting the $17\times17$
Westinghouse-type assembly: 264 fuel rods, 24 guide tubes and one
instrumentation tube. This changes the assembly \emph{skeleton}, not the
rod technology. The $17\times17$ lattice is the most widely fabricated
and best-benchmarked PWR geometry in service, so its adoption is an
incremental industrialisation step --- and it provides the validated
basis for benchmarking this model against the open
literature~\cite{nuscale_htp2_2017,nuscale_like_core_2024}.

Exactly two departures from strict Angra heritage are therefore
admitted: enrichment raised toward the LEU ceiling, and the
$17\times17$ lattice. Radial enrichment zoning and gadolinia burnable
absorbers, exercised by the optimiser, are standard PWR reload practice
consistent with the same basis.

\section{Optimisation problem}
\label{sec:meth-problem}

\subsection{Problem statement}

The core design task is posed as a constrained two-objective problem over
a five-dimensional design space. The design space is summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:design-space} and the constraints in
Table~\ref{tab:constraints}.

The first objective maximises the cycle length.

\begin{equation}
\max_{\mathbf{x}\,\in\,\mathcal{X}} \; \mathrm{EFPD}(\mathbf{x})
\label{eq:obj_efpd}
\end{equation}

The second objective minimises the radial peaking factor.

\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{x}\,\in\,\mathcal{X}} \; F_{\Delta H}(\mathbf{x})
\label{eq:obj_fdh}
\end{equation}

Both are subject to the feasibility condition below.

\begin{equation}
g_j(\mathbf{x}) \le 0,
\qquad j = 1,\dots,5
\label{eq:constraint_form}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathbf{x}$ is the design vector, whose five components and their
        units are listed in Table~\ref{tab:design-space}.

  \item $\mathcal{X}$ is the box-bounded design space defined by those
        same bounds.

  \item $\mathrm{EFPD}(\mathbf{x})$ is the cycle length of design
        $\mathbf{x}$ in effective full-power days, in d.

  \item $F_{\Delta H}(\mathbf{x})$ is the radial peaking factor of design
        $\mathbf{x}$, dimensionless.

  \item $g_j(\mathbf{x})$ is the $j$-th inequality constraint in
        normalised violation form, dimensionless, feasible when
        non-positive.
\end{itemize}

The evaluation level of $F_{\Delta H}$ changes between campaigns and is
defined in Section~\ref{sec:meth-core-objective}.

\subsection{Geometric feasibility}

The vessel-fit constraint $g_\mathrm{geom}$ uses the circumscribed
envelope of the intact core footprint.

\begin{equation}
R_\mathrm{env}(p) = \sqrt{13}\,\cdot\,17\,p
\label{eq:renv}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $R_\mathrm{env}$ is the circumscribed radius of the fuel region,
        in cm.

  \item $p$ is the pin pitch, in cm.

  \item $17$ is the number of pin cells per assembly side, dimensionless,
        so that $17p$ is the assembly pitch in cm.

  \item $\sqrt{13} \approx 3.606$ is the dimensionless distance from the
        core centre to the outermost assembly corner of the 32-assembly
        footprint, expressed in assembly pitches.
\end{itemize}

The sensitivity of the envelope to the pitch follows directly.

\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}R_\mathrm{env}}{\mathrm{d}p} = 61.3~\mathrm{cm\,cm^{-1}}
\label{eq:drenv}
\end{equation}

A pitch increase of one millimetre therefore consumes about six
millimetres of radial budget. Pitch and reflector thickness compete
directly for the fixed 90\,cm allowance, which makes the two variables
strongly coupled rather than independent.

The constraint is enforced analytically inside the optimiser rather than
during model construction. Campaign 1 showed that omitting it lets the
genetic algorithm converge on designs that cannot be built, because the
fuel region and the reflector together exceed the vessel envelope.

\subsection{Objective functions}
\label{sec:objective_functions}

\subsubsection{Cycle length}

The cycle length is the depletion time at which the core loses
criticality, evaluated from the depletion history of the multiplication
factor.

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{EFPD} = \min \left\{\, t > 0 \;:\; k_\mathrm{eff}(t) \le 1 \,\right\}
\label{eq:efpd_definition}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathrm{EFPD}$ is the cycle length in effective full-power days,
        in d.

  \item $t$ is the depletion time at nominal full power, in d.

  \item $k_\mathrm{eff}(t)$ is the multiplication factor at depletion time
        $t$, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

The crossing time is obtained by interpolation between the two depletion
steps that bracket unity. Designs that remain critical at the end of the
prescribed depletion horizon are right-censored and are recorded at the
horizon value rather than extrapolated. The stopping rules of
Section~\ref{sec:stopping_rules} require that no censored design remains
on the Pareto front at convergence.

\subsubsection{Radial peaking factor}

The radial peaking factor is the ratio between the axially integrated
linear power of the most heavily loaded fuel rod and the average axially
integrated linear power of all fuel rods in the tally domain.

\begin{equation}
F_{\Delta H}
=
\frac{\displaystyle \max_{i \in \{1,\dots,N\}} \int_{0}^{H} q'_i(z)\,\mathrm{d}z}
     {\displaystyle \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} \int_{0}^{H} q'_j(z)\,\mathrm{d}z}
\label{eq:fdh_definition}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $F_{\Delta H}$ is the radial peaking factor, dimensionless, equal
        to unity for a perfectly flat radial power distribution.

  \item $q'_i(z)$ is the linear power density of fuel rod $i$ at axial
        elevation $z$, in W\,cm$^{-1}$.

  \item $z$ is the axial coordinate measured from the bottom of the active
        fuel region, in cm.

  \item $H$ is the active fuel height, in cm.

  \item $N$ is the number of fuel rods in the tally domain,
        dimensionless.

  \item $i$ and $j$ are fuel rod indices, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

The name of the factor follows from the coolant enthalpy rise along the
hot channel, which is proportional to the axially integrated power of the
adjacent rod. The factor is therefore a thermal-hydraulic margin
indicator expressed in purely neutronic terms.

\paragraph{Numerical estimator.}
The integrals of Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_definition} are replaced by their
Monte Carlo counterparts. A pin-wise fission energy deposition tally is
accumulated over the active fuel region, so each rod contributes one
volume-integrated score.

\begin{equation}
\widehat{F}_{\Delta H}
=
\frac{\displaystyle \max_{i \in \{1,\dots,N\}} \widehat{P}_i}
     {\displaystyle \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} \widehat{P}_j}
\label{eq:fdh_estimator}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\widehat{F}_{\Delta H}$ is the Monte Carlo estimate of the radial
        peaking factor, dimensionless.

  \item $\widehat{P}_i$ is the tallied fission energy deposition rate of
        fuel rod $i$ over the full active height, in W.
\end{itemize}

Two properties of this estimator govern the selection methodology.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The estimate carries a statistical uncertainty inherited from the
        finite number of simulated histories. Differences between
        candidate designs smaller than that uncertainty cannot be resolved
        by a single evaluation.

  \item The estimate is positively biased. The numerator is a maximum over
        $N$ noisy scores, so fluctuation alone raises the sampled maximum
        above the true maximum, and the bias grows with $N$ and with the
        tally variance.
\end{enumerate}

Both properties are quantified in Section~\ref{sec:peaking_statistics}, and the procedure that separates true optima from noise-driven apparent optima is developed in Section~\ref{sec:selection_methodology}.

\paragraph{Evaluation fidelity.}
\label{sec:meth-core-objective}
The tally domain of Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_estimator} determines what the
objective represents. Two levels are used in this work.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Assembly level, in which $N$ covers the fuel rods of a single
        reflected assembly. This level is inexpensive and served as the
        screening objective in Campaigns 1 to 3.

  \item Core level, in which $N$ covers the fuel rods of the full
        32-assembly core at beginning of life. This level supplies both
        the objective and the peaking constraint from Campaign 4 onward.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Leakage-corrected reactivity target (Route B)}
\label{sec:meth-ktarget}

End of cycle on the reflective assembly is defined at the
leakage-corrected target
\begin{equation}
  k_\mathrm{target}(p, t_\mathrm{refl})
  \;=\; \frac{k_\infty^\mathrm{assembly}}{k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}},
\end{equation}
pre-computed on a $3\times7$ grid over (pitch, reflector thickness) and
bilinearly interpolated. Route~B and a reflected-assembly sleeve model
(Route~A) are mutually exclusive --- using both would double-count
reflector leakage; Route~B is adopted. The construction is validated
a~posteriori on the finalists (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-corevalidation}).

\section{Surrogate-assisted active-learning loop}
\label{sec:meth-loop}

The campaign follows the dual-stage
pattern~\cite{kit_inr_proposal,whyte_parks_2021}: Latin-hypercube design
of experiments~\cite{mckay_lhs_1979}; Gaussian-process surrogate with
automatic relevance determination~\cite{rasmussen_gp_2006} trained on
the whole archive; NSGA-II~\cite{deb_nsga2_2002} search on the
surrogate; small infill batches evaluated with OpenMC. Blocks of three
iterations are separated by analysis, and the stopping rule requires
hypervolume gain below 1\% for three consecutive iterations with the
front fully resolved.

The Campaign-3 fidelity was chosen from a measured ladder: it removes
$\approx$87\% of the peaking-estimator bias and two thirds of its noise
at $\approx$7$\times$ cost. Per-design deterministic seeds (a CRC32 hash
of the design vector; the core solve adds a salt) keep every evaluation
reproducible while making the noise independent across designs --- the
i.i.d.\ assumption of the Gaussian-process noise model.

\section{ATF discharge-burnup limit}
\label{sec:meth-atf}

Cycle length and burnup are linearly related through the constant
specific power,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{EFPD} = \frac{1000\,B_\mathrm{EOC}}{P_\mathrm{spec}},
  \qquad P_\mathrm{spec} = 9.9834~\mathrm{W/gHM}.
\end{equation}
Campaign 2 imposed the accident-tolerant-fuel limit retrospectively as
an exact transform; Campaigns 3--4 terminate depletion at
75\,MWd/kgHM inside the loop, so censoring is eliminated by
construction. Hypervolume histories reset whenever the objective
definition changes.

\section{Statistical treatment of the peaking estimator}
\label{sec:meth-stats}

$F_{\Delta H}$ is the maximum over $\sim$264 noisy mesh cells: an
extreme order statistic, noisy \emph{and} biased upward, with the bias
largest for flat power distributions --- exactly the designs a
peaking-minimising optimiser prefers.

Final selection therefore uses two-stage subset selection over a
\emph{noise-margin} candidate set (every feasible design within
$k\sigma_\mathrm{seed}$ of the observed minimum, $k=4$--$6$): Gupta-style
screening~\cite{gupta_1965}, then Welch tests with Bonferroni correction
at confirmation fidelity. Values adjusted across fidelities carry the
measured inter-fidelity shift with its spread propagated, and are
flagged as adjusted wherever reported.

\section{Proxy validation and the core-level objective (Campaign 4)}
\label{sec:meth-core-objective}

Optimising a reduced-order proxy requires validating the proxy's
\emph{rank fidelity} to the true objective, not only its statistical
convergence. Three instruments implement this.

\textbf{Finalist validation.} Each Pareto finalist is rebuilt as the
full 32-assembly core and solved at BOL with a pin-resolved mesh over
the whole footprint, seed-replicated; the ratio
$F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}/F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}$ is the proxy gap.
The same run yields $k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}$, closing Route~B on
the finalists for free.

\textbf{Archive rescoring.} Every feasible archive design is rescored at
core level (two-stage: 3-seed screen, 8-seed resolution of the
noise-margin top), giving the Spearman rank correlation between proxy
and truth and a direct answer to whether the proxy discarded superior
designs.

\textbf{Bridge model.} The multiplicative discrepancy is fitted in the
Kennedy--O'Hagan spirit~\cite{kennedy_ohagan_2000,forrester_2007},
\begin{equation}
  \frac{F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}}{F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}}
  \;\approx\; a + b\,t_\mathrm{refl} + c\,p ,
  \label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}
to quantify how much of the gap a low-dimensional correction could
carry.

The Campaign-4 decision follows from cost: the core BOL solve costs
$\sim$104\,s against a $\sim$110\,min evaluation,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\Delta t}{t} \;\approx\; \frac{113 - 110}{110}
  \;\approx\; 3\%,
  \label{eq:corecost}
\end{equation}
(measured wall times), so the true quantity is measured
\emph{inside the loop} rather than corrected: $F_{\Delta H}$ and
$g_\mathrm{peak}$ are evaluated on the core, the assembly value is
retained as a diagnostic, and EFPD keeps the validated assembly +
Route-B evaluation. This is the deletion of the proxy where deletion is
affordable, preferred over co-kriging or trust-region model
management~\cite{alexandrov_2001,poloczek_miso_2017,kandasamy_boca_2017}
on simplicity grounds.

\section{Soluble boron treatment}
\label{sec:meth-boron}

The coolant carries a \textbf{constant 1000\,ppm} of natural boron in
all campaigns, matching the NuScale-like benchmark
conditions~\cite{nuscale_like_core_2024}. Real operation instead
\emph{lets boron down} continuously toward $\sim$0\,ppm at end of
cycle; and naval practice avoids soluble boron altogether, controlling
with burnable absorbers and rods
(soluble-boron-free SMR practice~\cite{ismr_sbf_2024,acp100_physor2020}).

Two consequences are recorded rather than fixed here. Removing the
boron \emph{raises} beginning-of-life reactivity by
$\Delta k \approx +0.08$ to $+0.10$, tightening $g_{k\max}$ and shifting
the entire feasibility envelope --- boron-free is more realistic for the
naval mission but makes the reactivity problem harder, not easier.
Second, the constant-boron end-of-cycle criterion is conservative for
below-cap designs (a real core dilutes and runs longer), with a bias
that is enrichment-dependent. Under the one-change rule the treatment is
kept fixed through Campaign~4; the planned Campaign-5 decision is
preceded by a direct measurement (two finalists re-depleted at 0\,ppm)
so the design-basis choice rests on numbers, not estimates.

\section{Diagnostics, environments, reproducibility}
\label{sec:meth-diagnostics}

Shannon-entropy source-convergence monitoring is attached to every
model; the reflective single assembly is source-converged from batch~1,
while the finite core has a real transient --- one core case converged
only at batch~94, hence the generous 60--120 inactive batches of the
core solves, with a per-run sentinel recorded in the archive.

All work runs on a 64-core workstation without administrative
privileges. The reproducibility unit is the build-pinned Conda
environment (bit-identical OpenMC binaries, verified by smoke execution
and $k_\infty$ agreement); checkpoints, the $k$-target table, every
analysis script and every figure generator are version-controlled, so
each reported number and figure is reproducible from the repository
alone.
