\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

\section{Design basis and technological conservatism}
\label{sec:design-basis}

The reference small modular reactor is conceived under a deliberate
constraint of \emph{technological conservatism}: the core must rest on
nuclear-fuel technology already industrialised in Brazil. The work then
characterises a plausibly deployable naval core, not a green-field
concept. Departures from established domestic practice are admitted
only where physically necessary to meet the mission objective, namely a
long refuelling interval under low-enriched uranium (LEU).

The element carried over unchanged from national production is the fuel
rod. UO$_2$ ceramic pellets, Zircaloy-4 cladding, and the
rod-fabrication route operated by Ind\'ustrias Nucleares do Brasil
(INB) at the F\'abrica de Combust\'iveis Nucleares in Resende, which
supplies reload fuel for Angra~1 and~2~\cite{inb_fcn_resende}, are
adopted without modification.

The reference core departs from the $16\times16$ lattice of the Angra
units~\cite{angra_fuel_1_2} by adopting the $17\times17$
Westinghouse-type assembly, with 264 fuel rods, 24 guide tubes and one
instrumentation tube. This changes the assembly \emph{skeleton}, not
the rod technology. The $17\times17$ lattice is the most widely
fabricated and best-benchmarked PWR geometry in service, so its
adoption is an incremental industrialisation step. It also provides the
validated basis for benchmarking this model against the open
literature~\cite{nuscale_htp2_2017,nuscale_like_core_2024}.

Exactly two departures from strict Angra heritage are therefore
admitted.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Enrichment raised toward the LEU ceiling.

  \item The $17\times17$ lattice.
\end{enumerate}

Radial enrichment zoning and gadolinia burnable absorbers, exercised by
the optimiser, are standard PWR reload practice consistent with the
same basis.

\section{Optimisation problem}
\label{sec:meth-problem}

\subsection{Problem statement}

The core design task is posed as a constrained two-objective problem
over a five-dimensional design space. The design space is summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:design-space} and the constraints in
Table~\ref{tab:constraints}.

The first objective maximises the cycle length.

\begin{equation}
\max_{\mathbf{x}\,\in\,\mathcal{X}} \, \mathrm{EFPD}(\mathbf{x})
\label{eq:obj_efpd}
\end{equation}

The second objective minimises the radial peaking factor.

\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{x}\,\in\,\mathcal{X}} \, F_{\Delta H}(\mathbf{x})
\label{eq:obj_fdh}
\end{equation}

Both are subject to the feasibility condition below.

\begin{equation}
g_j(\mathbf{x}) \le 0,
\qquad j = 1,\dots,5
\label{eq:constraint_form}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathbf{x}$ is the design vector, whose five components and
        their units are listed in Table~\ref{tab:design-space}.

  \item $\mathcal{X}$ is the box-bounded design space defined by those
        same bounds.

  \item $\mathrm{EFPD}(\mathbf{x})$ is the cycle length of design
        $\mathbf{x}$ in effective full-power days, in d, obtained from
        Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd} with the end-of-cycle criterion of
        Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}.

  \item $F_{\Delta H}(\mathbf{x})$ is the radial peaking factor of
        design $\mathbf{x}$, dimensionless, defined in
        Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_definition}.

  \item $g_j(\mathbf{x})$ is the $j$-th inequality constraint in
        normalised violation form, dimensionless, feasible when
        non-positive.
\end{itemize}

The evaluation level of $F_{\Delta H}$ changes between campaigns and is
fixed in Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}, with the validation
that motivated the change in Section~\ref{sec:meth-core-objective}.

The numerical bound of $g_\mathrm{peak}$ is anchored in licensing
practice. The radial enthalpy-rise hot channel factor is a controlled
design limit that every pressurised water reactor documents in its
safety analysis report, and the publicly available U.S.~EPR Final
Safety Analysis Report is the representative example adopted
here~\cite{epr_fsar_ch4}. The value of 2.0 is a deliberately generous
screening bound for this exploratory single-batch study rather than the
limit of any specific plant. Feasibility against it therefore screens
candidate designs and does not claim compliance with a licensed core.

\subsection{Geometric feasibility}

The vessel-fit constraint $g_\mathrm{geom}$ uses the circumscribed
envelope of the intact core footprint.

\begin{equation}
R_\mathrm{env}(p) = \sqrt{13}\,\cdot\,17\,p
\label{eq:renv}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $R_\mathrm{env}$ is the circumscribed radius of the fuel
        region, in cm.

  \item $p$ is the pin pitch, in cm.

  \item $17$ is the number of pin cells per assembly side,
        dimensionless, so that $17p$ is the assembly pitch in cm.

  \item $\sqrt{13} \approx 3.606$ is the dimensionless distance from
        the core centre to the outermost fuel corner of the
        $6\times6$-minus-corners map, in assembly pitches. The extreme
        corner lies three assembly pitches along one axis and two along
        the other, so its distance is $\sqrt{3^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{13}$.
\end{itemize}

The sensitivity of the envelope to the pitch follows directly.

\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}R_\mathrm{env}}{\mathrm{d}p} = 61.3~\mathrm{cm\,cm^{-1}}
\label{eq:drenv}
\end{equation}

A pitch increase of one millimetre therefore consumes about six
millimetres of radial budget. Pitch and reflector thickness compete
directly for the fixed allowance of the 90\,cm vessel internal radius
of Table~\ref{tab:model-geometry}, which makes the two variables
strongly coupled rather than independent.

The constraint is enforced analytically inside the optimiser rather
than during model construction. Campaign 1 showed that omitting it lets
the genetic algorithm converge on designs that cannot be built, because
the fuel region and the reflector together exceed the vessel envelope.

\subsection{Objective functions}
\label{sec:objective_functions}

\subsubsection{Cycle length}

The cycle-length objective of Equation~\eqref{eq:obj_efpd} is computed
from the end-of-cycle burnup through the general conversion of
Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd}, with the specific power of
Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}. What remains to be fixed here is the
criterion that defines the end of cycle on the depletion workhorse, the
reflective single assembly.

\begin{equation}
B_\mathrm{EOC} = \min\!\left( B_k,\, B_\mathrm{ATF} \right)
\label{eq:eoc}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
B_k = \min\left\{\, B > 0 \,:\, k_\infty(B) \le k_\mathrm{target} \,\right\}
\label{eq:eoc-k}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $B_\mathrm{EOC}$ is the end-of-cycle burnup entering
        Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd}, in MWd/kgHM.

  \item $B_k$ is the reactivity-defined end-of-cycle burnup, in
        MWd/kgHM.

  \item $B_\mathrm{ATF}$ is the accident-tolerant-fuel discharge-burnup
        limit, equal to 75\,MWd/kgHM, treated in
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-atf}.

  \item $B$ is the burnup along the depletion history, in MWd/kgHM.

  \item $k_\infty(B)$ is the infinite multiplication factor of the
        reflective assembly at burnup $B$, dimensionless.

  \item $k_\mathrm{target}$ is the leakage-corrected end-of-cycle
        target, dimensionless, defined in
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget}.
\end{itemize}

The crossing $B_k$ is obtained by interpolation between the two
depletion steps that bracket the target.

The discharge limit entered the objective in Campaign 2,
retrospectively, through the exact post-processing transform of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-atf}. Designs still critical at the depletion
horizon in the early campaigns were right-censored and recorded at the
horizon value rather than extrapolated. From Campaign 3 onward the
depletion terminates at $B_\mathrm{ATF}$ inside the loop, so
Equation~\eqref{eq:eoc} holds by construction and censoring is
eliminated. The stopping rule of Section~\ref{sec:meth-loop}
additionally requires that no censored design remains on the Pareto
front at convergence.

\subsubsection{Radial peaking factor}

The peaking objective of Equation~\eqref{eq:obj_fdh} uses the radial
peaking factor of Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_definition}, formally defined
in Section~\ref{sec:bg-peaking}. That definition leaves two
methodological choices open, and both are fixed here: the numerical
estimator and the evaluation domain.

\paragraph{Numerical estimator.}
The integrals of Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_definition} are replaced by
their Monte Carlo counterparts. A pin-wise fission energy deposition
tally is accumulated over the active fuel region, so each rod
contributes one volume-integrated score.

\begin{equation}
\widehat{F}_{\Delta H}
=
\frac{\displaystyle \max_{i \in \{1,\dots,N\}} \widehat{P}_i}
     {\displaystyle \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} \widehat{P}_j}
\label{eq:fdh_estimator}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\widehat{F}_{\Delta H}$ is the Monte Carlo estimate of the
        radial peaking factor, dimensionless.

  \item $\widehat{P}_i$ is the tallied fission energy deposition rate
        of fuel rod $i$ over the full active height, in W.

  \item $N$ is the number of fuel rods in the evaluation domain,
        dimensionless.

  \item $i$ and $j$ are fuel rod indices, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

Two properties of this estimator govern the selection methodology.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The estimate carries a statistical uncertainty inherited from
        the finite number of simulated histories. Differences between
        candidate designs smaller than that uncertainty cannot be
        resolved by a single evaluation.

  \item The estimate is positively biased. The numerator is a maximum
        over $N$ noisy scores, so fluctuation alone raises the sampled
        maximum above the true maximum, and the bias grows with $N$ and
        with the tally variance.
\end{enumerate}

Both properties are quantified in Section~\ref{sec:meth-stats}, which
also develops the two-stage subset-selection procedure that separates
true optima from noise-driven apparent optima.

\paragraph{Evaluation domain.}
The tally domain of Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_estimator} determines what
the objective represents. Two levels are used in this work.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Assembly level, in which $N$ covers the 264 fuel rods of a
        single reflected assembly. This level is inexpensive and served
        as the screening objective in Campaigns 1 to 3.

  \item Core level, in which $N$ covers the fuel rods of the full
        32-assembly core at beginning of life. This level supplies both
        the objective and the peaking constraint from Campaign 4
        onward.
\end{enumerate}

The validation programme that motivated the move to core level is
presented in Section~\ref{sec:meth-core-objective}, and its
measurements in Section~\ref{sec:res-corevalidation}.

\section{Leakage-corrected reactivity target (Route B)}
\label{sec:meth-ktarget}

End of cycle on the reflective assembly is defined at the
leakage-corrected target introduced qualitatively in
Section~\ref{sec:bg-k}.

\begin{equation}
k_\mathrm{target}(p, t_\mathrm{refl})
= \frac{k_\infty^\mathrm{assembly}}{k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}}
\label{eq:ktarget}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $k_\mathrm{target}$ is the leakage-corrected end-of-cycle
        target entering Equation~\eqref{eq:eoc-k}, dimensionless.

  \item $k_\infty^\mathrm{assembly}$ is the infinite multiplication
        factor of the reflective single assembly, dimensionless.

  \item $k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}$ is the effective multiplication
        factor of the 32-assembly core built with the same pitch and
        reflector thickness, dimensionless.

  \item $p$ is the pin pitch, in cm.

  \item $t_\mathrm{refl}$ is the reflector thickness, in cm.
\end{itemize}

The ratio is pre-computed on a $3\times7$ grid over pitch and reflector
thickness and bilinearly interpolated inside the loop.

Two constructions could carry the leakage correction, and they are
mutually exclusive because using both would double-count the reflector
leakage.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Route A, a reflected-assembly sleeve model.

  \item Route B, the pre-computed target of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:ktarget}.
\end{enumerate}

Route B is adopted. The construction is validated a posteriori on the
finalists in Section~\ref{sec:res-corevalidation}.

\section{Surrogate-assisted active-learning loop}
\label{sec:meth-loop}

The campaign follows the dual-stage
pattern~\cite{kit_inr_proposal,whyte_parks_2021}, whose four steps
repeat until the stopping rule is met.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A Latin-hypercube design of
        experiments~\cite{mckay_lhs_1979} seeds the archive, and each
        subsequent iteration adds a small infill batch evaluated with
        OpenMC.

  \item A Gaussian-process surrogate with automatic relevance
        determination~\cite{rasmussen_gp_2006} is trained on the whole
        cumulative archive.

  \item NSGA-II~\cite{deb_nsga2_2002} searches the surrogate in place
        of the expensive code.

  \item The most informative proposals form the next infill batch.
\end{enumerate}

Blocks of three iterations are separated by analysis. The empirical
stopping rule has two conditions, both required.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The hypervolume gain stays below 1\% for three consecutive
        iterations.

  \item No censored design remains on the Pareto front, so the front is
        fully resolved.
\end{enumerate}

\section{ATF discharge-burnup limit}
\label{sec:meth-atf}

The discharge limit adopted is $B_\mathrm{ATF} = 75$\,MWd/kgHM.

Cycle length and burnup are linearly related through the constant
specific power, Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd}, with $P_\mathrm{spec}$ given
in Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}. The burnup limit therefore maps
one-to-one onto a cycle-length cap.

Campaign 2 imposed the limit retrospectively, as an exact
post-processing transform of the completed campaign. Campaigns 3 and 4
terminate depletion at $B_\mathrm{ATF}$ inside the loop, so censoring
is eliminated by construction. Hypervolume histories reset whenever the
objective definition changes.

\section{Statistical treatment of the peaking estimator}
\label{sec:meth-stats}

$F_{\Delta H}$ is the maximum over $N$ noisy mesh cells, with $N = 264$
at assembly level. It is therefore an extreme order statistic, noisy
\emph{and} biased upward, as anticipated in Section~\ref{sec:bg-mc}.
The bias is largest for flat power distributions, which are exactly the
designs a peaking-minimising optimiser prefers.

Final selection therefore uses a two-stage subset selection over a
\emph{noise-margin} candidate set. The candidate set contains every
feasible design within $k\,\sigma_\mathrm{seed}$ of the observed
minimum, where $\sigma_\mathrm{seed}$ is the seed-to-seed standard
deviation of the estimator and $k = 4$--$6$ is the margin multiplier.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Gupta-style screening~\cite{gupta_1965} retains every candidate
        whose confidence interval overlaps the incumbent's.

  \item Welch tests with Bonferroni correction, at confirmation
        fidelity, resolve the retained candidates pairwise.
\end{enumerate}

Values adjusted across fidelities carry the measured inter-fidelity
shift with its spread propagated. They are flagged as adjusted wherever
reported.

\section{Proxy validation and the core-level objective (Campaign 4)}
\label{sec:meth-core-objective}

Optimising a reduced-order proxy requires validating the proxy's
\emph{rank fidelity} to the true objective, not only its statistical
convergence. Three instruments implement this.

\paragraph{Finalist validation.}
Each Pareto finalist is rebuilt as the full 32-assembly core and solved
at BOL with a pin-resolved mesh over the whole footprint,
seed-replicated. The ratio
$F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}/F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}$ is the proxy
gap. The same run yields $k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}$, closing
Route~B on the finalists at no additional cost.

\paragraph{Archive rescoring.}
Every feasible archive design is rescored at core level in two stages,
a 3-seed screen followed by an 8-seed resolution of the noise-margin
top. This gives the Spearman rank correlation between proxy and truth
and a direct answer to whether the proxy discarded superior designs.

\paragraph{Bridge model.}
The multiplicative discrepancy is fitted in the Kennedy--O'Hagan
spirit~\cite{kennedy_ohagan_2000,forrester_2007}.

\begin{equation}
\frac{F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}}{F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}}
\approx a + b\,t_\mathrm{refl} + c\,p
\label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}$ and $F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}$
        are the peaking factors of the same design at core and assembly
        level, dimensionless.

  \item $a$ is the fitted intercept, dimensionless.

  \item $b$ is the fitted coefficient of the reflector thickness, in
        cm$^{-1}$.

  \item $c$ is the fitted coefficient of the pin pitch, in cm$^{-1}$.

  \item $t_\mathrm{refl}$ is the reflector thickness, in cm.

  \item $p$ is the pin pitch, in cm.
\end{itemize}

The purpose of the fit is to quantify how much of the gap a
low-dimensional correction could carry. The fitted values on the
Campaign-3 archive are given in Equation~\eqref{eq:bridge-fit}.

The Campaign-4 decision follows from cost. The core BOL solve costs
$\sim$104\,s against a $\sim$110\,min evaluation.

\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta t}{t} \approx \frac{113 - 110}{110} \approx 3\%
\label{eq:corecost}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Delta t$ is the measured wall-time increment of one
        evaluation when the core BOL solve is added, in min.

  \item $t$ is the measured wall time of one evaluation without the
        core solve, in min.

  \item $113$ and $110$ are the measured wall times with and without
        the core solve, in min.
\end{itemize}

At this cost the true quantity is measured \emph{inside the loop}
rather than corrected. $F_{\Delta H}$ and $g_\mathrm{peak}$ are
evaluated on the core, the assembly value is retained as a diagnostic,
and EFPD keeps the validated assembly plus Route-B evaluation. This is
the deletion of the proxy where deletion is affordable, preferred over
co-kriging or trust-region model
management~\cite{alexandrov_2001,poloczek_miso_2017,kandasamy_boca_2017}
on simplicity grounds.

\section{Soluble boron treatment}
\label{sec:meth-boron}

The coolant carries a \textbf{constant 1000\,ppm} of natural boron in
all campaigns, matching the NuScale-like benchmark
conditions~\cite{nuscale_like_core_2024}.

Real operation differs in two ways. An operating PWR \emph{lets boron
down} continuously toward $\sim$0\,ppm at end of cycle. Naval practice
avoids soluble boron altogether and controls with burnable absorbers
and rods~\cite{ismr_sbf_2024,acp100_physor2020}.

Two consequences are recorded rather than fixed here.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Removing the boron \emph{raises} beginning-of-life reactivity
        by $\Delta k \approx +0.08$ to $+0.10$, tightening $g_{k\max}$
        and shifting the entire feasibility envelope. Boron-free is
        more realistic for the naval mission but makes the reactivity
        problem harder, not easier.

  \item The constant-boron end-of-cycle criterion is conservative for
        below-cap designs, because a real core dilutes and runs longer,
        with a bias that is enrichment-dependent.
\end{enumerate}

Under the one-change rule the treatment is kept fixed through
Campaign~4. The planned Campaign-5 decision is preceded by a direct
measurement, with two finalists re-depleted at 0\,ppm, so the
design-basis choice rests on numbers, not estimates.

\section{Diagnostics, environments, reproducibility}
\label{sec:meth-diagnostics}

Shannon-entropy source-convergence monitoring is attached to every
model. The reflective single assembly is source-converged from
batch~1. The finite core has a real transient, and one core case
converged only at batch~94, hence the generous 60--120 inactive batches
of the core solves, with a per-run sentinel recorded in the archive.

All work runs on a 64-core workstation without administrative
privileges. The reproducibility unit is the build-pinned Conda
environment, with bit-identical OpenMC binaries verified by smoke
execution and $k_\infty$ agreement. Checkpoints, the $k$-target table,
every analysis script and every figure generator are
version-controlled, so each reported number and figure is reproducible
from the repository alone.
