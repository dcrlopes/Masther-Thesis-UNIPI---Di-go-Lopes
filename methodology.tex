\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

\section{Design basis and technological conservatism}
\label{sec:design-basis}

The reference small modular reactor is conceived under a deliberate
constraint of \emph{technological conservatism}: the core must rest on
nuclear-fuel technology already industrialised in Brazil. The work then
characterises a plausibly deployable naval core, not a green-field
concept. Departures from established domestic practice are admitted
only where physically necessary to meet the mission objective, namely a
long refuelling interval under low-enriched uranium (LEU).

The element carried over unchanged from national production is the fuel
rod. UO$_2$ ceramic pellets, zircaloy cladding, and the rod-fabrication
route operated by Ind\'ustrias Nucleares do Brasil (INB) at the
F\'abrica de Combust\'ivel Nuclear in Resende, which supplies the fuel of
Angra~1 and~2 with pellets enriched between 2 and
5\,wt\%~\cite{inb_montagem_ec,inb_resende}, are adopted without
modification. The cladding is modelled as Zircaloy-4. INB also
fabricated the UO$_2$ pellets of the LABGENE fuel, under a 2021 contract
with CTMSP, between August and December
2021~\cite{marinha_inb_labgene_2022}.

The reference core departs from the $16\times16$ lattice of the Angra
units by adopting the $17\times17$ Westinghouse-type assembly, with 264
fuel rods, 24 guide tubes and one instrumentation tube. The
$16\times16$ lattice of the Angra units follows from the published
counts. An Angra~1 assembly holds 235 fuel rods, 20 guide tubes and one
instrumentation tube, and an Angra~2 assembly holds 236 fuel rods and 20
guide tubes, so both fill the 256 positions of a $16\times16$
array~\cite{inb_montagem_ec,eletronuclear_angra1,eletronuclear_angra2}. This changes the assembly \emph{skeleton}, not
the rod technology. The $17\times17$ lattice is the most widely
fabricated and best-benchmarked PWR geometry in service, so its
adoption is an incremental industrialisation step. It also provides the
validated basis for benchmarking this model against the open
literature~\cite{nuscale_htp2_2017,nuscale_like_core_2024}.

Exactly two departures from strict Angra heritage are therefore
admitted.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Enrichment raised toward the LEU ceiling.

  \item The $17\times17$ lattice.
\end{enumerate}

Radial enrichment zoning and gadolinia burnable absorbers, exercised by
the optimiser, are standard PWR reload practice consistent with the
same basis.

\section{Reactor model}
\label{sec:reactor-model}

The model is built in OpenMC~0.15.3~\cite{romano_openmc_2015} with
ENDF/B-VII.1 data, from one parametric design dictionary driving three
fidelities: pin cell, $17\times17$ assembly, and the 32-assembly core.
Figure~\ref{fig:model-geometry} defines the geometry. In panel~(a) the
gadolinia rods are shaded by the value of $n_\mathrm{Gd}$ at which they
enter the nested ladder. The ranges of the six design variables of
Campaigns 5 to 7 are tabulated beneath the panels, where $p$ and
$t_\mathrm{refl}$ appear coupled by $g_\mathrm{geom}$. The numerical
basis is concentrated in Tables~\ref{tab:model-geometry}
and~\ref{tab:model-operating}.

The vessel internal radius is the 900\,mm inner radius of the simplified
vessel model drawn according to CTMSP in~\cite{maruyama_2013},
Figure~13, as detailed in Figure~\ref{fig:vessel-dimensioned}. The pin
and guide-tube dimensions follow NuScale-class
practice~\cite{nuscale_htp2_2017}. The core thermal power is the
48\,MW of thermal power that the Navy gives for the LABGENE
reactor~\cite{demartini_nuclep_2022}, within the 48 to 50\,MWth of the
academic literature~\cite{dinizcosta_2017}. The active height, the
enrichment zoning, the poisoned-pin pattern, the core map, the
reflector, the temperatures, the soluble boron concentration and the
nuclear data are modelling choices of this thesis.

Every depletion is run with the coupled operator of the OpenMC depletion
module~\cite{romano_2021_depletion} and the
\texttt{PredictorIntegrator}, the first-order constant-extrapolation
scheme of Equation~\eqref{eq:dep-ce}. The operator and the integrator
keep their default settings: reaction rates tallied directly, reaction
rates normalised to the power through the fission Q values of the
depletion chain (Equation~\eqref{eq:dep-norm}), and the matrix
exponential evaluated by CRAM of order 48
(Equation~\eqref{eq:cram})~\cite{openmc_docs}. The power of the depleted
model is its fresh heavy-metal mass multiplied by the specific power of
Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}, held constant through the cycle.

Verification at reference conditions reproduces the expected
multiplication hierarchy of Section~\ref{sec:bg-k}, with pin
$k_\infty = 1.296$, assembly $k_\infty = 1.288$ and core
$k_\mathrm{eff} = 1.221$. The assembly-internal peaking at reference
conditions is $F_{\Delta H} = 1.137$.

Three quantities of the campaign model depart from the benchmark
input files, and each is carried as a sensitivity in the
three-dimensional confirmation rather than corrected retrospectively,
because correcting them would change the basis of every campaign.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The heavy reflector is homogenised at \SI{90}{\%} steel by
        volume, a deliberately moderator-rich engineering estimate,
        against \SI{95.6}{\%} in the benchmark. A denser reflector
        returns more neutrons and raises the core eigenvalue by a
        design-independent amount.

  \item The control rod absorber radius is \SI{0.4331}{cm} for both
        absorbers, against \SI{0.4229}{cm} for boron carbide and
        \SI{0.4267}{cm} for silver-indium-cadmium in the benchmark.
        The clad radii, \num{0.4369} and \SI{0.4839}{cm}, and the
        absorber number densities are the benchmark's. The larger
        radius overstates the bank worth by a small amount that the
        confirmation quantifies.

  \item The coolant density is \SI{0.72}{g/cm^3} at \SI{580}{K},
        against \SI{0.752}{g/cm^3} implied by the benchmark number
        densities at its stated \SI{600}{K}. The boron mass fraction
        of \SI{1000}{ppm} is the same in both.
\end{enumerate}

The two-dimensional control screen also differs from the benchmark
rod in one axial respect that only the confirmation can resolve. The
screen inserts boron carbide over the whole fuel height, whereas the
benchmark rod leaves the bottom \SI{12.04}{cm} of the fuel unrodded,
the empty guide tube and the end plug, and carries \SI{30.48}{cm} of
silver-indium-cadmium before the boron carbide. The confirmation
solves both stacks, so that the conservatism of the screen is
measured in the presence of the benchmark rod rather than assumed.

\subsection{Treatment of the barrel and the downcomer in the campaign model}
\label{sec:meth-barrel}

The campaign transport model ends in a vacuum boundary at the outer
surface of the heavy reflector. The core barrel, the downcomer water
and the vessel wall are not in it. From Campaign 8 the space they
occupy is reserved by the vessel-fit constraint, so the geometry is
consistent, but the material is absent from the transport. This is a
deliberate simplification and it is defended on four grounds.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The effect is a nearly constant shift, and a constant shift
        does not reorder a Pareto front. What the barrel and the water
        do is return to the core a fraction of the neutrons that the
        vacuum boundary loses. The return depends on what lies behind
        the reflector, which is identical for every design, and on the
        current of neutrons reaching the reflector surface, which
        varies only weakly with the design. The evidence that such
        variations are weak in this core is measured rather than
        assumed. The Route B calibration of
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget} found the radial leakage factor
        governed by the geometry, the axial factor of
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-lax} varied by \SI{160}{pcm} over a
        factor of two in excess reactivity, and the reflector itself,
        which is the same kind of effect, moves the end-of-cycle target
        by \SI{51.6}{pcm} per centimetre of thickness.

  \item Route B was built for exactly this kind of correction. The
        leakage correction enters the loop through the end-of-cycle
        target, calibrated once per geometry on the full core. A barrel
        return of a few hundred pcm would enter the same way, at no
        cost to the loop, and the campaign designs would deplete a few
        hundred pcm further before reaching the target. The cycle
        lengths would shift together and the front would not.

  \item The controllability screen is conservative without it. A
        vacuum boundary makes the rodded core less subcritical than the
        real core, because the neutrons the barrel would return are
        lost. A design that passes the screen of
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-ctrl} therefore passes it with more
        margin once the barrel is present, and no design that fails
        the screen is made feasible by leakage that the model does not
        credit.

  \item The correction is measured on the candidates, not argued for
        the archive. The three-dimensional confirmation model of the
        candidate designs carries the structural components of the
        assemblies, the barrel, the downcomer and the vessel wall
        explicitly. Its eigenvalue against the two-dimensional solve of
        the same design gives a leakage factor with the barrel present,
        $L_\mathrm{ax}^\mathrm{hw}$, and the difference from the campaign
        value of \num{1.0289} is the effect of the barrel and the
        structural components. If it is constant across the
        candidates within its statistical resolution, the defence
        above is complete. If it is not, the candidates are re-ranked
        with the measured values, which the archive permits, because
        the correction acts on the target and not on the design.
\end{enumerate}

What this simplification does not permit is the claim that the
campaign cycle lengths are those of the finite core with its barrel.
They are leakage-corrected two-dimensional values, three-dimensional
in the axial direction from Campaign 8 onward, whose barrel correction
is measured on the candidates and reported with them.

The barrel return can be expressed as a correction to the target.

\begin{equation}
k_\mathrm{target}^\mathrm{hw}(\mathbf{x}) = k_\mathrm{target}^\mathrm{3D}(\mathbf{x}) \times \frac{L_\mathrm{ax}^\mathrm{hw}(\mathbf{x})}{L_\mathrm{ax}}
\label{eq:barrel-return}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $k_\mathrm{target}^\mathrm{hw}$ is the end-of-cycle target with
        the barrel, the downcomer and the structural components accounted for,
        dimensionless.

  \item $k_\mathrm{target}^\mathrm{3D}$ is the campaign target of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:ktarget-3d}, dimensionless.

  \item $L_\mathrm{ax}^\mathrm{hw}$ is the ratio of the two-dimensional
        eigenvalue to the eigenvalue of the finite-height model with
        structural components and barrel, measured on the candidate, dimensionless.

  \item $L_\mathrm{ax}$ is the campaign axial factor, \num{1.0289}.
\end{itemize}

A ratio below unity means the barrel returns more than the vacuum
boundary of the water-slab model lost, so the campaign target was
conservative and the finite-core cycle is longer than reported by the
corresponding amount.


\section{Optimisation problem}
\label{sec:meth-problem}

\subsection{Problem statement}

The core design task is posed as a constrained two-objective problem
over a design space of four to six variables, depending on the
campaign (Tables~\ref{tab:design-space}, \ref{tab:design-space-c56}
and~\ref{tab:design-space-c78}). The constraints are summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:constraints}. The evaluation level of $g_\mathrm{peak}$ is
the central methodological change of Campaign 4, the reactivity window
moves to the core basis in Campaign 6 (Section~\ref{sec:meth-kbasis}),
and the measured controllability screen enters in Campaign 7
(Section~\ref{sec:meth-ctrl}).

The first objective maximises the cycle length.

\begin{equation}
\max_{\mathbf{x}\,\in\,\mathcal{X}} \, \mathrm{EFPD}(\mathbf{x})
\label{eq:obj_efpd}
\end{equation}

The second objective minimises the radial peaking factor.

\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{x}\,\in\,\mathcal{X}} \, F_{\Delta H}(\mathbf{x})
\label{eq:obj_fdh}
\end{equation}

Both are subject to the feasibility condition below.

\begin{equation}
g_j(\mathbf{x}) \le 0,
\qquad j = 1,\dots,n_g
\label{eq:constraint_form}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathbf{x}$ is the design vector, whose components and their
        units are listed in Tables~\ref{tab:design-space},
        \ref{tab:design-space-c56} and~\ref{tab:design-space-c78}. It
        has five components in Campaigns 1 to 4, six in Campaigns 5
        to 7, and four in Campaigns 8 and 9.

  \item $\mathcal{X}$ is the box-bounded design space defined by those
        same bounds.

  \item $\mathrm{EFPD}(\mathbf{x})$ is the cycle length of design
        $\mathbf{x}$ in effective full-power days, in d, obtained from
        Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd} with the end-of-cycle criterion of
        Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}.

  \item $F_{\Delta H}(\mathbf{x})$ is the radial peaking factor of
        design $\mathbf{x}$, dimensionless, defined in
        Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_definition}.

  \item $g_j(\mathbf{x})$ is the $j$-th inequality constraint of
        Table~\ref{tab:constraints}, expressed in its own native unit
        and feasible when non-positive. The normalisation applied
        before the constraints are aggregated to rank infeasible
        populations is introduced in Campaign 6 and defined in
        Equation~\eqref{eq:cv-norm}.

  \item $n_g$ is the number of active inequality constraints,
        dimensionless, as recorded in the campaign checkpoints:

        \begin{itemize}
          \item four in Campaign 1, namely $g_{k\min}$, $g_{k\max}$,
                $g_\mathrm{enr}$ and $g_\mathrm{peak}$;

          \item five in Campaigns 2 to 6, when the analytic vessel-fit
                constraint $g_\mathrm{geom}$ enters;

          \item six in Campaigns 7 and 8, when the controllability screen
                $g_\mathrm{ctrl}$ enters;

          \item eight in Campaign 9, which adds the control screen at the
                operating maximum $g_\mathrm{ctrl,peak}$ and the
                cycle-length floor of Section~\ref{sec:meth-c9}.
        \end{itemize}

        The two-bank reading $g_\mathrm{ctrl,12}$ and the boron record
        $g_\mathrm{boron}$ of Campaign 9 are recorded and not
        constrained, so they do not enter the count.
\end{itemize}

The evaluation level of $F_{\Delta H}$ changes between campaigns and is
fixed in Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}, with the validation
that motivated the change in Section~\ref{sec:meth-core-objective}.

The rod count is a discrete variable. The optimiser proposes a
continuous value inside the bounds and the evaluator snaps it to the
ladder before the model is built, recording both the proposed and the
applied value in the archive. The ladder follows the interior-first,
guide-tube-ringed placement policy established for the absorber study,
under four rules.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The patterns are nested, so each contains the one below it and
        the twelve-rod heritage arrangement of Campaigns 1 to 4 is the
        base of the ladder.
  \item Every pattern is octant-symmetric.
  \item The outer two rod rows are kept absorber free.
  \item Every gadolinia rod is face-adjacent or diagonally adjacent to
        a guide tube, and no two gadolinia rods are face-adjacent to
        each other, which limits mutual shadowing.
\end{enumerate}

enrichment box to 3.0 to 10.43\,wt\%. Campaign 8 fixes two variables and searches
four (Section~\ref{sec:meth-c8-space}). The Campaign 8 reflector bound
follows from the vessel budget once the barrel and downcomer clearance
$c_\mathrm{v}$ of that section is reserved at the fixed pitch.

The numerical bound of $g_\mathrm{peak}$ is anchored in licensing
practice. The radial enthalpy-rise hot channel factor is a controlled
design limit that every pressurised water reactor documents in its
safety analysis report, and the publicly available U.S.~EPR Final
Safety Analysis Report is the representative example adopted
here~\cite{epr_fsar_ch4}. The value of 2.0 is a deliberately generous
screening bound for this exploratory single-batch study rather than the
limit of any specific plant. Feasibility against it therefore screens
candidate designs and does not claim compliance with a licensed core.

\subsection{Geometric feasibility}

The vessel-fit constraint $g_\mathrm{geom}$ uses the circumscribed
envelope of the intact core footprint.

\begin{equation}
R_\mathrm{env}(p) = \sqrt{13}\,\cdot\,17\,p
\label{eq:renv}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $R_\mathrm{env}$ is the circumscribed radius of the fuel
        region, in cm.

  \item $p$ is the pin pitch, in cm.

  \item $17$ is the number of pin cells per assembly side,
        dimensionless, so that $17p$ is the assembly pitch in cm.

  \item $\sqrt{13} \approx 3.606$ is the dimensionless distance from
        the core centre to the outermost fuel corner of the
        $6\times6$-minus-corners map, in assembly pitches. The extreme
        corner lies three assembly pitches along one axis and two along
        the other, so its distance is $\sqrt{3^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{13}$.
\end{itemize}

The sensitivity of the envelope to the pitch follows directly.

\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}R_\mathrm{env}}{\mathrm{d}p} = 61.3~\mathrm{cm\,cm^{-1}}
\label{eq:drenv}
\end{equation}

A pitch increase of one millimetre therefore consumes about six
millimetres of radial budget. Pitch and reflector thickness compete
directly for the fixed allowance of the 90\,cm vessel internal radius
of Table~\ref{tab:model-geometry}, which makes the two variables
strongly coupled rather than independent.

The enforced form of the constraint carries two further terms. The
geometry builder pads the lattice corner by a fixed allowance, and
Campaign 8 reserves a clearance for the core barrel and the downcomer.
Both must appear in the analytic test, or the test and the builder
disagree at the boundary.

\begin{equation}
g_\mathrm{geom} \;=\; R_\mathrm{env}(p) \;+\; \delta \;+\;
t_\mathrm{refl} \;+\; c_\mathrm{v} \;-\; R_\mathrm{v} \;\le\; 0
\label{eq:ggeom}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $R_\mathrm{env}(p)$ is the circumscribed radius of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:renv}, in cm.

  \item $\delta$ is the lattice-corner pad of the geometry builder,
        \SI{0.02}{cm}, held in a single constant shared by the builder
        and the constraint.

  \item $t_\mathrm{refl}$ is the reflector thickness, in cm.

  \item $c_\mathrm{v}$ is the reserved barrel and downcomer clearance,
        zero through Campaign 7 and \SI{7.08}{cm} in Campaign 8, in cm,
        as derived in Section~\ref{sec:meth-c8-space}.

  \item $R_\mathrm{v}$ is the vessel internal radius, \SI{90}{cm}.
\end{itemize}

The pad is not cosmetic. In Campaign 4 the analytic test and the
builder disagreed by exactly this amount, the optimiser located the
discrepant corner of the design space, and the first infill attempt
failed there. Since the repair the constant is defined once and
imported by both consumers.

The constraint is enforced analytically inside the optimiser rather
than during model construction. Campaign 1 showed that omitting it lets
the genetic algorithm converge on designs that cannot be built, because
the fuel region and the reflector together exceed the vessel envelope.

\subsection{Reactivity window basis}
\label{sec:meth-kbasis}

Through Campaign 5 the reactivity window acted on the assembly infinite
multiplication factor. The Route B construction of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget} makes the corresponding core eigenvalue
available at no additional cost, and the leakage gap between the two
bases is not a constant. Across the design space it varies between
about \num{4300} and \SI{8060}{pcm}, growing with the reflector
thickness and the pitch, so a fixed assembly-basis bound is
inconsistently strict in a way that depends on two of the design
variables themselves.

From Campaign 6 both bounds of the window therefore act on the core
effective multiplication factor at beginning of life. The ceiling
itself is not a constant of the work. It is the adopted screening value
through Campaign 6 and a derived quantity afterwards, so it is written
here as a symbol.

\begin{equation}
1.02 \;\le\; k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}(\mathbf{x}) \;\le\; k_{\max}
\label{eq:k-window-core}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}$ is the effective multiplication
        factor of the 32-assembly core at the beginning of life,
        dimensionless, from the same core solve that supplies the
        peaking objective.

  \item $\mathbf{x}$ is the design vector of
        Table~\ref{tab:design-space}.

  \item $k_{\max}$ is the reactivity ceiling of the campaign,
        dimensionless. It is 1.35 through Campaign 6 and takes the
        derived values of Table~\ref{tab:ctrl-bounds} from Campaign 7.
\end{itemize}

Two consequences follow. First, the re-scoring of the Campaign 5
archive in Section~\ref{sec:res-c5-kbasis} shows that the core basis at
1.35 and the assembly basis at 1.40 admit the same designs, so the
change is a basis correction with a stated numerical consequence and
not a relaxation of the design intent. Second, the lower bound becomes
physically meaningful. On the assembly basis a floor of 1.02 was
vacuous, whereas on the core basis it rejects genuinely subcritical
cores, and it does so repeatedly in Campaign 6
(Section~\ref{sec:res-c6-doe}).

\subsection{Controllability screen and the reactivity ceiling}
\label{sec:meth-ctrl}

Through Campaign 6 the reactivity ceiling proxied controllable excess
reactivity without a rod model. From Campaign 7 the proxy is replaced
by a measurement. Every evaluation solves the beginning-of-life core a
second time with the control rod assemblies of the sixteen central
assemblies inserted, which are the four regulating banks RE1 to RE4 of
the NuScale-inspired seven-bank scheme adopted in Campaign 6, with the
three shutdown banks of the outer sixteen assemblies withdrawn. The
absorber is B$_4$C.

\begin{equation}
k_\mathrm{RE}(\mathbf{x}) = k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}\big(\mathbf{x},\ \mathrm{RE1\ to\ RE4\ inserted}\big)
\label{eq:k-re}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $k_\mathrm{RE}$ is the core effective multiplication factor at
        beginning of life with the sixteen regulating-bank assemblies
        inserted and the sixteen shutdown-bank assemblies withdrawn,
        dimensionless.

  \item $\mathbf{x}$ is the design vector.
\end{itemize}

The screen demands an operating subcriticality margin.

\begin{equation}
g_\mathrm{ctrl}(\mathbf{x}) = k_\mathrm{RE}(\mathbf{x}) - (1 - \Delta\rho_\mathrm{op}) \le 0
\label{eq:g-ctrl-meth}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $g_\mathrm{ctrl}$ is the constraint value, dimensionless,
        negative when satisfied.

  \item $\Delta\rho_\mathrm{op}$ is the operating margin, \num{0.01} in
        units of $k$, that is \SI{1000}{pcm}.
\end{itemize}

The margin is a modelling choice. An insertion of the regulating banks
alone with a \SI{1000}{pcm} margin is not a licensing shutdown margin,
which additionally requires the shutdown banks, the highest-worth
assembly assumed stuck out, and the cold, xenon-free state. The screen
therefore tests whether the regulating system can hold the fresh core
subcritical at power conditions, which is the question the reactivity
ceiling was standing in for.

Campaign 8 adds a second reading with only the first two banks
inserted, RE1 in the four central assemblies and RE2 in the four
diagonal assemblies of the middle ring. It is recorded as
$g_\mathrm{ctrl,12}$ and is not a constraint, so that the front can be
split afterwards into designs controllable with two banks and designs
needing all four, without shrinking the feasible region in advance.

The notation deserves an explicit statement, because in reactor
practice the label all rods in denotes the state with every control rod
assembly of the core inserted, which is not the state screened here.
Three rodded states are distinguished in this work.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $k_\mathrm{RE}$, with the sixteen regulating-bank assemblies
        RE1 to RE4 inserted and the sixteen shutdown-bank assemblies
        withdrawn. It is the state of the constraint of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:g-ctrl-meth} and of the bank worth of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:bank-worth}.

  \item $k_\mathrm{RE12}$, with the eight assemblies of banks RE1 and
        RE2 inserted and all others withdrawn. It is recorded from
        Campaign 8 and is not constrained.

  \item The all-rods-in state, with all 32 control rod assemblies
        inserted. It is used only to derive the retrospective ceiling
        of 1.368 in Table~\ref{tab:ctrl-bounds} and never as a
        campaign constraint.
\end{enumerate}

The implementation carries a historical label that does not follow this
convention. In \texttt{boron\_worth.py} the state named \texttt{ARI}
and the archive key \texttt{k\_allre} both denote the sixteen
regulating-bank assemblies of $k_\mathrm{RE}$, so the columns $M_{16}$
and $M_{8}$ of the boron-worth table of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-boron-worth} are the margins of the
sixteen-assembly and of the eight-assembly insertion respectively, and
neither is a margin of the all-rods-in state.

The reactivity ceiling of $g_{k\max}$ can now be derived from the
measured bank worth rather than adopted.

\begin{equation}
k_{\max} = (1 - \Delta\rho_\mathrm{op}) + \min_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{S}} \rho_\mathrm{bank}(\mathbf{x})
\label{eq:k-max-derived}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $k_{\max}$ is the derived ceiling on the unrodded core
        eigenvalue, dimensionless.

  \item $\rho_\mathrm{bank}$ is the bank worth, the difference between
        the unrodded and the rodded eigenvalues, dimensionless.

  \item $\mathcal{S}$ is the sample of designs on which the worth was
        measured.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:ctrl-bounds} lists the ceilings used and derived. Its
three-dimensional values come from the axial study of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-lax}.

Two lessons of Campaign 7 govern the use of the derived ceiling. A
minimum over a small sample is not a robust estimator, and the
Campaign 7 value of 1.126 rejected fourteen designs whose own measured
worth showed them controllable (Section~\ref{sec:res-c7-screens}).
And the derived ceiling can only be a surrogate for a quantity that is
measured on every evaluation anyway. From Campaign 8 the measured
screen is the constraint that does the work, and the derived ceiling
is retained as a screening bound with its provenance stated.

The retrospective value of 1.368 also settles the status of the
ceiling used in Campaigns 1 to 6. Derived from the worth of all 32
control rod assemblies with the same margin, it lies two per cent
above the adopted 1.35, which is therefore the measured all-bank
controllability bound of this core to within that tolerance.


\subsection{Objective functions}
\label{sec:objective_functions}

\subsubsection{Cycle length}

The cycle-length objective of Equation~\eqref{eq:obj_efpd} is computed
from the end-of-cycle burnup through the general conversion of
Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd}, with the specific power of
Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}. What remains to be fixed here is the
criterion that defines the end of cycle on the model used for the
depletion, the reflective single assembly.

\begin{equation}
B_\mathrm{EOC} = \min\!\left( B_k,\, B_\mathrm{ATF} \right)
\label{eq:eoc}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
B_k = \min\left\{\, B > 0 \,:\, k_\infty(B) \le k_\mathrm{target} \,\right\}
\label{eq:eoc-k}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $B_\mathrm{EOC}$ is the end-of-cycle burnup entering
        Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd}, in MWd/kgHM.

  \item $B_k$ is the reactivity-defined end-of-cycle burnup, in
        MWd/kgHM.

  \item $B_\mathrm{ATF}$ is the accident-tolerant-fuel discharge-burnup
        limit, equal to 75\,MWd/kgHM, treated in
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-atf}.

  \item $B$ is the burnup along the depletion history, in MWd/kgHM.

  \item $k_\infty(B)$ is the infinite multiplication factor of the
        reflective assembly at burnup $B$, dimensionless.

  \item $k_\mathrm{target}$ is the leakage-corrected end-of-cycle
        target, dimensionless, defined in
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget}.
\end{itemize}

The crossing $B_k$ is obtained by interpolation between the two
depletion steps that bracket the target.

Designs still critical at the depletion horizon in the early
campaigns were right-censored and recorded at the horizon value
rather than extrapolated. The horizon was the evaluator default of
35.5\,MWd/kgHM in Campaign 1 and an explicit 100\,MWd/kgHM in
Campaign 2, and the censored fraction was 54.2\% and 59.5\%
respectively, as quantified in Section~\ref{sec:res-c1c2}. The
discharge limit entered the objective in Campaign 2,
retrospectively, through the exact post-processing transform of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-atf}. From Campaign 3 onward the depletion
terminates at $B_\mathrm{ATF}$ inside the loop, so
Equation~\eqref{eq:eoc} holds by construction and censoring is
eliminated in intent. The qualification is necessary because the
ceiling was reached at half the intended burnup in the campaigns
affected by the restart defect described below.

The Campaign 2 schedule opens with a beginning-of-life ramp at 0.5, 1,
2, 4 and \SI{6}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram}, continues in steps
of \SI{4}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram} committed in blocks of two,
and terminates adaptively, giving 10 to 28 transport states per
evaluation. Campaign 1 used the fixed 14-state default of the
evaluator. From Campaign 3 onward the schedule terminates at the
discharge limit inside the loop.

The adaptive termination is what forces the depletion to advance in
short blocks. The solver requires its complete step list before the
calculation starts, while the stopping burnup is a property of the
design being evaluated and is not known in advance. Each block is
therefore a separate depletion call that restarts from the results
file written by the previous one, and a restart requires that file to
carry both the material composition and the reaction rates.

The depletion solver of the version used here writes no reaction rates
to its results files unless explicitly instructed. A restart from a
rate-free file advances its first step with every transmutation
channel set to zero, so the fuel evolves by radioactive decay alone
while the burnup label advances by the full step. The failure is
silent, because the calculation completes and every value it returns
is of a plausible magnitude. The evaluator therefore now requests the
rates explicitly at every block and refuses to restart from a file
that carries none.

That guard postdates the campaigns reported here. Their recorded
burnup labels therefore overstate the physical burnup, the in-loop
ceiling of Equation~\eqref{eq:eoc} was reached at
\SI{43.0}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram} of physical burnup rather
than at the intended \SI{75.0}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram}, and
censoring was reintroduced despite the in-loop termination. The
mechanism, the controlled single-block against multi-block experiment
that established it, and the exact transform that recovers the true
burnup from the archived histories are given in
Section~\ref{sec:restart_defect}. Every cycle length in this work is
corrected by that transform before use.

\subsubsection{Radial peaking factor}

The peaking objective of Equation~\eqref{eq:obj_fdh} uses the radial
peaking factor of Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_definition}, formally defined
in Section~\ref{sec:bg-peaking}. That definition leaves two
methodological choices open, and both are fixed here: the numerical
estimator and the evaluation domain.

\paragraph{Numerical estimator.}
The integrals of Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_definition} are replaced by
their Monte Carlo counterparts. A pin-wise fission energy deposition
tally is accumulated over the active fuel region, so each rod
contributes one volume-integrated score.

\begin{equation}
\widehat{F}_{\Delta H}
=
\frac{\displaystyle \max_{i \in \{1,\dots,N\}} \widehat{P}_i}
     {\displaystyle \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} \widehat{P}_j}
\label{eq:fdh_estimator}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\widehat{F}_{\Delta H}$ is the Monte Carlo estimate of the
        radial peaking factor, dimensionless.

  \item $\widehat{P}_i$ is the tallied fission energy deposition rate
        of fuel rod $i$ over the full active height, in W.

  \item $N$ is the number of fuel rods in the evaluation domain,
        dimensionless.

  \item $i$ and $j$ are fuel rod indices, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

Two properties of this estimator govern the selection methodology.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The estimate carries a statistical uncertainty inherited from
        the finite number of simulated histories. Differences between
        candidate designs smaller than that uncertainty cannot be
        resolved by a single evaluation.

  \item The estimate is positively biased. The numerator is a maximum
        over $N$ noisy scores, so fluctuation alone raises the sampled
        maximum above the true maximum, and the bias grows with $N$ and
        with the tally variance.
\end{enumerate}

Both properties are quantified in Section~\ref{sec:meth-stats}, which
also develops the two-stage subset-selection procedure that separates
true optima from noise-driven apparent optima.

\paragraph{Evaluation domain.}
The tally domain of Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_estimator} determines what
the objective represents. Two levels are used in this work.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Assembly level, in which $N$ covers the 264 fuel rods of a
        single reflected assembly. This level is inexpensive and served
        as the screening objective in Campaigns 1 to 3.

  \item Core level, in which $N$ covers the fuel rods of the full
        32-assembly core at beginning of life. This level supplies both
        the objective and the peaking constraint from Campaign 4
        onward.
\end{enumerate}

The validation programme that motivated the move to core level is
presented in Section~\ref{sec:meth-core-objective}, and its
measurements in Section~\ref{sec:res-corevalidation}.

\subsubsection{Normalisation of the constraint violation}
\label{subsubsec:cv-norm}

The constraints of the problem are expressed in four different physical
units. The reactivity window acts on the multiplication factor, the enrichment
cap on a weight percentage, the peaking limit on a dimensionless power ratio,
and the vessel-fit condition on a length in centimetres. The
controllability screen added in Campaign 7 acts on a multiplication
factor and takes the scale of that window.

The constraint handling of the NSGA-II (Non-dominated Sorting Genetic
Algorithm II) implementation ranks infeasible candidates by the total
constraint violation. When the population contains no feasible member, which
is the situation throughout Campaigns 4 and 5, this ranking alone drives the
search. Summing raw violations across heterogeneous units therefore weights
the constraints by their units rather than by their engineering importance. A
violation of one centimetre in the vessel-fit condition would outweigh a
violation of several thousand pcm in the reactivity window.

From Campaign 6 onward, each constraint is normalised by its own limit before it
enters the optimiser. The normalised violation is defined below.

\begin{equation}
  V = \sum_{j=1}^{n_g} \max\!\left(0, \frac{g_j}{s_j}\right)
  \label{eq:cv-norm}
\end{equation}

The terms of Equation~\eqref{eq:cv-norm} have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $V$ is the total constraint violation used to rank infeasible
    candidates, dimensionless.
  \item $g_j$ is the raw value of constraint $j$ in its native unit, with
    $g_j \leq 0$ denoting feasibility.
  \item $s_j$ is the normalisation scale of constraint $j$, taken equal to the
    limit of that constraint, in the same unit as $g_j$.
  \item $n_g$ is the number of inequality constraints, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

The scales adopted are $s = 1.02$ for the lower reactivity bound,
$s = k_{\max}$ for the upper reactivity bound, equal to 1.35 in
Campaign 6 and to the campaign value of
Table~\ref{tab:ctrl-bounds} thereafter, $s = 19.75$ for the enrichment
cap up to Campaign 6, replaced by the peripheral cap of 12 in
Campaign 7 and 16 from Campaign 8, $s = 2.0$ for the peaking limit, $s = 90.0$~cm for the vessel-fit
condition and $s = 0.99$ for the controllability screen. Each
normalised violation is then the fraction of its own limit.

Three properties of this change are noted.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The feasible set is unchanged, because the scales are positive and the
    sign of every $g_j$ is preserved.
  \item The objectives and the hypervolume history are unchanged, because the
    normalisation acts only on the constraint matrix.
  \item Archived evaluations from earlier campaigns remain valid, because the
    physical constraint values are stored unscaled and the normalisation is
    applied when the archive is loaded.
\end{enumerate}

The active scales are written into the checkpoint metadata at launch,
and a resumed campaign refuses to start if its constraint set differs
from the stored one, so the normalisation cannot change silently
between blocks.

One caveat belongs with the choice, because the normalisation is a
numerical device and not a physical statement. Dividing each violation
by its own limit makes a one per cent exceedance of the reactivity
ceiling count exactly as much as a one per cent exceedance of the
peaking bound. Nothing in the physics of the two limits justifies that
exchange rate. A safety-informed weighting would need the margin to
failure behind each limit, and the open record does not supply it for
this core. The peaking bound of 2.0 is itself an exploratory screening
value (Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}) and the reactivity
ceiling was a derived screening bound until the measured controllability
screen replaced it in Campaign 7
(Section~\ref{sec:meth-ctrl}). The fraction-of-limit rule is therefore
adopted as the neutral choice in the absence of a defensible
alternative.

The exchange rate only matters where two constraints are active on the
same design, because a single violation orders the infeasible
population by itself. In Campaign 8 that situation does not
arise between the two constraints in question. Of the 41 infeasible
designs of Campaign 8, 35 fail the controllability screen and 6 fail
the reactivity floor. The 23 designs that also exceed the reactivity
ceiling are a strict subset of the 35, and no design violates the
peaking bound at all, the largest value of $g_\mathrm{peak}$ over the
archive being $-0.218$. The reactivity ceiling and the peaking limit are
therefore never simultaneously active in Campaign 8, and no reported
result depends on the weighting between them.

\section{Leakage-corrected reactivity target (Route B)}
\label{sec:meth-ktarget}

End of cycle on the reflective assembly is defined at the
leakage-corrected target introduced qualitatively in
Section~\ref{sec:bg-k}.

\begin{equation}
k_\mathrm{target}(p, t_\mathrm{refl})
= \frac{k_\infty^\mathrm{assembly}}{k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}}
\label{eq:ktarget}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $k_\mathrm{target}$ is the leakage-corrected end-of-cycle
        target entering Equation~\eqref{eq:eoc-k}, dimensionless.

  \item $k_\infty^\mathrm{assembly}$ is the infinite multiplication
        factor of the reflective single assembly, dimensionless.

  \item $k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}$ is the effective multiplication
        factor of the 32-assembly core built with the same pitch and
        reflector thickness, dimensionless.

  \item $p$ is the pin pitch, in cm.

  \item $t_\mathrm{refl}$ is the reflector thickness, in cm.
\end{itemize}

The ratio is pre-computed on a $3\times7$ grid over pitch and reflector
thickness and bilinearly interpolated inside the loop.

Two constructions could carry the leakage correction, and they are
mutually exclusive because using both would double-count the reflector
leakage.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Route A, a reflected-assembly sleeve model.

  \item Route B, the pre-computed target of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:ktarget}.
\end{enumerate}

Route B is adopted. The construction is validated a posteriori on the
finalists in Section~\ref{sec:res-corevalidation}.

\subsection{Axial leakage factor and the three-dimensional target}
\label{sec:meth-lax}

The Route B target of Equation~\eqref{eq:ktarget} corrects the
infinite-lattice depletion for the radial leakage of the
two-dimensional core. The transport model has no axial extent, so the
axial leakage of the finite core is absent from every cycle length of
Campaigns 1 to 7. A dedicated study measured it before Campaign 8.

The three-dimensional model extends the two-dimensional core to a
finite active height of \SI{120}{cm}, adds a \SI{15}{cm} slab of
borated water above and below the fuel as the axial reflector, and
places a vacuum boundary beyond the slabs. The radial reflector spans
the full height. The materials and the zoned enrichment map are those
of the campaign evaluator, so the only difference between the two
models is the axial extent. Each paired solve ran at \num{150000}
particles and 200 batches with 80 inactive, the raised inactive count
allowing the axial source shape to converge, in two seeds.

The axial leakage factor is the ratio of the two eigenvalues.

\begin{equation}
L_\mathrm{ax}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{2D}(\mathbf{x})}{k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{3D}(\mathbf{x})}
\label{eq:lax-def}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $L_\mathrm{ax}$ is the axial leakage factor, dimensionless.

  \item $k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{2D}$ is the eigenvalue of the
        two-dimensional core with the campaign radial reflector,
        dimensionless.

  \item $k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{3D}$ is the eigenvalue of the same core
        at finite height with the axial reflectors, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:lax-study} reports the six Campaign 6 designs studied.
They span an unrodded eigenvalue from 1.071 to 1.279 and enrichments
from 3 to \SI{14}{wt\%}, and their two-dimensional regulating-bank
worths, \num{14839} to \SI{17815}{pcm}, lie within one standard
deviation of the Campaign 7 archive median. In the rodded columns all
sixteen regulating banks are inserted, and the subcriticality is
$(1 - 1/k)$ in pcm, negative when the core is supercritical.

Three results carry into the method.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The factor is effectively a constant. It ranges from
        \num{1.0282} to \num{1.0298} over a factor of two in excess
        reactivity, a spread of \SI{160}{pcm}. One value, the mean
        \num{1.0289}, therefore serves the whole design space with a
        stated systematic of $\pm\SI{80}{pcm}$, about $\pm 13$ EFPD at
        the end-of-cycle slopes of Section~\ref{sec:res-c7-uncertainty}.
        This is the same structural property that justifies Route B
        itself, namely that a leakage factor is governed by the
        geometry, and the geometry is fixed.

  \item The rodded core is \num{2900} to \SI{3100}{pcm} more
        subcritical in three dimensions than in two. The
        two-dimensional control screen of Section~\ref{sec:meth-ctrl}
        is therefore conservative by that amount, and the direction of
        the conservatism is measured rather than argued.

  \item Design 86, the high-enrichment Campaign 6 champion, is
        supercritical with all regulating banks inserted in both
        models. The long-cycle end of the unconstrained design space
        cannot be held down by the regulating system, which is the
        physical reason for the enrichment cap and the control screen
        of Campaigns 8 and 9.
\end{enumerate}

From Campaign 8 the axial factor multiplies the target, so that the
end of cycle is defined on a finite-height core from the start.

\begin{equation}
k_\mathrm{target}^\mathrm{3D}(t_\mathrm{refl}) = k_\mathrm{target}^\mathrm{2D}(t_\mathrm{refl}) \times L_\mathrm{ax}
\label{eq:ktarget-3d}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $k_\mathrm{target}^\mathrm{3D}$ is the target that the depleting
        assembly must retain at end of cycle for the finite core to be
        critical, dimensionless.

  \item $k_\mathrm{target}^\mathrm{2D}$ is the Route B target of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:ktarget} at the fixed pitch, dimensionless.

  \item $L_\mathrm{ax}$ is the axial factor, \num{1.0289}.

  \item $t_\mathrm{refl}$ is the reflector thickness, in cm.
\end{itemize}

With the pitch fixed at \SI{1.26}{cm} the calibration table of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget} collapses to one dimension. It was
recomputed at seven reflector nodes from 2.0 to \SI{5.66}{cm}, one
assembly solve and seven core solves. The per-node statistical error
from the core solve alone is 43 to \SI{60}{pcm}, and the single
assembly solve shifts every node together by \SI{90}{pcm} without
affecting the shape. A weighted straight line describes the seven
nodes with a chi-square per degree of freedom of 0.38 and a worst
residual of 1.16 standard deviations, and the fitted values replace the
raw ones to remove a one-sigma fluctuation at \SI{3.8}{cm}.

\begin{equation}
k_\mathrm{target}^\mathrm{2D}(t_\mathrm{refl}) = 1.054528 - 0.000516\, t_\mathrm{refl}
\label{eq:ktarget-c8-fit}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $k_\mathrm{target}^\mathrm{2D}$ is the two-dimensional target at
        pitch \SI{1.26}{cm}, dimensionless.

  \item $t_\mathrm{refl}$ is the reflector thickness, in cm, over 2.0
        to 5.66.

  \item The slope is \SI{51.6}{pcm} per centimetre of reflector.
\end{itemize}

The three-dimensional target runs from 1.0839 at \SI{2.0}{cm} to
1.0820 at \SI{5.66}{cm}. Across the entire Campaign 8 design space the
target therefore moves by about \SI{200}{pcm}, roughly 0.3 MWd/kgHM or
34 EFPD, so the reflector thickness has ceased to be a reactivity
variable in this campaign and acts on the radial power shape alone.

The correction raises the target by about \SI{2900}{pcm} relative to
the two-dimensional campaigns. At the median end-of-cycle slope of
\SI{607}{pcm} per MWd/kgHM that is 4.8 MWd/kgHM, roughly 450 to 500
EFPD. Campaign 8 cycle lengths are therefore not comparable with those
of Campaigns 1 to 7 without adding that amount back, and they are the
first in this work that refer to a finite-height core.


\section{Surrogate-assisted active-learning loop}
\label{sec:meth-loop}

The campaign follows the surrogate model optimisation pattern, in which
a model trained on simulator results is optimised and the resulting
non-dominated designs are re-evaluated with the
simulator~\cite{whyte_2020}. Here the pattern runs as a loop whose four
steps repeat until the stopping rule is met.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A Latin-hypercube design of
        experiments~\cite{mckay_lhs_1979} seeds the archive, and each
        subsequent iteration adds a small infill batch evaluated with
        OpenMC.

  \item A Gaussian-process surrogate with automatic relevance
        determination~\cite{rasmussen_gp_2006} is trained on the whole
        cumulative archive.

  \item NSGA-II~\cite{deb_nsga2_2002} searches the surrogate in place
        of the expensive code.

  \item The most informative proposals form the next infill batch.
\end{enumerate}

Blocks of three iterations are separated by analysis. The empirical
stopping rule has two conditions, both required.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The hypervolume gain stays below 1\% for three consecutive
        iterations.

  \item No censored design remains on the Pareto front, so the front is
        fully resolved.
\end{enumerate}

The stopping rule is a necessary condition and not a sufficient one.
Campaign 6 demonstrates the distinction experimentally. The rule fired
after three iterations whose acquisition was defective and whose
hypervolume gain was exactly zero, and it fired again, this time
correctly, after the acquisition had been repaired and the gains had
decayed monotonically below the threshold. The two firings are
presented side by side in Section~\ref{sec:res-c6-margin}.

Two per-iteration health indicators are therefore recorded alongside
the hypervolume and inspected before the rule is trusted.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The number of candidates that pass the feasibility margin of
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-acq}, which measures whether the
        constraint surrogate is still learning.

  \item The depth of the diversity relaxation cascade, which measures
        whether the surrogate front still contains materially new
        designs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Surrogate and loop settings}
\label{sec:meth-loop-settings}

The Gaussian processes of Section~\ref{sec:bg-gp} are set up as follows:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The inputs are the design variables of the campaign,
        standardised to zero mean and unit variance so that one
        dimensionless length-scale per variable is meaningful. The
        outputs are normalised likewise.

  \item One independent Gaussian process of the form of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:kernel} is fitted per output: one for each of
        the two objectives, and a parallel set for the constraints. The
        surrogate therefore returns predicted objectives, predicted
        constraint values and a standard deviation for each.

  \item All hyperparameters, the length-scales $\ell_d$, the signal
        variance $\sigma_f^{2}$ and the noise variance $\sigma_n^{2}$,
        are refitted at every iteration by maximising the marginal
        likelihood with four restarts, on the \emph{whole} cumulative
        archive.

  \item That archive grows with every iteration and holds between 54
        and 114 designs at the end of the completed campaigns. This is
        within the range in which Kriging models remain affordable, fewer
        than 20 variables and 500 samples~\cite{forrester_book_2008}.

  \item The fitted $\ell_d$ are read as a ranking of the influence of
        each variable. The fitted $\sigma_n$ is compared against the
        measured seed-to-seed scatter of
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-fidelity} as a model-misfit diagnostic.
\end{itemize}

The loop of Section~\ref{sec:bg-al} was run with the following settings:

\begin{itemize}
  \item In Campaign 3 the design of experiments holds 36 Latin
        hypercube points, drawn with an acceptance filter on the analytic
        vessel-fit constraint of Equation~\eqref{eq:renv}, so that no
        expensive evaluation is spent on a geometry that cannot be built.

  \item Each iteration adds 6 infill evaluations, and iterations are
        grouped in blocks of three, that is, 18 evaluations per block.
        This is the granularity visible in the Block-1 and Block-2
        feasibility counts of Section~\ref{sec:res-c3front}.

  \item Campaign 3 reached its stopping rule after two blocks, for a
        total budget of $36 + 2 \times 18 = 72$ evaluations. That budget
        was discovered empirically and was not fixed in advance.

  \item The per-design seeding of Section~\ref{sec:meth-fidelity} keeps
        the evaluation noise independent across archive members, as the
        surrogate noise model assumes.

  \item Campaign 4 runs the identical loop with the objective moved to
        core level at the $\approx3\%$ cost of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:corecost}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Acquisition function}
\label{sec:meth-acq}

The infill batch is drawn from the non-dominated set of the final
NSGA-II population on the surrogate. The base criterion ranks these
candidates by their aggregated predictive uncertainty.

\begin{equation}
a_i \;=\; \sum_{m=1}^{2} \frac{\sigma_{im}}{\max_{l}\,\sigma_{lm}}
\label{eq:acq-score}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $a_i$ is the acquisition score of candidate $i$,
        dimensionless.

  \item $\sigma_{im}$ is the Gaussian-process posterior standard
        deviation of objective $m$ at candidate $i$, in the unit of
        that objective.

  \item $\max_{l}\,\sigma_{lm}$ is the largest posterior standard
        deviation of objective $m$ over the candidate set, in the same
        unit, so that each objective contributes on a common scale.
\end{itemize}

This is a pure exploration criterion in the sense of
\cite{forrester_book_2008}. Campaign 6 exposed three distinct failure
modes of the bare criterion, and each motivated one revision. The
revisions were introduced one per block, so every behavioural change is
attributable, and the measurements that motivated them are reported in
Section~\ref{sec:res-c6-defects}.

\paragraph{Ceiling-aware surrogate objective.}
The truth evaluator censors every cycle length at the depletion
ceiling, but a Gaussian process trained on the archive extrapolates
through it, and the search then concentrates on predictions that the
evaluator cannot return. The surrogate objective is therefore clipped
at the ceiling before it enters the search.

\begin{equation}
\tilde{f}_1(\mathbf{x}) \;=\;
\max\!\left( f_1(\mathbf{x}),\; -E_\mathrm{cap} \right),
\qquad
E_\mathrm{cap} \;=\; \frac{1000\, B_\mathrm{max}}{P_\mathrm{spec}}
\label{eq:efpd-clip}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\tilde{f}_1$ is the clipped first objective in minimisation
        form, in d, equal to the negative predicted cycle length.

  \item $f_1$ is the raw Gaussian-process prediction of the first
        objective, in d.

  \item $E_\mathrm{cap}$ is the depletion ceiling expressed as a cycle
        length, in d, equal to \num{10016.7} at the Campaign 6 ceiling.

  \item $B_\mathrm{max}$ is the depletion ceiling, in MWd/kgHM.

  \item $P_\mathrm{spec}$ is the specific power of
        Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}, in W/gHM.
\end{itemize}

On the resulting plateau the first objective is constant, so
non-dominated sorting ranks the plateau by peaking alone, which is
exactly how the constrained-domination principle ranks censored truth
designs~\cite{deb_nsga2_2002}. The cap
is derived at launch from the declared ceiling and the model specific
power, and is recorded in the checkpoint metadata.

\paragraph{Batch diversity.}
Ranking a smooth uncertainty field returns neighbours, because the $k$
highest values of a continuous function are adjacent. A minimum
separation is therefore enforced greedily down the ranking, between
each pick and the archive and between the picks themselves.

\begin{equation}
d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}) \;=\;
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_x}}
\left\lVert \frac{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}}
{\mathbf{x}_u-\mathbf{x}_l} \right\rVert_2
\;\ge\; \delta_\mathrm{min}
\label{eq:batch-div}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$ is the normalised separation of two
        designs, dimensionless.

  \item $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are two design vectors, in the
        native units of Table~\ref{tab:design-space}.

  \item $\mathbf{x}_l$ and $\mathbf{x}_u$ are the lower and upper
        bounds of the design box, in the same units, applied
        componentwise.

  \item $n_x$ is the number of design variables, dimensionless, so
        that $\delta_\mathrm{min}$ reads as a mean per-variable
        fraction of range.

  \item $\delta_\mathrm{min}$ is the minimum separation,
        dimensionless. Campaign 6 used 0.10 in block 3 and block 4 and
        0.14 in block 5. The value 0.14 is the established setting,
        and Campaign 8 restores it explicitly after two Campaign 7
        blocks ran at the launcher default of 0.05 and collapsed
        (Section~\ref{sec:res-c7-blocks}).
\end{itemize}

One limitation of the separation is recorded. It is a mean over all
design variables, so two variables can supply the whole distance while
the others repeat. The two collapsed Campaign 7 blocks satisfied the
rule as coded, at separations of 0.059 to 0.089, in exactly that way.
A per-variable minimum spread is the natural refinement and is left as
future work.

If the candidate set cannot supply the full batch at
$\delta_\mathrm{min}$, the threshold is halved and the scan repeats,
so a small front still fills the batch with the best diversity it can
offer. Only when the candidates are exhausted entirely is the batch
completed with space-filling random designs. The depth of this
relaxation cascade is the second health indicator of the stopping
rule.

\paragraph{Feasibility margin.}
The bare criterion never consults the constraint surrogate, and the
Gaussian-process mean is optimistic exactly where the archive is thin.
Every candidate is therefore scored on the constraint surrogate with an
uncertainty margin before the uncertainty ranking is applied.

\begin{equation}
s_i \;=\; \max_{j \,\in\, \mathcal{G}_\mathrm{GP}}
\left( \mu_{ij} + \kappa\,\sigma_{ij} \right)
\label{eq:feas-margin}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $s_i$ is the margin score of candidate $i$, dimensionless in
        the normalised constraint units of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:cv-norm}, margin-feasible when
        non-positive.

  \item $\mathcal{G}_\mathrm{GP}$ is the set of surrogate-predicted
        constraints. Constraints computed analytically are excluded,
        because the search population satisfies them exactly and their
        surrogate uncertainty is meaningless.

  \item $\mu_{ij}$ is the Gaussian-process mean of constraint $j$ at
        candidate $i$, dimensionless.

  \item $\sigma_{ij}$ is the corresponding posterior standard
        deviation, dimensionless.

  \item $\kappa$ is the margin multiplier, dimensionless, set to 1.5
        from Campaign 6, subject to the same launcher regression and
        restoration in Campaigns 7 and 8 as the separation above.
\end{itemize}

Margin-feasible candidates rank first, ordered by
Equation~\eqref{eq:acq-score} as before. The remaining candidates are
not discarded. They follow, ordered by ascending $s_i$, so a front with
too few margin-feasible members still fills the batch with the least
risky picks and the batch never degenerates. Where the surrogate is
confident the margin is small, and where it extrapolates the margin is
large, which is the discrimination the bare criterion lacked. The
construction is the deterministic counterpart of the probability of
feasibility weighting of constrained surrogate
optimisation~\cite{forrester_book_2008}, and it applies the rule of
safe Bayesian optimisation, in which a point is admitted only where the
confidence bound of every constraint surrogate is
satisfied~\cite{sui_safeopt_2015}. Setting $\kappa = 0$ restores the
bare criterion exactly.

surrogate and the study measures the search alone.

Two observations fix the choice.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The seed-to-seed standard deviation at the historical setting

  \item The most expensive setting costs \SI{8.2}{\second} against

\section{Validation of the optimisation framework}
\label{sec:validation-framework}

Two questions are addressed before the Campaign 8 results are
interpreted. The first is whether the surrogate that drove the
acquisition predicts the transport results well enough, and with
uncertainties that are calibrated. The second is whether the
surrogate-assisted search recovers the Pareto front that an exhaustive
enumeration finds, and at what fraction of the enumeration cost.

The first question is answered on the archive itself, at no additional
transport cost. The second requires new transport solves and is
therefore restricted to a two-variable slice of the design space, where
enumeration is affordable.

\subsection{Cross-validation of the surrogate on the archive}
\label{sec:validation-surrogate}

The surrogate under test is the one used at every iteration of the
active-learning loop of Section~\ref{sec:meth-loop}. One anisotropic
Gaussian Process (GP) with a Mat\'ern kernel of smoothness $5/2$ and a
white-noise term is fitted to each output column, after standardisation
of the design variables and of the output. The objective matrix holds
the negated cycle length and the radial enthalpy-rise hot channel
factor $F_{\Delta H}$. The constraint matrix holds every constraint
divided by its own limit. Both matrices are fitted over all archived
designs, feasible and infeasible alike, exactly as in the loop.

Two protocols are applied. In leave-one-out cross-validation, each
archived design is held out in turn, the surrogate is refitted on the
remaining designs, and the held-out design is predicted. This measures
the surrogate at the end of the campaign. In walk-forward validation,
for every infill block the surrogate is fitted on every evaluation that
preceded the block and the designs of the block are predicted. This
measures the surrogate at decision time, on the very designs it chose.

For each output $y$ with held-out predictions $\mu_i$ and predictive
standard deviations $\sigma_i$, the following quantities are reported.

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(y_i - \mu_i\right)^2}
  \label{eq:val-rmse}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
  R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(y_i - \mu_i\right)^2}
                 {\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(y_i - \bar{y}\right)^2}
  \label{eq:val-r2}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
  s_z = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(z_i - \bar{z}\right)^2},
  \qquad z_i = \frac{y_i - \mu_i}{\sigma_i}
  \label{eq:val-sz}
\end{equation}

where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $N$ is the number of held-out predictions, dimensionless.
  \item $y_i$ is the true value of the output for design $i$, in the
        unit of the output.
  \item $\mu_i$ and $\sigma_i$ are the predictive mean and standard
        deviation of the surrogate for design $i$, in the unit of the
        output.
  \item $\bar{y}$ and $\bar{z}$ are the sample means of $y_i$ and of
        $z_i$.
  \item $\mathrm{RMSE}$ is the root mean square error, in the unit of
        the output.
  \item $R^2$ is the coefficient of determination, dimensionless.
  \item $s_z$ is the standard deviation of the standardised residual,
        dimensionless, equal to 1 for a calibrated predictor, above 1
        for an over-confident one and below 1 for an under-confident
        one.
\end{itemize}

The coverage of the one-sigma and two-sigma predictive intervals, with
nominal values 0.683 and 0.954 for Gaussian errors, and the Spearman rank
correlation between predicted and true values complete the set. The rank
correlation matters because the acquisition ranks candidates rather than
reading their absolute values. Constraint columns are converted back to
the eigenvalues they encode, so that errors can be read in reactivity
units.

The feasibility verdict of the surrogate is also scored. Two rules are
compared against the true verdict of the transport solves. The mean rule
declares a design feasible when every predicted constraint mean is at or
below zero. The margin rule, used by the acquisition, requires every
predicted constraint mean plus $\kappa$ predictive standard deviations to
be at or below zero, with $\kappa$ the campaign value of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-acq}.

\subsection{Stability of the archived front}
\label{sec:validation-front}

The feasible non-dominated set is recomputed after every evaluation in
the order the campaign ran them, together with its hypervolume at the
frozen reference point of the campaign. The evaluation at which each
final front member entered the set, and every design that was displaced
from the set later, are recorded. A front whose members all entered in
the first blocks and stayed is converged. A front that gains a member in
its last block is not.

The exclusive hypervolume contribution of each member, defined as the
hypervolume lost when that member alone is removed, identifies the
members that carry the front. The hypervolume expected after the removal
of $k$ evaluations chosen at random from the archive is computed
exactly, since the removal of designs cannot create new non-dominated
points and the outcome depends only on which front members survive.

Finally, the objectives of every feasible design are perturbed by
Gaussian noise representing the Monte Carlo uncertainty of the transport
solves, the non-dominated set is recomputed, and the probability that
each design belongs to it is estimated over many replicates. The cycle
length noise of a design follows from the eigenvalue uncertainty through
the mean reactivity slope of that design.

\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\mathrm{cycle}} = \sqrt{2}\,\frac{\sigma_k}{\left(k_{\mathrm{BOL}} - k_{\mathrm{target}}\right)/T}
  \label{eq:val-sigma-cycle}
\end{equation}

where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\sigma_{\mathrm{cycle}}$ is the one-sigma noise on the cycle
        length, in effective full-power days.
  \item $\sigma_k$ is the one-sigma Monte Carlo uncertainty of one
        depletion eigenvalue solve, dimensionless.
  \item $k_{\mathrm{BOL}}$ is the beginning-of-life multiplication factor
        of the depleted assembly model, dimensionless.
  \item $k_{\mathrm{target}}$ is the leakage-corrected end-of-cycle
        target of the design defined in Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget},
        dimensionless.
  \item $T$ is the archived cycle length of the design, in effective
        full-power days.
  \item The factor $\sqrt{2}$ accounts for the interpolation of the
        end-of-cycle crossing between two independently noisy
        eigenvalues.
\end{itemize}

Two front members whose membership probabilities are well below one are
not resolved by the transport statistics and should be reported as
equivalent.

\subsection{Search against exhaustive enumeration on a slice}
\label{sec:validation-grid}

The reflector thickness and the gadolinia rod count are frozen at the
values of the central Campaign 8 candidate, so that the enrichment and
the gadolinia weight fraction remain as live variables within the design
space of Section~\ref{sec:meth-c8-space}. On this slice, the truth
evaluator is run twice with identical limits, transport fidelity of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-fidelity}, depletion schedule and
$k_{\mathrm{target}}$ table.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Exhaustive enumeration of a regular grid of 7 enrichment values
        between 3.0 and 7.0\,wt\% and 5 gadolinia weight fractions
        between 0 and 8\,wt\%, 35 evaluations in total. The feasible
        non-dominated set of the grid is the reference front.
  \item The surrogate-assisted search with the campaign settings on the
        same box, with a design of experiments of 8 designs followed by
        two infill iterations of 6, 20 evaluations in total. Its feasible
        non-dominated set is the recovered front.
\end{enumerate}

The grid box is deliberately wider than the feasible band of the
archive, so that the enumerated front is bounded by its own constraint
boundaries on every side. Both objectives are normalised by their range
over the union of the two fronts before any distance is computed, so
that the cycle length in days and the dimensionless peaking factor
weigh equally.

The following indicators are reported.

\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{HV}} = \frac{\mathrm{HV}\!\left(P_{\mathrm{search}}, \mathbf{r}\right)}
                         {\mathrm{HV}\!\left(P_{\mathrm{grid}}, \mathbf{r}\right)}
  \label{eq:val-hvratio}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{IGD} = \frac{1}{\left|P_{\mathrm{grid}}\right|}
                 \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in P_{\mathrm{grid}}}
                 \min_{\mathbf{p}\in P_{\mathrm{search}}}
                 \left\lVert \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{q} \right\rVert_2
  \label{eq:val-igd}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
  I_{\varepsilon+}\!\left(P_{\mathrm{search}}, P_{\mathrm{grid}}\right)
  = \max_{\mathbf{q}\in P_{\mathrm{grid}}}
    \min_{\mathbf{p}\in P_{\mathrm{search}}}
    \max_{j\in\{1,2\}} \left(p_j - q_j\right)
  \label{eq:val-eps}
\end{equation}

where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $P_{\mathrm{grid}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{search}}$ are the enumerated
        and the recovered fronts, as sets of points in the normalised
        objective space, dimensionless.
  \item $\mathbf{r}$ is the shared hypervolume reference point, the nadir
        of the union of the two fronts plus ten per cent of the range,
        dimensionless.
  \item $\mathrm{HV}$ is the hypervolume dominated by a front with
        respect to $\mathbf{r}$, dimensionless.
  \item $r_{\mathrm{HV}}$ is the hypervolume ratio, dimensionless. A
        value above one is possible, because the search is continuous
        and can place designs between grid nodes.
  \item $\mathrm{IGD}$ is the inverted generational distance, the mean
        distance from each enumerated-front point to the nearest
        recovered-front point, dimensionless. A value of zero means that
        every enumerated-front point is reproduced.
  \item $I_{\varepsilon+}$ is the additive epsilon indicator, the
        smallest shift that makes the recovered front weakly dominate
        every enumerated-front point, dimensionless. It is also reported
        in effective full-power days and in units of $F_{\Delta H}$ by
        multiplication with the respective ranges.
  \item $p_j$ and $q_j$ are the $j$-th normalised objectives of the
        points $\mathbf{p}$ and $\mathbf{q}$.
\end{itemize}

The generational distance in the reverse direction, the fraction of
enumerated-front points strictly dominated or dominated within a
tolerance by the recovered front, and the provenance of the members of
the union of the two fronts complete the comparison. The surrogate
fitted on the search archive is finally evaluated at all 35 grid nodes,
none of which it has seen, with the metrics of
Section~\ref{sec:validation-surrogate}.

Both fronts are measured at the campaign transport fidelity and carry
the same Monte Carlo noise. Differences smaller than the noise
propagated by Equation~\eqref{eq:val-sigma-cycle} and by the
seed-to-seed spread of $F_{\Delta H}$ of Section~\ref{sec:meth-stats}
are not resolved and are reported as such.

\section{ATF discharge-burnup limit}
\label{sec:meth-atf}

The discharge limit adopted is $B_\mathrm{ATF} = 75$\,MWd/kgHM. It is a
screening value of this work, placed between two limits reported in the
literature:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Uranium dioxide fuel in zirconium cladding carries an
        established exposure limit of \SI{62}{MWd/kgHM} rod average and
        \SI{55}{MWd/kgHM} assembly average~\cite{song_sanchez_2026}. The
        assembly-average value is the one comparable with
        $B_\mathrm{ATF}$, because the depletion of this work is computed
        on the assembly and its burnup is referred to the heavy-metal
        mass of the whole assembly. The limit follows from cladding
        oxidation, and accident-tolerant cladding is the route to
        exceeding it~\cite{alam_2019_hotchannel}.

  \item Studies of advanced fuel for long-cycle marine cores adopt
        discharge limits of up to
        \SI{100}{MWd/kgHM}~\cite{alam_partI_2019}.
\end{itemize}

The value used here is therefore compatible with an accident-tolerant
fuel that exceeds the present limit, without assuming the performance of
the most advanced concepts. It is not taken from a qualification
programme.

Cycle length and burnup are linearly related through the constant
specific power, Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd}, with $P_\mathrm{spec}$ given
in Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}. The burnup limit therefore maps
one-to-one onto a cycle-length cap.

Campaign 2 imposed the limit retrospectively, as an exact
post-processing transform of the completed campaign. Campaigns 3 and 4
terminate depletion at $B_\mathrm{ATF}$ inside the loop, so censoring
is eliminated by construction in the labelled burnup. It is not
eliminated in the physical burnup for the campaigns affected by the
restart defect of Section~\ref{sec:restart_defect}, whose effective
ceiling was half the intended one. Hypervolume histories reset whenever
the objective definition changes.

Campaign 6 raises the depletion ceiling from 75 to
\SI{100}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram}. The two numbers play different
roles and the distinction is maintained throughout.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $B_\mathrm{ATF} = \SI{75}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram}$
        remains the discharge-qualification screen, applied at
        candidate selection in
        Section~\ref{sec:res-c6-bscreen}.

  \item $B_\mathrm{max} = \SI{100}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram}$ is a
        numerical depletion horizon whose purpose is to keep the
        Pareto front resolvable. At a ceiling of 75 a substantial part
        of the interesting design space terminates at the ceiling
        rather than at the reactivity target, the censored designs
        distort the objective surrogate, and the front cannot satisfy
        the second condition of the stopping rule.
\end{enumerate}

The surrogate is coupled to the ceiling through the clip of
Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd-clip}, so the search cannot spend evaluations
on predictions beyond the horizon. Under this arrangement exactly one
of the 114 Campaign 6 evaluations censored at the ceiling, and that
design is infeasible on the reactivity window in any case.

The internal consistency of the ceiling constant is checked against
the archives. The censoring value is \num{10016.7} effective full
power days at \SI{100}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram} in Campaigns 2
and 6 and \num{7512.5} at \SI{75}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram} in
Campaigns 3 to 5, and the two are in the exact ratio of the ceilings,
as required by the fixed heavy-metal inventory.

\section{Statistical treatment of the peaking estimator}
\label{sec:meth-stats}

$F_{\Delta H}$ is the maximum over $N$ noisy mesh cells, with $N = 264$
at assembly level. It is therefore an extreme order statistic, noisy
\emph{and} biased upward, as anticipated in Section~\ref{sec:bg-mc}.
The bias is largest for flat power distributions, which are exactly the
designs a peaking-minimising optimiser prefers.

Final selection therefore uses a two-stage subset selection over a
\emph{noise-margin} candidate set. The candidate set contains every
feasible design within $k\,\sigma_\mathrm{seed}$ of the observed
minimum, where $\sigma_\mathrm{seed}$ is the seed-to-seed standard
deviation of the estimator and $k = 4$--$6$ is the margin multiplier.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Gupta-style screening~\cite{gupta_1965} retains every candidate
        whose confidence interval overlaps the incumbent's.

  \item Welch tests with Bonferroni correction, at confirmation
        fidelity, resolve the retained candidates pairwise.
\end{enumerate}

Values adjusted across fidelities carry the measured inter-fidelity
shift with its spread propagated. They are flagged as adjusted wherever
reported.

\section{Proxy validation and the core-level objective (Campaign 4)}
\label{sec:meth-core-objective}

Optimising a reduced-order proxy requires validating the proxy's
\emph{rank fidelity} to the true objective, not only its statistical
convergence. Three instruments implement this.

\paragraph{Finalist validation.}
Each Pareto finalist is rebuilt as the full 32-assembly core and solved
at BOL with a pin-resolved mesh over the whole footprint,
seed-replicated. The ratio
$F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}/F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}$ is the proxy
gap. The same run yields $k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}$, closing
Route~B on the finalists at no additional cost.

\paragraph{Archive rescoring.}
Every feasible archive design is rescored at core level in two stages,
a 3-seed screen followed by an 8-seed resolution of the noise-margin
top. This gives the Spearman rank correlation between proxy and truth
and a direct answer to whether the proxy discarded superior designs.

\paragraph{Bridge model.}
The multiplicative discrepancy is fitted in the Kennedy--O'Hagan
spirit~\cite{kennedy_ohagan_2000,forrester_book_2008}.

\begin{equation}
\frac{F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}}{F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}}
\approx a + b\,t_\mathrm{refl} + c\,p
\label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}$ and $F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}$
        are the peaking factors of the same design at core and assembly
        level, dimensionless.

  \item $a$ is the fitted intercept, dimensionless.

  \item $b$ is the fitted coefficient of the reflector thickness, in
        cm$^{-1}$.

  \item $c$ is the fitted coefficient of the pin pitch, in cm$^{-1}$.

  \item $t_\mathrm{refl}$ is the reflector thickness, in cm.

  \item $p$ is the pin pitch, in cm.
\end{itemize}

The purpose of the fit is to quantify how much of the gap a
low-dimensional correction could carry. The fitted values on the
Campaign-3 archive are given in Equation~\eqref{eq:bridge-fit}.

The Campaign-4 decision follows from cost. The core BOL solve costs
$\sim$104\,s against a $\sim$110\,min evaluation.

\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta t}{t} \approx \frac{113 - 110}{110} \approx 3\%
\label{eq:corecost}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Delta t$ is the measured wall-time increment of one
        evaluation when the core BOL solve is added, in min.

  \item $t$ is the measured wall time of one evaluation without the
        core solve, in min.

  \item $113$ and $110$ are the measured wall times with and without
        the core solve, in min.
\end{itemize}

At this cost the true quantity is measured \emph{inside the loop}
rather than corrected. $F_{\Delta H}$ and $g_\mathrm{peak}$ are
evaluated on the core, the assembly value is retained as a diagnostic,
and EFPD keeps the validated assembly plus Route-B evaluation. This is
the deletion of the proxy where deletion is affordable. On simplicity
grounds it was preferred over trust-region model
management~\cite{alexandrov_2001} and over multi-fidelity Bayesian
optimisation, which draws on cheap and expensive information sources
within one model~\cite{kandasamy_boca_2017,poloczek_miso_2017}.

Both peaking factors are archived on every evaluation from Campaign~4
onward, the core value under the objective and the assembly value as a
diagnostic, so the ratio of Equation~\eqref{eq:bridge} can be
recomputed on any later archive without further transport.
Section~\ref{sec:res-proxy-gap-zoned} recomputes it on the zoned
archives of Campaigns~6 to~8.

One reporting defect of the run logs is recorded here, although it
affects no result. From Campaign~4 to Campaign~9, until the evaluator
was corrected after the last of them to print both values,
the per-case console line printed the assembly peaking factor under the
label of the core one. The stored quantities were always correct, the
objective and the constraint always used the core value, and no number,
figure or table of this work is taken from the console line. A run log
of these campaigns therefore shows a peaking factor lower than the
tables of Chapter~\ref{ch:results} by the ratio of
Equation~\eqref{eq:bridge}: about 1.31 on the zoned archives of
Campaigns~6 to~9, and the larger, geometry-dependent value of
Equation~\eqref{eq:bridge-fit} on the uniform loadings of Campaigns~4
and~5.

\section{Evaluator-level radial zoning and the enrichment search box}
\label{sec:meth-zoning}

From Campaign 6 the beginning-of-life core map is zoned radially inside
the evaluator, so the peaking objective and the peaking constraint act
on the zoned core rather than on a uniform loading.

The 32-assembly map is partitioned into three concentric rings by the
Euclidean distance of each assembly position from the centre of the
map, measured in lattice units.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Ring C, the centre, 4 assemblies at a distance below 1.0.
  \item Ring M, the middle, 12 assemblies at a distance from 1.0 to
        2.3.
  \item Ring P, the periphery, 16 assemblies at a distance of 2.3 or
        above.
\end{enumerate}

The enrichment of each ring is the corresponding design enrichment
scaled by a fixed ring multiplier.

\begin{equation}
e_r \;=\; m_r \, e, \qquad r \in \{C, M, P\}
\label{eq:zoning}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $e_r$ is the as-loaded enrichment of ring $r$, in
        wt\%~$^{235}$U.

  \item $e$ is the design enrichment assigned to that ring, in
        wt\%~$^{235}$U.

  \item $m_r$ is the multiplier of ring $r$, dimensionless, equal to
        $m_C = 0.720$ for the centre, $m_M = 0.8933$ for the middle
        ring and $m_P = 1.150$ for the periphery.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{How the design enrichments enter the rings}
\label{sec:meth-zoning-map}

The zoning does not assign one design enrichment to one ring. Every
assembly of the core keeps the two-zone intra-assembly structure of
Table~\ref{tab:model-geometry}, namely the centred $9\times9$ block of
72 rods at $e_\mathrm{in}$ and the surrounding 192 rods at
$e_\mathrm{out}$. What the ring multiplier does is scale \emph{both}
intra-assembly enrichments of every assembly in that ring by the same
factor.

\begin{equation}
e_\mathrm{in}^{\,r} = m_r \, e_\mathrm{in},
\qquad
e_\mathrm{out}^{\,r} = m_r \, e_\mathrm{out}
\label{eq:zoning-pair}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $e_\mathrm{in}^{\,r}$ and $e_\mathrm{out}^{\,r}$ are the
        as-loaded inner-zone and outer-zone enrichments of an assembly
        in ring $r$, in wt\%~$^{235}$U.

  \item $e_\mathrm{in}$ and $e_\mathrm{out}$ are the two enrichment
        design variables, in wt\%~$^{235}$U.

  \item $m_r$ is the multiplier of ring $r$, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

Three consequences follow, and all three are properties of the
implementation rather than modelling choices made afterwards.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Zoning is an enrichment redistribution only. Pitch, reflector
        thickness, gadolinia weight fraction and gadolinia rod count
        are copied to every ring unchanged, so the three ring
        assemblies are built by the same builders with the same pin
        layout as the base assembly.

  \item The gadolinia rod enrichment follows automatically. It is
        derived from the enrichment of its own zone through the
        existing derate of the material builder, floored at
        \SI{0.2}{wt\%}, so no material logic is duplicated in the
        zoning path.

  \item The zoning is applied on core solves only. The beginning-of-life
        core solve of Section~\ref{sec:meth-core-objective} and the two
        rodded solves of Section~\ref{sec:meth-ctrl} are built from the
        zoned map, while the depletion of Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}
        runs on the unzoned base assembly. This is admissible because
        of the balance imposed in Equation~\eqref{eq:zoning-balance}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Derivation of the middle multiplier}
\label{sec:meth-zoning-balance}

Only $m_C$ and $m_P$ are free. The middle multiplier is solved at every
call from the fissile balance, so that the assembly-count-weighted mean
multiplier of the core is exactly one.

\begin{equation}
n_C\, m_C + n_M\, m_M + n_P\, m_P \;=\; n_C + n_M + n_P
\label{eq:zoning-balance}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $n_C$, $n_M$ and $n_P$ are the assembly counts of the three
        rings, equal to 4, 12 and 16, dimensionless.

  \item $m_C$, $m_M$ and $m_P$ are the ring multipliers,
        dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

With $m_C = 0.720$ and $m_P = 1.150$ the balance returns
$m_M = 0.8933$. The consequence is the one that matters for the
evaluator. The zoned core carries the same total fissile inventory as
the uniform core of the same design vector, so the base assembly still
represents the core average and the unzoned depletion path remains the
correct cycle-length proxy for the zoned core.

\subsection{The enrichment search box}
\label{sec:meth-zoning-box}

Because the periphery carries the largest multiplier, the
low-enriched-uranium cap binds through the as-loaded peripheral ring
and not through the design variable directly. From Campaign 6 the
enrichment constraint is therefore written on the zoned map.

\begin{equation}
g_\mathrm{enr} \;=\; \max_r \left( m_r\, e \right) - 19.75 \;\le\; 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
e \;\le\; \frac{19.75}{m_P} \;=\; 17.174
\label{eq:leu-box}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $g_\mathrm{enr}$ is the enrichment-cap constraint of
        Table~\ref{tab:constraints}, in wt\%~$^{235}$U, redefined on
        the zoned map from Campaign 6.

  \item $19.75$ is the low-enriched-uranium screening cap, in
        wt\%~$^{235}$U.

  \item $m_P$ is the peripheral multiplier, dimensionless, equal to
        1.150.

  \item $17.174$ is the resulting upper bound of both enrichment
        design variables in Campaign 6, in wt\%~$^{235}$U.
\end{itemize}

The box bound and the constraint are consistent by construction, so no
design of experiments point can violate the cap after zoning. This
removes the wasted samples observed under the earlier formulation, and
it is also what bounds $m_P$ from above.

The consequence is the one design decision of this section that is not
a measurement. The study confirmed $m_P = 1.250$ in Stage 3, and
Campaign 6 adopts 1.150 instead. Raising the peripheral multiplier
flattens the power further, as Table~\ref{tab:zoning-confirm} shows,
but it shrinks the enrichment search box in the same proportion,
from \SI{17.174}{wt\%} at 1.150 to \SI{15.800}{wt\%} at 1.250. Stage T
had already shown that 10 of the 60 Campaign 5 designs violate the
screen once zoned. Campaign 6 therefore trades a peaking improvement
of about 0.06 to 0.09 for the wider box, and the trade is recorded here
so that a later campaign can reverse it if the box ceases to bind.

Campaign 7 lowers the as-loaded peripheral cap to \SI{12}{wt\%}, so
the same construction gives an upper bound of \SI{10.43}{wt\%}, and it
raises the lower bound to \SI{3.0}{wt\%}. Campaign 8 collapses the two
enrichment variables to one and raises the peripheral cap to
\SI{16}{wt\%}, which gives an upper bound of \SI{13.913}{wt\%}
(Section~\ref{sec:meth-c8-space}).

The centre-suppressed, periphery-enhanced pattern flattens the radial
power distribution, and it is the physically real driver of the
peaking improvement observed in Campaign 6, since neither of the two
bookkeeping changes of that campaign acts on a beginning-of-life
quantity. The attribution argument is completed in
Section~\ref{sec:res-c6-compare}.
\section{The Campaign 8 design space}
\label{sec:meth-c8-space}

Campaign 8 fixes what the earlier campaigns established and searches
only what remains open. Campaign 9 keeps this design space and only
returns the lower enrichment bound to \SI{2.0}{wt\%}. Four decisions
define it.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The pin pitch is fixed at \SI{1.26}{cm}, the Westinghouse
        $17\times17$ value that the reference fuel design of
        Section~\ref{sec:design-basis} already adopts. Two reasons
        compel it. The guide-tube outer radius of \SI{0.6121}{cm}
        exceeds the half-pitch below \SI{1.2242}{cm}, and an OpenMC
        lattice clips a universe at its cell boundary silently, so
        every earlier design below that pitch carried guide tubes with
        up to \SI{27}{\%} of their wall replaced by moderator. And the
        pitch was the strongest single lever on the regulating-bank
        worth (Section~\ref{sec:res-c7-worth}), so fixing it removes a
        source of variability that the control screen would otherwise
        have to absorb.

  \item The two assembly enrichment zones collapse to one variable.
        The core-level enrichment variation comes entirely from the
        C, M, P ring multipliers of Section~\ref{sec:meth-zoning},
        \num{0.720}, \num{0.893} and \num{1.150}, and the cap on the
        as-built peripheral ring is raised to \SI{16}{wt\%}. The upper
        bound of the variable follows from
        Equation~\eqref{eq:leu-box} with this cap,
        $16/1.150 = \SI{13.913}{wt\%}$, so no proposed design can
        violate $g_\mathrm{enr}$. The lower bound is \SI{3.0}{wt\%} in
        Campaign~8 and \SI{2.0}{wt\%} in Campaign~9.

  \item The vessel-fit constraint reserves a clearance for a core
        barrel and a downcomer. The barrel is the \SI{5.08}{cm}
        stainless steel wall of the NuScale-like benchmark and the
        downcomer is \SI{2.0}{cm}, chosen as a minimum coolant return
        path, with a sensitivity to \SI{3.7}{cm} run on the candidate
        designs only. The clearance enters the geometric constraint
        and not the transport model, whose outer boundary remains a
        vacuum at the reflector surface. The barrel and the downcomer
        are represented physically in the three-dimensional
        confirmation of the candidates.

  \item The reflector thickness is re-bounded to the budget that
        remains.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{equation}
t_\mathrm{refl}^{\max} = R_\mathrm{v} - c_\mathrm{v} - R_\mathrm{env}(p) - \delta
\label{eq:refl-budget-c8}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $t_\mathrm{refl}^{\max}$ is the largest reflector thickness that
        fits, \SI{5.669}{cm}, bounded in the design space at
        \SI{5.66}{cm} so that the box edge is strictly feasible.

  \item $R_\mathrm{v}$ is the vessel internal radius, \SI{90}{cm}.

  \item $c_\mathrm{v}$ is the reserved clearance, \SI{7.08}{cm}.

  \item $R_\mathrm{env}(p)$ is the circumscribed radius of the
        32-assembly fuel envelope at pitch $p$, \SI{77.231}{cm} at
        \SI{1.26}{cm}.

  \item $\delta$ is the lattice pad, \SI{0.02}{cm}.
\end{itemize}

The consequence is stated rather than discovered. Five of the six
designs of the Campaign 7 control-screen front, and 14 of its 60
designs, are unbuildable at the fixed pitch, because the low-peaking
end of that front lives at thick reflectors that fit only at narrow
pitch. Campaign 8 therefore trades the lowest peaking values of
Campaign 7 for a geometry that is consistent, and the peaking floor is
expected to rise to about 1.60.

Two further settings close the specification. The acquisition runs
with the minimum separation of 0.14 and the feasibility margin of 1.5
standard deviations established in Campaign 6, stated explicitly on
the command line after the Campaign 7 regression. And the design of
experiments has 36 points for four variables, nine per variable,
followed by four infill blocks of six, 60 evaluations in all.

\section{The Campaign 9 formulation}
\label{sec:meth-c9}

Campaigns 1 to 8 maximise the cycle length. Campaign 8 showed that every
design on its front exceeds the reported refuelling interval by a wide
margin, and that the excess reactivity bought for the longer cycle
drives the regulating banks deep into the core
(Sections~\ref{sec:res-c8-mission} and~\ref{sec:res-c8-split}).
Campaign 9 keeps the design space and the evaluator of Campaign 8 and
changes the formulation. The cycle length becomes a constraint at the
required value, and the second objective becomes the soluble boron
concentration that the core needs at the most reactive state of its
cycle.

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf{x}}\ &\big(F_{\Delta H}(\mathbf{x}),\ c_\mathrm{max}(\mathbf{x})\big) \\
\text{subject to}\ &\mathrm{EFPD}(\mathbf{x}) \ge 1826,\quad
F_{\Delta H}(\mathbf{x}) \le 1.65,\quad g_j(\mathbf{x}) \le 0
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:c9-problem}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $F_{\Delta H}$ is the radial peaking factor of the zoned core
        with all rods out at beginning of life, the objective of
        Campaign 8, dimensionless.

  \item $c_\mathrm{max}$ is the critical boron concentration at the
        operating maximum of the reactivity history, in ppm, defined in
        Equation~\eqref{eq:c9-cmax}.

  \item $\mathrm{EFPD}$ is the cycle length of
        Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}, in days. The floor of
        1826 days is five years at a capacity factor of one, the upper
        end of the interval of Equation~\eqref{eq:mission-efpd}. The
        choice is deliberately conservative, because a core that lasts
        five years at full power also lasts five calendar years at any
        lower capacity factor of the prototype.

  \item $1.65$ is the peaking screen, tightened from the exploratory
        value of 2.0 in Table~\ref{tab:constraints} to the Technical
        Specification limit of 1.65 of a licensed pressurised water
        reactor~\cite{robinson_gd10}.

  \item $g_j$ are the constraints $g_{k\min}$, $g_{k\max}$,
        $g_\mathrm{enr}$ and $g_\mathrm{geom}$ of
        Table~\ref{tab:constraints} with their Campaign 8 values, the
        control screen $g_\mathrm{ctrl}$ at beginning of life, and the
        same screen evaluated at the operating maximum,
        $g_\mathrm{ctrl,peak}$.
\end{itemize}

The critical concentration is measured inside the evaluator. The core
solve at \SI{1000}{ppm} is supplemented by unrodded core solves at
\SI{2000}{ppm} and, where the root is not yet bracketed,
\SI{3000}{ppm}. The beginning-of-life concentration $c_\mathrm{BOL}$ is
the root of the measured reactivity $\rho(c)$, interpolated between the
bracketing solves or extrapolated above the highest one, and the
differential worth is the local slope of $\rho(c)$ at that root. The
concentration at the operating maximum then adds the part of the
gadolinium hump that the boron has to hold.

\begin{equation}
c_\mathrm{max} = c_\mathrm{BOL} + \frac{\max\!\big(L\,\Delta\rho_\mathrm{hump},\ 0\big)}{w_\mathrm{B}}
\label{eq:c9-cmax}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $c_\mathrm{BOL}$ is the critical boron concentration at
        beginning of life, in ppm.

  \item $\Delta\rho_\mathrm{hump}$ is the hump of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:c7-hump}, taken from the xenon-free assembly
        depletion history that the evaluator already computes, in pcm.
        A hump below the noise floor of \SI{400}{pcm} is set to zero.

  \item $L$ is the ratio of the assembly to the core beginning-of-life
        eigenvalue of the same design, which carries the assembly hump
        to the core, dimensionless.

  \item $w_\mathrm{B}$ is the differential boron worth at the root, in
        pcm per ppm.
\end{itemize}

Two guards bound the objective where the measurement cannot reach. A
design that cannot be made critical at zero boron returns a negative
root, which would be the best value in the archive for a minimised
objective and would steer the surrogate toward a region where no design
operates, so such designs are clamped to \SI{0}{ppm}, and they are in any
case infeasible on $g_{k\min}$. A root extrapolated far beyond the
measured points is clipped at \SI{6000}{ppm}, twice the highest measured
concentration. Self-shielding makes the true value higher still, so the
ranking survives, and the plateau prevents a single design at many
thousand ppm from stretching the surrogate.

The boron ceiling of Section~\ref{sec:meth-mtc}, \SI{2763}{ppm}, is
recorded on every design as $g_\mathrm{boron}$ and is not a
constraint, because it was measured on the lattice of a single
Campaign 8 design. The design of experiments has 24 points, followed by
six infill iterations of six designs, 60 evaluations in all.

\section{Soluble boron treatment}
\label{sec:meth-boron}

The coolant carries a constant concentration of \SI{1000}{ppm} of
natural boron in every campaign of this work. The value is taken from
the operating conditions of the NuScale-like neutronics benchmark
defined by Fridman, Bilodid and
Valtavirta~\cite{fridman_nuscale_benchmark_2023}, together with the
uniform fuel temperature of \SI{900}{\kelvin} and the uniform coolant
and structure temperature of \SI{600}{\kelvin} adopted in
Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}. The concentration is a
fixed modelling parameter and is not exposed to the optimiser as a
design variable.

The same benchmark specification fixes the assembly geometry inherited
by the present model, namely a $17 \times 17$ lattice with 264 fuel
rods, 24 guide tubes and one central instrument tube, an active height
of \SI{200}{\centi\metre}, and a heavy reflector enclosed by a
stainless steel core barrel. Adopting the benchmark boron concentration
therefore keeps the coolant composition consistent with the geometry
and material specification from which the model derives, rather than
introducing an independent assumption.

The benchmark is a static Beginning of Life (BOL) specification and
does not prescribe a depletion history. The extension of the benchmark
conditions to a depleted core is an assumption of the present work and
is treated as such in Section~\ref{sec:meth-boron-limits}.

\subsection{Basis for the choice}
\label{sec:meth-boron-basis}

Soluble boron is the reactivity-control method of the boron-controlled
Pressurized Water Reactor (PWR) lineage from which the present core
inherits its assembly geometry, and it is retained by the majority of
land-based light-water Small Modular Reactor (SMR) concepts now under
development. Four considerations support retaining it here.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Traceability of the reference conditions.} The
        NuScale-like benchmark provides a published specification, a
        Monte Carlo reference solution obtained with
        Serpent~\cite{fridman_nuscale_benchmark_2023}, and an
        independent reproduction with the same transport code and
        nuclear data library used in this work, namely OpenMC with
        ENDF/B-VII.1~\cite{ez_aldeen_2025}. That
        reproduction reports agreement within 16 to \SI{31}{pcm} in the
        effective multiplication factor and a root mean square relative
        difference below \SI{0.11}{\percent} in assembly radial power.
        Equivalent open documentation does not exist for the
        LABGENE-class naval core that motivates the study. Brazil
        provides updated design information for the land-based prototype
        reactor to the International Atomic Energy Agency, while an
        arrangement under the special procedures of Article~13 of the
        Quadripartite Safeguards Agreement is still being
        discussed~\cite{eu_statement_bog_2025}. No
        soluble boron specification for that core is available in the
        public literature.
  \item \textbf{Containment of the design space.} Soluble boron and the
        two absorber-related design variables, the gadolinium oxide
        (\ce{Gd2O3}) weight fraction and the gadolinium-bearing pin
        count, act on the same physical quantity, namely the excess
        reactivity available at beginning of life. Allowing boron to
        vary would make the study partly a study of boron rather than
        of the fuel and lattice variables under investigation.
  \item \textbf{Scope of the safety demonstration.} A
        Soluble-Boron-Free (SBF) core transfers the entire
        excess-reactivity budget to burnable absorbers and control
        rods. A credible SBF design therefore requires a control rod
        bank layout, a Cold Shutdown Margin (CSDM) demonstration under
        the single-failure assumption, and an explicit Moderator
        Temperature Coefficient (MTC) verification. Of these, only the
        bank layout and an operating controllability screen with the
        regulating banks at power conditions lie within the constraint
        set, and only from Campaign 7
        (Section~\ref{sec:meth-ctrl}). The shutdown margin under the
        single-failure assumption remains outside the set defined in
        Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}, and the coefficient is
        evaluated outside the loop, on one design, through the boron
        ceiling of Section~\ref{sec:meth-mtc}. Treating SBF operation as an
        assumption without that supporting analysis would weaken rather
        than strengthen the design basis.
  \item \textbf{Known direction of the bias.} As shown in
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-boron-bias}, the presence of soluble
        boron relaxes the reactivity constraint and shortens the
        computed cycle length relative to boron-free operation. The
        choice is therefore not a concealed relaxation of the
        optimisation problem in the objective that is being maximised.
\end{enumerate}

The sequence of verifying a model against a documented boron-controlled
specification before addressing boron-free operation is itself
established practice. Ez Aldeen et
al.~\cite{ez_aldeen_2025} adopt the boron-controlled
NuScale-like benchmark as the verification basis for a modelling
programme whose stated target is boron-free SMR cores.

Soluble-boron-free operation is nevertheless a recurring feature of
proposed compact and civil marine light-water designs in the open
literature. The reported motivations are a more negative Moderator
Temperature Coefficient (MTC), reduced primary-circuit corrosion, lower
liquid waste production, simplification of the Chemical and Volume
Control System (CVCS), and, for shipboard application, the concern that
seawater ingress following a hull breach could dilute borated coolant
and introduce a criticality
hazard~\cite{kusunoki_mrx_2000,alam_partI_2019,alzaben_boronfree_2019,ismr_sbf_2024,rr_smr_atf_2025,song_sanchez_2026}.
Reactivity-control practice in operating naval propulsion reactors is
not documented in publicly available sources, and no claim about it is
made here.

\subsection{Direction and magnitude of the bias}
\label{sec:meth-boron-bias}

Removing the soluble boron raises the beginning-of-life multiplication
factor. The reactivity worth of the boron inventory converts to a
change in multiplication factor through

\begin{equation}
  \Delta k = k_{0}\,k_{1}\,\Delta\rho ,
  \label{eq:boron-rho-to-k}
\end{equation}

where
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Delta k$ is the change in effective multiplication factor,
        dimensionless,
  \item $k_{0}$ is the multiplication factor with soluble boron
        present, dimensionless,
  \item $k_{1}$ is the multiplication factor with the boron removed,
        dimensionless,
  \item $\Delta\rho$ is the integral reactivity worth of the removed
        boron, expressed in units of $\Delta k / k$.
\end{itemize}

For the beginning-of-life values encountered in this work the product
$k_{0}k_{1}$ is close to $2$, so a boron worth of one unit of
reactivity appears as approximately two units of multiplication factor.
The integral worth itself depends on the moderator-to-fuel ratio and on
the competition for thermal neutrons with the gadolinium-bearing pins,
both of which vary across the design space, and it is therefore
measured rather than assumed in this work.

The measurement was made on the eleven Campaign 8 candidates rather
than on the Campaign 5 finalists. Between 0 and \SI{1000}{ppm} the
integral worth is \num{3130} to \SI{8540}{pcm}, a differential worth
of 3.13 to 8.54\,pcm per ppm that falls with enrichment, and
6.98\,pcm per ppm for the candidate design 47
(Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-boron}, Table~\ref{tab:c8-post-boron}).

Two consequences follow, and both are recorded as documented biases
rather than corrected within the campaigns.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Reactivity constraint.} Boron-free operation tightens
        the beginning-of-life reactivity constraint $g_{k\max}$ and
        displaces the entire feasibility envelope. Boron-free operation
        is closer to the naval mission but makes the reactivity-control
        problem harder, not easier.
  \item \textbf{Cycle length.} An operating PWR dilutes the boron
        progressively toward zero over the cycle, which releases
        reactivity that is unavailable to the constant-concentration
        model used here. The computed cycle length in Effective Full
        Power Days (EFPD) is therefore a lower bound for designs that
        terminate below the burnup ceiling. The magnitude of the
        shortfall scales with the ratio of the boron worth at end of
        cycle to the reactivity decline rate, and is consequently
        design-dependent. The bias is positive for every design, so the
        ordering of the Pareto front along the cycle-length axis is
        expected to be preserved, but the front is stretched rather
        than translated.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Declared limitations}
\label{sec:meth-boron-limits}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The benchmark from which the concentration is taken is a static
        Beginning of Life (BOL) specification. Its extension to a
        depleted core is an assumption of the present work and is not
        supported by the benchmark itself.
  \item The concentration is held constant throughout depletion. No
        critical boron search is performed at any burnup step, so the
        depletion trajectory represents a reactivity-tracking model and
        not an operating history.
  \item Soluble boron makes the Moderator Temperature Coefficient (MTC)
        less negative than boron-free operation would, so the present
        choice is not conservative for that parameter, in contrast to
        the reactivity and cycle-length effects described in
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-boron-bias}. The MTC is not evaluated
        inside the loop. It is evaluated on one Campaign 8 design as
        the boron ceiling of Section~\ref{sec:meth-mtc}, against which
        the critical concentrations of the candidates are compared.
  \item Boron depletion and \isotope[10]{B} burnout are not modelled,
        which is consistent with the constant-concentration assumption.
\end{enumerate}

Under the one-change rule stated in
Section~\ref{sec:meth-campaigns} the treatment is held fixed at
\SI{1000}{ppm} through every campaign of this work. Campaign 9 keeps
the depletion at that concentration and measures the critical
concentration as an objective (Section~\ref{sec:meth-c9}). The design basis
is therefore closed on the benchmark-traceable value, and the
soluble-boron-free case is recorded as future work. The direct
measurement that would precede any such change has been performed in
part, as the boron worth study of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-boron-worth} reports the differential worth and
the rods-only margin of the Campaign 8 candidates at
\SIrange{0}{1500}{ppm}, so the eventual decision would rest on
computed values rather than on estimates.

\subsection{Boron worth and the rods-only margin of the candidates}
\label{sec:meth-boron-worth}

Every eigenvalue in this work is computed with the benchmark
concentration of \SI{1000}{ppm} of natural boron in the coolant, as a
mass fraction, and with the gadolinia of the design in place. The
beginning-of-life excess reactivity of a core is therefore the excess
that remains \emph{after} the boron and the burnable absorber have
acted, and it is the quantity that the control rods alone must hold.

\begin{equation}
\rho(k) = \frac{k - 1}{k}
\label{eq:rho-def}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\rho$ is the reactivity, dimensionless, reported in pcm by
        multiplying by $10^5$.

  \item $k$ is the core effective multiplication factor in the state
        considered, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

The share of the hold-down that the soluble boron carries is not
visible in a single solve. It is measured on the candidate designs by
re-solving the same core at several concentrations, unrodded and
rodded, and reading the differences.

\begin{equation}
W_\mathrm{B}(c_1 \rightarrow c_2) = \rho\big(k_\mathrm{ARO}(c_1)\big) - \rho\big(k_\mathrm{ARO}(c_2)\big)
\label{eq:boron-worth}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $W_\mathrm{B}$ is the boron worth between two concentrations,
        positive, in pcm.

  \item $k_\mathrm{ARO}(c)$ is the unrodded core eigenvalue at
        concentration $c$, dimensionless.

  \item $c_1$ and $c_2$ are the two concentrations, in ppm by mass,
        with $c_1 < c_2$.
\end{itemize}

The differential worth is $W_\mathrm{B}$ divided by the concentration
difference, in pcm per ppm. The rods-only margin at a concentration is
the subcriticality with the banks inserted.

\begin{equation}
M_\mathrm{bank}(c) = -\rho\big(k_\mathrm{bank}(c)\big)
\label{eq:rod-only-margin}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $M_\mathrm{bank}$ is the margin, positive when subcritical, in
        pcm.

  \item $k_\mathrm{bank}(c)$ is the core eigenvalue with the bank set
        inserted at concentration $c$, dimensionless. At
        \SI{1000}{ppm} and the sixteen regulating banks it reproduces
        the campaign screen of Equation~\eqref{eq:g-ctrl-meth}.
\end{itemize}

The margin at zero concentration answers the soluble-boron-free
question, which is not the design basis of this core and is reported
as a sensitivity only. The share of the beginning-of-life hold-down
that the boron carries is the ratio below.

\begin{equation}
s_\mathrm{B} = \frac{W_\mathrm{B}(0 \rightarrow 1000)}{W_\mathrm{B}(0 \rightarrow 1000) + \rho_\mathrm{bank}(1000)}
\label{eq:boron-share}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $s_\mathrm{B}$ is the boron share of the hold-down,
        dimensionless.

  \item $\rho_\mathrm{bank}(1000)$ is the sixteen-bank worth at the
        benchmark concentration, in pcm, from
        Equation~\eqref{eq:bank-worth}.
\end{itemize}

The measurement runs one core solve per state, concentration and
seed, at the campaign core fidelity, on the candidates only. It is
implemented in \texttt{boron\_worth.py} with the same solver path as
the campaign evaluator, so the values at \SI{1000}{ppm} close on the
archive to within the Monte Carlo resolution.

\section{Limits of the calibrated and screened quantities}
\label{sec:meth-limits}

The loop of Section~\ref{sec:meth-loop} owes its throughput to
substitution. A transport solve that would otherwise be repeated in every
evaluation is replaced by a quantity measured once and reused: the
end-of-cycle target of Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget}, the axial factor
of Section~\ref{sec:meth-lax} and the boron worth of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-boron-worth} are all substitutions of this kind.

Every such quantity carries a qualification that is not part of the
number. A slope holds over an interval, a screen holds at a state, and an
estimate has an uncertainty. When the qualification is not stored with
the value, later use can produce an error that goes unnoticed, because a
lookup returns a plausible value for every input it is given.

Three instances were found during this work. They are reported in the
methodology because each is a property of the method and not of a
particular design, and because the correction in each case is
procedural.

\subsection{A secant used in place of a local derivative}
\label{sec:meth-limits-secant}

A quantity tabulated at a few widely spaced nodes gives, on differencing,
the mean sensitivity over the interval between them.

\begin{equation}
\bar{S} = \frac{f(x_2) - f(x_1)}{x_2 - x_1}
\label{eq:secant}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\bar{S}$ is the secant slope over the tabulated interval, in
        units of $f$ per unit of $x$.
  \item $f$ is the calibrated response.
  \item $x_1$ and $x_2$ are the bracketing node values of the
        independent variable, in its own unit.
\end{itemize}

The evaluator needs instead the local derivative at the operating point
of the design under evaluation.

\begin{equation}
S(x_0) = \left. \frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x} \right|_{x_0}
\label{eq:local-derivative}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $S(x_0)$ is the local sensitivity at the operating point, in
        units of $f$ per unit of $x$.
  \item $x_0$ is the value of the independent variable for the design
        being evaluated, in its own unit.
\end{itemize}

Equations~\eqref{eq:secant} and~\eqref{eq:local-derivative} coincide
only where $f$ is linear over the tabulated interval. Two instances were
quantified.

\paragraph{Enrichment sensitivity of the end-of-cycle target.}
A single sensitivity of \SI{-64.1}{pcm} per weight per cent describes the
target over the enrichment interval. A dedicated sweep over the same
variable resolves a quadratic term at 6.4 standard deviations from zero,
so the response is not linear over the interval and the single figure is
a secant. On the composition of C8-47 the consequence is \SI{9}{pcm},
and the corresponding uncertainty on the cycle length is 1.3 per cent.

\paragraph{Differential worth of soluble boron.}
The worth used to convert an excess reactivity into a critical
concentration was built as the difference between the boron-free state
and the reference state of Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}, divided by
that concentration.

\begin{equation}
\bar{w}_\mathrm{B} = \frac{\rho(0) - \rho(c_\mathrm{ref})}{c_\mathrm{ref}}
\label{eq:boron-chord}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\bar{w}_\mathrm{B}$ is the mean differential boron worth over the
        interval, in pcm per ppm.
  \item $\rho(0)$ is the core reactivity with no soluble boron, in pcm.
  \item $\rho(c_\mathrm{ref})$ is the core reactivity at the reference
        concentration, in pcm.
  \item $c_\mathrm{ref}$ is the reference soluble boron concentration of
        Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}, \SI{1000}{ppm}.
\end{itemize}

Equation~\eqref{eq:boron-chord} is a secant anchored at the
low-concentration end of the interval, where the boron shields itself
least and the worth per unit concentration is largest.

Two independent measurements resolve the concentration dependence. The
sweep of Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-boron} gives, for C8-47, \SI{7.104}{pcm}
per ppm over 0 to \SI{500}{ppm}, \SI{6.863}{pcm} per ppm over 500 to
\SI{1000}{ppm} and \SI{6.626}{pcm} per ppm over 1000 to \SI{1500}{ppm}.
The scan of Section~\ref{sec:meth-mtc} extends the measurement, with
\SI{6.78}{pcm} per ppm at \SI{1000}{ppm}, \SI{6.64}{pcm} per ppm at
\SI{2200}{ppm} and \SI{6.12}{pcm} per ppm at \SI{5000}{ppm}. The worth
decreases monotonically with concentration, as expected of a strong
thermal absorber that progressively shields itself. The reliable
procedure is therefore to locate the root of the measured reactivity
curve instead of inverting a single worth.

\begin{equation}
\rho(c_\mathrm{BOL}) = 0
\label{eq:cbol-root}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\rho(c)$ is the unrodded core reactivity at soluble boron
        concentration $c$, in pcm, interpolated between measured points.
  \item $c_\mathrm{BOL}$ is the critical soluble boron concentration at
        beginning of life, in ppm.
\end{itemize}

For C8-47 the linear estimate of Table~\ref{tab:c8-swing} is
\SI{2348}{ppm} and the measured root is \SI{2327}{ppm}, a correction of
\SI{-21}{ppm}. Self-shielding alone would place the measured root above
the estimate, because a worth taken where the boron shields itself less
overstates the worth at the concentration of interest and so understates
the concentration needed. The measured root lies below the estimate. The
power law of Equation~\eqref{eq:wb-proxy} underestimates the worth of this
design by about 2 per cent, against its relative root-mean-square residual
of 1.6 per cent, and an underestimated worth overstates the concentration.
The measured correction is the net of the two effects, and it is small.
Whatever its sign, a worth measured as a chord is not the worth at the
concentration where it is applied, and the curve has to be measured.

\subsection{A constraint evaluated where it does not bind}
\label{sec:meth-limits-state}

A constraint screened at a single state is conservative only if that
state bounds the constrained quantity over the interval of interest.
With an integral burnable absorber this is not guaranteed, because
depletion of the absorber can release reactivity faster than the fuel
consumes it and produce an interior maximum of the multiplication factor,
the hump of Equation~\eqref{eq:c7-hump}. The control margin that must be
screened is then the beginning-of-life margin reduced by the core-level
hump.

\begin{equation}
m_\mathrm{peak} = m_\mathrm{BOL} - \max\!\left( L\,
\Delta \rho_\mathrm{hump},\ 0 \right)
\label{eq:margin-peak}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $m_\mathrm{peak}$ is the control margin at the operating maximum
        of the cycle, in pcm.
  \item $m_\mathrm{BOL}$ is the margin at beginning of life from the
        screen of Equation~\eqref{eq:g-ctrl-meth}, in pcm.
  \item $L$ is the ratio of the assembly to the core beginning-of-life
        eigenvalue of the same design, which carries the assembly hump to
        the core, dimensionless.
  \item $\Delta \rho_\mathrm{hump}$ is the assembly hump of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:c7-hump}, in pcm.
\end{itemize}

A non-positive hump leaves the margin unchanged, and for such a design the
beginning-of-life screen bounds the whole cycle. The resolution of the
depletion solve is 150 to \SI{250}{pcm} per state, so amplitudes below
\SI{400}{pcm} are reported as unresolved and are not corrected for. Two
instances were quantified.

\paragraph{Controllability screen.}
Applied retrospectively to the 58 Campaign 7 designs that carry a
validated depletion history, Equation~\eqref{eq:margin-peak} leaves 32
designs satisfying the screen of Equation~\eqref{eq:g-ctrl-meth} at their
own operating maximum, against 38 at beginning of life
(Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-retro}). Six designs were admitted as feasible by
the campaign and are not controllable over their own cycle. All six carry
a thin gadolinia loading spread over 32 to 40 pins per assembly, the
pattern whose absorber burns out first and so produces the largest hump,
consistent with the self-shielding behaviour of gadolinia
rods~\cite{gd_enriched_2021}. Within the eleven Campaign 8 designs of the
post-analysis one design, C8-31, changes verdict: its two-bank reading
falls from \SI{5856}{pcm} at beginning of life to \SI{901}{pcm} at its own
maximum, below the screening threshold of \SI{1010}{pcm}.

\paragraph{Critical boron as a figure of merit.}
The same error affects any figure of merit built on the critical soluble
boron concentration evaluated at beginning of life. A design whose
reactivity is temporarily suppressed by unburned gadolinium reports a low
concentration, while the operationally binding quantity is the maximum
over the cycle. That maximum is Equation~\eqref{eq:c9-cmax}, and it needs
no additional transport, because the depletion history that supplies
$\Delta \rho_\mathrm{hump}$ is already computed by the evaluator.

\subsection{An uncertainty computed and then discarded}
\label{sec:meth-limits-sigma}

The third instance is the mildest and the easiest to repeat. The
end-of-cycle target table of Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget} stores the
fitted reflector sensitivity, the fitted intercept and the reduced
chi-squared of the fit, and not the uncertainty of the slope, so later
comparisons treated an estimate as exact. Refitting from the raw
eigenvalues and their reported standard deviations, which the table does
store, reproduces the stored slope and the stored reduced chi-squared of
0.378 and also returns the uncertainty of the slope.

\begin{equation}
\sigma_S = \sqrt{ \frac{\sum_i w_i}
{\left( \sum_i w_i \right) \left( \sum_i w_i t_i^{2} \right)
- \left( \sum_i w_i t_i \right)^{2}} },
\qquad w_i = \frac{1}{\sigma_{k,i}^{2}}
\label{eq:slope-sigma}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\sigma_S$ is the standard deviation of the fitted slope, in pcm
        per cm.
  \item $w_i$ is the weight of the $i$-th node, in inverse pcm squared.
  \item $\sigma_{k,i}$ is the Monte Carlo standard deviation of the
        eigenvalue at the $i$-th node, in pcm.
  \item $t_i$ is the reflector thickness at the $i$-th node, in cm.
\end{itemize}

The result is \SI{-47.6}{pcm} per centimetre with a standard deviation of
\SI{15.7}{pcm} per centimetre, on the basis normalised by the reference
leakage factor, a relative uncertainty of one third. That is a property
of the measurement. Across the seven nodes from 2.00 to \SI{5.66}{cm} the
reflector is worth about \SI{195}{pcm} in total, while each eigenvalue
carries a Monte Carlo standard deviation of 47 to \SI{60}{pcm}. The whole
effect is about four times the noise on a single point, and no linear fit
through such data can constrain its slope better.

Two independent determinations of the same slope exist, one at the
reference composition of the table and one across six uniform
enrichments. With the slope uncertainty included, their separation from
the table slope is

\begin{equation}
n = \frac{\left| S_A - S_B \right|}
{\sqrt{\sigma_A^{2} + \sigma_B^{2}}}
\label{eq:nsigma}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $n$ is the separation of two determinations, dimensionless.
  \item $S_A$ and $S_B$ are the two slopes, in pcm per cm.
  \item $\sigma_A$ and $\sigma_B$ are their standard deviations, in pcm
        per cm.
\end{itemize}

\noindent which gives 1.47 against the determination at the reference
composition and 1.39 against the enrichment sweep, whose slope is
$-25.1 \pm \SI{4.0}{pcm}$ per centimetre. The three determinations are
consistent. Without the recovered slope uncertainty the same comparisons
would have returned 2.8 standard deviations and suggested a defect that
is not present. The reflector sensitivity is reported in this work as
$-47.6 \pm \SI{15.7}{pcm}$ per centimetre, and no conclusion is drawn
from its difference from either independent determination.

\subsection{Consequence for the framework}
\label{sec:meth-limits-consequence}

The three instances share one origin. A surrogate-assisted framework
earns its throughput by replacing repeated transport with precomputed
quantities, and each replacement carries a qualification that cannot be
recovered from the stored number: the interval over which a response is
linear, the state at which a bound is attained, the uncertainty of an
estimate. Three procedural rules follow, all inexpensive.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every tabulated quantity carries the range of the data on which
        it was fitted, and every consumer emits a warning when it is
        evaluated outside that range.
  \item Every constraint screened at a single state is accompanied by an
        argument, or a measurement, that the state chosen bounds the
        constraint over the interval of interest.
  \item Every fitted quantity is stored with its uncertainty, and no two
        quantities are compared without combining both.
\end{enumerate}

The correction of Equation~\eqref{eq:margin-peak} implements the second
rule, and the retrospective count of Section~\ref{sec:meth-limits-state}
is the measured cost of having omitted it. Section~\ref{sec:meth-limits-sigma}
illustrates the third, at the lower cost of a false alarm instead of a
wrong acceptance.


\section{Moderator temperature coefficient and the soluble boron ceiling}
\label{sec:meth-mtc}

The reference concentration of Section~\ref{sec:meth-boron} is fixed at
\SI{1000}{ppm} throughout the optimisation, so the campaigns do not test
whether a candidate could be held critical by soluble boron at an
acceptable concentration. Soluble boron cannot be increased without limit.
As the concentration rises, the boron expelled from the core by thermal
expansion of the moderator contributes an increasingly positive
reactivity, and above some concentration the moderator temperature
coefficient changes sign. A positive coefficient removes the
self-stabilising response of the core to a power excursion and is not
acceptable at power. The concentration at which the coefficient vanishes
is therefore a ceiling, and it is a property of the lattice.

\subsection{Definition and evaluation}
\label{sec:meth-mtc-def}

The coefficient is evaluated as a two-point difference at constant
pressure, with the moderator density taken consistently with the
temperature from the IAPWS-IF97 formulation~\cite{wagner_iapws_if97_2000}.

\begin{equation}
\alpha_\mathrm{M}(c) = \frac{\rho\!\left(T + \Delta T,\ c\right)
- \rho\!\left(T,\ c\right)}{\Delta T}
\label{eq:mtc}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\alpha_\mathrm{M}$ is the moderator temperature coefficient, in
        pcm per K.
  \item $\rho$ is the core reactivity at the stated moderator temperature
        and soluble boron concentration, in pcm.
  \item $T$ is the moderator temperature, in K, with the density set from
        the steam tables at the operating pressure.
  \item $\Delta T$ is the temperature perturbation, in K.
  \item $c$ is the soluble boron concentration, in ppm.
\end{itemize}

The ceiling is the root of Equation~\eqref{eq:mtc} in concentration,
found by linear interpolation between the bracketing points of a scan.

\begin{equation}
\alpha_\mathrm{M}\!\left(c_\mathrm{ceil}\right) = 0
\label{eq:ceiling}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $c_\mathrm{ceil}$ is the soluble boron ceiling, in ppm,
and the remaining symbols are as in Equation~\eqref{eq:mtc}.

A design is acceptable on this criterion when its critical concentration
of Equation~\eqref{eq:cbol-root}, or its cycle maximum of
Equation~\eqref{eq:c9-cmax} where a positive hump is resolved, lies below
the ceiling.

The ceiling is evaluated outside the optimisation loop. It is recorded
in the archive as $g_\mathrm{boron}$ and is never imposed as a
constraint. The scan was run with \texttt{mtc\_scan.py} with two seeds
per point, on C8-47 at $100\,000$ particles and 170 batches on the zoned
core. It covers the following designs:

\begin{itemize}
  \item C8-47, the Campaign 8 champion, at \SI{12.8}{MPa} and
        \SI{15.5}{MPa}.

  \item Eight Campaign 9 lattices at \SI{12.8}{MPa}, of which C9-47,
        C9-34 and C9-44 are also scanned at \SI{15.5}{MPa}.
\end{itemize}

The two champions, C8-47 and C9-47, are scanned at each pressure both on
a two-dimensional core and on the three-dimensional core model of
Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-3d}, with the withdrawn rods parked, so the
ceiling and the three-dimensional confirmation rest on the same geometry.
The pair of scans separates the axial leakage contribution to the ceiling
from the lattice contribution. The remaining lattices are scanned on the
three-dimensional core model only, because the leakage contribution is
measured on the champions and is not the quantity that distinguishes one
design from another.

Two brackets were scanned: 570 to \SI{590}{K} at \SI{15.5}{MPa}, centred on
the moderator temperature of \SI{580}{K} of
Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}, and 547 to \SI{567}{K} at
\SI{12.8}{MPa}. The ceiling is sensitive to the pressure through the
moderator density, and no open source gives the primary conditions of
LABGENE or of any naval propulsion reactor, so the two brackets are
reference points and carry no claim about the prototype.

add the entry to the bibliography.}

\subsection{Two verification requirements}
\label{sec:meth-mtc-checks}

Equation~\eqref{eq:mtc} is a small difference between two large numbers,
so it is sensitive to any inconsistency between the perturbed and
unperturbed models. Two checks are applied.

\paragraph{The radial zoning must match the evaluator.}
The scan must build the core with the radial enrichment map of the
evaluator, described in Section~\ref{sec:meth-zoning}. A first
implementation omitted the map and solved a radially uniform core. Its
eigenvalue differed from the evaluator by about \SI{2470}{pcm}, and a
further \SI{157}{pcm} came from the difference in moderator density
between the scan state and the campaign reference state. The coefficient
itself proved far less sensitive than the eigenvalue, moving only from
$-26.39 \pm \SI{1.42}{pcm}$ per K to $-24.87 \pm \SI{2.15}{pcm}$ per K,
so radial zoning changes the multiplication factor substantially and the
feedback very little. For the method, the ceiling and the critical
concentration must be computed in the same model: a concentration from
one model compared against a ceiling from another is not a valid
comparison, however well the two numbers agree.

\paragraph{The Doppler coefficient must be resolved and negative.}
The fuel temperature coefficient is evaluated alongside the moderator
coefficient as a validity check. A null or positive value would indicate
that the cross-section library carries a single temperature and that the
temperature interpolation behind Equation~\eqref{eq:mtc} is not active.

\subsection{Dimensionality of the ceiling}
\label{sec:meth-mtc-3d}

The two-dimensional model cannot represent the axial component of the
feedback. When the moderator heats and expands, axial leakage increases
and contributes negative reactivity, so a two-dimensional evaluation of
Equation~\eqref{eq:mtc} omits a stabilising term and returns a ceiling
that is too low. The omitted term is measured as the difference between
the three- and two-dimensional coefficients at the same state.

\begin{equation}
\alpha_\mathrm{ax} = \alpha_\mathrm{M}^\mathrm{3D}
- \alpha_\mathrm{M}^\mathrm{2D}
\label{eq:axial-feedback}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\alpha_\mathrm{ax}$ is the axial leakage contribution to the
        moderator temperature coefficient, in pcm per K.
  \item $\alpha_\mathrm{M}^\mathrm{3D}$ and
        $\alpha_\mathrm{M}^\mathrm{2D}$ are the coefficients evaluated in
        three and in two dimensions at the same state, in pcm per K.
\end{itemize}

The two-dimensional ceiling is reported as a conservative screen and the
three-dimensional ceiling as the physical result. Every margin quoted in
this work states its dimensionality, and no margin obtained in one
dimensionality is compared against a concentration obtained in the other.
The same discipline applies to the rodded margins of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-ctrl}, where the two- and three-dimensional readings
of the same state can differ in sign.


\section{Diagnostics, environments, reproducibility}
\label{sec:meth-diagnostics}

Shannon-entropy source-convergence monitoring is attached to every
model. The reflective single assembly is source-converged from
batch~1. The finite core has a real transient, and one core case
converged only at batch~94, hence the generous 60--120 inactive batches
of the core solves, with the convergence batch of each run recorded in the archive.

Campaigns 1 and 2 ran on a cloud instance (AWS EC2 c7a.8xlarge,
AMD EPYC 9R14, 32 physical cores without simultaneous
multithreading), inside a purpose-built Docker image that pins the
Conda environment. Campaigns 3 to 5 ran on a 32-core, 64-thread
workstation without administrative privileges, on the same pinned
environment. Campaigns 6 and 7 returned to the cloud instance under
the identical image, and Campaigns 8 and 9 ran on the workstation, whose
measured particle rate on 64 threads equals that of the 32 cloud cores
(Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-cost}). The reproducibility unit is the environment
definition committed to the repository. OpenMC 0.15.3 and pymoo
0.6.2 are identical across the two hosts, verified by smoke
execution and $k_\infty$ agreement.

\subsection{Cost accounting}
\label{sec:meth-cost}

The per-evaluation cost stored in the archive is the sum of the
assembly solve, the core solve and the depletion timers. Through
Campaign 7 the control solve ran after those timers closed and was
stored separately, so the archived cost understated the true cost by
the control share, \SI{11}{\%} over Campaign 7. The block-level wall
time of the run log, which is measured around the whole evaluation
call, was unaffected and is the figure reported in the cost tables.
From Campaign 8 every rodded solve is folded into the archived cost
and the per-case log line reports the full figure.

The per-case parser of the run log was also found never to have
matched a case line of any campaign, because its regular expression
did not anticipate the censoring marker inserted after the cycle
length. The failure was silent. No quantity in this thesis depends on
its output, since every per-design value is read from the archive, and
the parser was corrected before Campaign 8.

Each campaign needs a scratch directory of its own. Case directories
are numbered in evaluation order and not hashed, so a campaign that
shares the directory of its predecessor finds the depletion results of
a different design under the same case number. When Campaign 9 was
first started in the directory of Campaign 8, the depletion restart
failed on an array-shape mismatch in the results file, and the
campaign was run in a separate directory. The failure was loud, and no
result of either campaign was affected.

\subsection{Level of the depletion calculation}
\label{sec:meth-dep-level}

Every cycle length reported in this work comes from a depletion of the
two-dimensional reflected assembly. No campaign depletes the core. The
core enters only through the leakage-corrected target of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-lax}, which is calibrated once per reflector
thickness and then reused. This subsection states what the alternative
would have cost, because the choice is the single largest approximation
in the cost model of Section~\ref{sec:meth-cost} and it is defended here
with measured timings rather than with an assertion.

\subsubsection{Cost model of one transport solve}

The wall time of a single OpenMC solve on the reference workstation is
described by a fixed overhead plus a throughput term.

\begin{equation}
t_\mathrm{solve} = c_0 + \frac{H}{r}
\label{eq:t-solve}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $t_\mathrm{solve}$ is the wall time of one eigenvalue solve, in
        seconds.

  \item $c_0$ is the fixed overhead of a solve, that is the
        cross-section load, the geometry construction and the tally
        setup, in seconds.

  \item $H$ is the number of active neutron histories, that is the
        particle count multiplied by the number of active batches,
        dimensionless.

  \item $r$ is the sustained throughput of the machine, in active
        histories per second.
\end{itemize}

The two parameters are obtained from the two solve types the campaign
already times on every evaluation. The assembly solve runs at
\num{4000} particles and 60 batches with 20 inactive, that is
\num{160000} active histories, and costs \SI{11.4}{s} on average over
the sixty designs. The core solve runs at \num{100000} particles and
170 batches with 60 inactive, that is \num{11.0}{e6} active histories,
and costs \SI{106.9}{s}. Solving Equation~\eqref{eq:t-solve} for the
pair gives

\begin{equation}
c_0 = \SI{10.0}{s}, \qquad r = \SI{1.135e5}{histories\per\second}
\label{eq:t-solve-fit}
\end{equation}

\noindent on the sixty-four-thread workstation of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-cost}. The overhead is therefore not negligible
at the campaign fidelity. Of the \SI{11.4}{s} of an assembly solve,
only \SI{1.4}{s} is transport and \SI{10.0}{s} is setup.

\subsubsection{Cost model of one depletion step}

A depletion step is one transport solve followed by the depletion
bookkeeping, namely the construction of the coupled operator, the
reaction-rate tallies, the Bateman solution for every depletable
material and the results file written to disk.

\begin{equation}
t_\mathrm{step} = t_\mathrm{solve} + N_g\,\tau_B
\label{eq:t-step}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $t_\mathrm{step}$ is the wall time of one depletion step, in
        seconds.

  \item $N_g$ is the number of depletable material groups carried by
        the model, dimensionless.

  \item $\tau_B$ is the bookkeeping cost per material group per step,
        in seconds.
\end{itemize}

The archive gives both terms directly. Averaged over the sixty
Campaign 8 designs, the depletion costs \SI{925.3}{s} and comprises
\num{14.6} steps, so one step costs \SI{62.6}{s}. Subtracting the
\SI{11.4}{s} assembly solve leaves

\begin{equation}
N_g^\mathrm{asm}\,\tau_B = \SI{51.2}{s}
\label{eq:bateman-asm}
\end{equation}

\noindent for the assembly. The consequence is the central fact of this
subsection. At the campaign fidelity a depletion step is
\SI{82}{\percent} bookkeeping and \SI{18}{\percent} transport. The cost
of moving the depletion to the core is therefore governed by the number
of depletable material groups the core model creates, and not by the
size of its transport problem.

\subsubsection{Cost of a core-level depletion}

Two quantities change when the depletion moves to the core. The number
of material groups rises, because the radial enrichment zoning and any
axial subdivision of the burnup regions multiply the groups the Bateman
solver must advance. The particle count must rise with the number of
groups, because each group receives a smaller share of the histories
and its reaction rates would otherwise lose precision.

\begin{equation}
t_\mathrm{step}^\mathrm{core} = c_0 + f_p\,\frac{H_\mathrm{core}}{r}
 + \frac{N_g}{N_g^\mathrm{asm}}\,\left(N_g^\mathrm{asm}\,\tau_B\right)
\label{eq:t-step-core}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $t_\mathrm{step}^\mathrm{core}$ is the wall time of one
        core-level depletion step, in seconds.

  \item $f_p$ is the factor by which the particle count is raised to
        preserve the per-group reaction-rate precision, dimensionless.

  \item $H_\mathrm{core}$ is the active history count of the campaign
        core solve, \num{11.0e6}, dimensionless.

  \item $N_g / N_g^\mathrm{asm}$ is the ratio of core to assembly
        material groups, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{The cost of depleting in three dimensions}

The three-dimensional confirmation of Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-3d}
provides a measured geometric penalty, because it solves the same
designs in the two-dimensional core and in the model with structural
components at identical fidelity, \num{150000} particles and 200 batches
with 80 inactive, sixty-six solves of each. The two-dimensional solve
costs \SI{216.0}{s} and the solve with structural components
\SI{269.2}{s}. Removing the
overhead of Equation~\eqref{eq:t-solve-fit} from both gives a pure
transport ratio of \num{1.26}. The assembly stack, the spacer grids,
the control-rod stacks, the barrel, the downcomer and the vessel wall
together therefore cost a quarter more transport time, not a factor.

Table~\ref{tab:dep-3d-cost} applies that penalty to the second row of

running. The penalty of the water-slab model is an estimate, since that
model was never timed at the campaign core fidelity.

\subsubsection{Consequence for the method}

The separation adopted throughout this work follows from the figures
above. The depletion is performed on the two-dimensional reflected
assembly, the radial and axial leakage of the finite core enters
through the calibrated target of Section~\ref{sec:meth-lax}, and the
three-dimensional information is recovered by confirmation at fixed
burnup on a small set of candidates rather than inside the loop. That
separation is what makes a sixty-evaluation campaign fit in one day of
machine time.

The estimates rest on four stated assumptions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The bookkeeping cost is taken to scale linearly with the number
        of depletable material groups. This is the expected behaviour
        of an independent Bateman solution per material, and it has not
        been measured at core level in this work.

  \item The particle factor is set equal to the group ratio. A looser
        reaction-rate tolerance than the eigenvalue tolerance would
        reduce the transport term, but not the bookkeeping term, which
        dominates in every row of Table~\ref{tab:dep-level-cost}.

  \item The transport penalty of the water-slab model is estimated at
        \num{1.10} and not measured, since the axial leakage study was
        run at a different fidelity and its timings were not archived.

  \item All figures are specific to the sixty-four-thread workstation
        and to the schedule of \num{14.6} steps. Memory, which grows
        with the number of depletable materials and their nuclide
        vectors, is not considered and may bind before wall time does
        in the third row of Table~\ref{tab:dep-level-cost}.
\end{enumerate}
