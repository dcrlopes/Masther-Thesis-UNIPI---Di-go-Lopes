\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

\section{Design basis and technological conservatism}
\label{sec:design-basis}

The reference small modular reactor is conceived under a deliberate
constraint of \emph{technological conservatism}: the core must rest on
nuclear-fuel technology already industrialised in Brazil. The work then
characterises a plausibly deployable naval core, not a green-field
concept. Departures from established domestic practice are admitted
only where physically necessary to meet the mission objective, namely a
long refuelling interval under low-enriched uranium (LEU).

The element carried over unchanged from national production is the fuel
rod. UO$_2$ ceramic pellets, Zircaloy-4 cladding, and the
rod-fabrication route operated by Ind\'ustrias Nucleares do Brasil
(INB) at the F\'abrica de Combust\'iveis Nucleares in Resende, which
supplies reload fuel for Angra~1 and~2~\cite{inb_fcn_resende}, are
adopted without modification.

The reference core departs from the $16\times16$ lattice of the Angra
units~\cite{angra_fuel_1_2} by adopting the $17\times17$
Westinghouse-type assembly, with 264 fuel rods, 24 guide tubes and one
instrumentation tube. This changes the assembly \emph{skeleton}, not
the rod technology. The $17\times17$ lattice is the most widely
fabricated and best-benchmarked PWR geometry in service, so its
adoption is an incremental industrialisation step. It also provides the
validated basis for benchmarking this model against the open
literature~\cite{nuscale_htp2_2017,nuscale_like_core_2024}.

Exactly two departures from strict Angra heritage are therefore
admitted.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Enrichment raised toward the LEU ceiling.

  \item The $17\times17$ lattice.
\end{enumerate}

Radial enrichment zoning and gadolinia burnable absorbers, exercised by
the optimiser, are standard PWR reload practice consistent with the
same basis.

\section{Optimisation problem}
\label{sec:meth-problem}

\subsection{Problem statement}

The core design task is posed as a constrained two-objective problem
over a five-dimensional design space. The design space is summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:design-space} and the constraints in
Table~\ref{tab:constraints}.

The first objective maximises the cycle length.

\begin{equation}
\max_{\mathbf{x}\,\in\,\mathcal{X}} \, \mathrm{EFPD}(\mathbf{x})
\label{eq:obj_efpd}
\end{equation}

The second objective minimises the radial peaking factor.

\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{x}\,\in\,\mathcal{X}} \, F_{\Delta H}(\mathbf{x})
\label{eq:obj_fdh}
\end{equation}

Both are subject to the feasibility condition below.

\begin{equation}
g_j(\mathbf{x}) \le 0,
\qquad j = 1,\dots,5
\label{eq:constraint_form}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathbf{x}$ is the design vector, whose five components and
        their units are listed in Table~\ref{tab:design-space}.

  \item $\mathcal{X}$ is the box-bounded design space defined by those
        same bounds.

  \item $\mathrm{EFPD}(\mathbf{x})$ is the cycle length of design
        $\mathbf{x}$ in effective full-power days, in d, obtained from
        Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd} with the end-of-cycle criterion of
        Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}.

  \item $F_{\Delta H}(\mathbf{x})$ is the radial peaking factor of
        design $\mathbf{x}$, dimensionless, defined in
        Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_definition}.

  \item $g_j(\mathbf{x})$ is the $j$-th inequality constraint in
        normalised violation form, dimensionless, feasible when
        non-positive.
\end{itemize}

The evaluation level of $F_{\Delta H}$ changes between campaigns and is
fixed in Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}, with the validation
that motivated the change in Section~\ref{sec:meth-core-objective}.

The numerical bound of $g_\mathrm{peak}$ is anchored in licensing
practice. The radial enthalpy-rise hot channel factor is a controlled
design limit that every pressurised water reactor documents in its
safety analysis report, and the publicly available U.S.~EPR Final
Safety Analysis Report is the representative example adopted
here~\cite{epr_fsar_ch4}. The value of 2.0 is a deliberately generous
screening bound for this exploratory single-batch study rather than the
limit of any specific plant. Feasibility against it therefore screens
candidate designs and does not claim compliance with a licensed core.

\subsection{Geometric feasibility}

The vessel-fit constraint $g_\mathrm{geom}$ uses the circumscribed
envelope of the intact core footprint.

\begin{equation}
R_\mathrm{env}(p) = \sqrt{13}\,\cdot\,17\,p
\label{eq:renv}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $R_\mathrm{env}$ is the circumscribed radius of the fuel
        region, in cm.

  \item $p$ is the pin pitch, in cm.

  \item $17$ is the number of pin cells per assembly side,
        dimensionless, so that $17p$ is the assembly pitch in cm.

  \item $\sqrt{13} \approx 3.606$ is the dimensionless distance from
        the core centre to the outermost fuel corner of the
        $6\times6$-minus-corners map, in assembly pitches. The extreme
        corner lies three assembly pitches along one axis and two along
        the other, so its distance is $\sqrt{3^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{13}$.
\end{itemize}

The sensitivity of the envelope to the pitch follows directly.

\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}R_\mathrm{env}}{\mathrm{d}p} = 61.3~\mathrm{cm\,cm^{-1}}
\label{eq:drenv}
\end{equation}

A pitch increase of one millimetre therefore consumes about six
millimetres of radial budget. Pitch and reflector thickness compete
directly for the fixed allowance of the 90\,cm vessel internal radius
of Table~\ref{tab:model-geometry}, which makes the two variables
strongly coupled rather than independent.

The constraint is enforced analytically inside the optimiser rather
than during model construction. Campaign 1 showed that omitting it lets
the genetic algorithm converge on designs that cannot be built, because
the fuel region and the reflector together exceed the vessel envelope.

\subsection{Reactivity window basis}
\label{sec:meth-kbasis}

Through Campaign 5 the reactivity window acted on the assembly infinite
multiplication factor. The Route B construction of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget} makes the corresponding core eigenvalue
available at no additional cost, and the leakage gap between the two
bases is not a constant. Across the design space it varies between
about \num{4300} and \SI{8060}{pcm}, growing with the reflector
thickness and the pitch, so a fixed assembly-basis bound is
inconsistently strict in a way that depends on two of the design
variables themselves.

From Campaign 6 both bounds of the window therefore act on the core
effective multiplication factor at beginning of life.

\begin{equation}
1.02 \;\le\; k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}(\mathbf{x}) \;\le\; 1.35
\label{eq:k-window-core}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}$ is the effective multiplication
        factor of the 32-assembly core at beginning of life,
        dimensionless, from the same core solve that supplies the
        peaking objective.

  \item $\mathbf{x}$ is the design vector of
        Table~\ref{tab:design-space}.
\end{itemize}

Two consequences follow. First, the re-scoring of the Campaign 5
archive in Section~\ref{sec:res-c5-kbasis} shows that the core basis at
1.35 and the assembly basis at 1.40 admit the same designs, so the
change is a basis correction with a stated numerical consequence and
not a relaxation of the design intent. Second, the lower bound becomes
physically meaningful. On the assembly basis a floor of 1.02 was
vacuous, whereas on the core basis it rejects genuinely subcritical
cores, and it does so repeatedly in Campaign 6
(Section~\ref{sec:res-c6-doe}).

\subsection{Objective functions}
\label{sec:objective_functions}

\subsubsection{Cycle length}

The cycle-length objective of Equation~\eqref{eq:obj_efpd} is computed
from the end-of-cycle burnup through the general conversion of
Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd}, with the specific power of
Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}. What remains to be fixed here is the
criterion that defines the end of cycle on the depletion workhorse, the
reflective single assembly.

\begin{equation}
B_\mathrm{EOC} = \min\!\left( B_k,\, B_\mathrm{ATF} \right)
\label{eq:eoc}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
B_k = \min\left\{\, B > 0 \,:\, k_\infty(B) \le k_\mathrm{target} \,\right\}
\label{eq:eoc-k}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $B_\mathrm{EOC}$ is the end-of-cycle burnup entering
        Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd}, in MWd/kgHM.

  \item $B_k$ is the reactivity-defined end-of-cycle burnup, in
        MWd/kgHM.

  \item $B_\mathrm{ATF}$ is the accident-tolerant-fuel discharge-burnup
        limit, equal to 75\,MWd/kgHM, treated in
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-atf}.

  \item $B$ is the burnup along the depletion history, in MWd/kgHM.

  \item $k_\infty(B)$ is the infinite multiplication factor of the
        reflective assembly at burnup $B$, dimensionless.

  \item $k_\mathrm{target}$ is the leakage-corrected end-of-cycle
        target, dimensionless, defined in
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget}.
\end{itemize}

The crossing $B_k$ is obtained by interpolation between the two
depletion steps that bracket the target.

Designs still critical at the depletion horizon in the early
campaigns were right-censored and recorded at the horizon value
rather than extrapolated. The horizon was the evaluator default of
35.5\,MWd/kgHM in Campaign 1 and an explicit 100\,MWd/kgHM in
Campaign 2, and the censored fraction was 54.2\% and 59.5\%
respectively, as quantified in Section~\ref{sec:res-c1c2}. The
discharge limit entered the objective in Campaign 2,
retrospectively, through the exact post-processing transform of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-atf}. From Campaign 3 onward the depletion
terminates at $B_\mathrm{ATF}$ inside the loop, so
Equation~\eqref{eq:eoc} holds by construction and censoring is
eliminated.

The Campaign 2 schedule opens with a beginning-of-life ramp at
0.5, 1, 2, 4 and 6 days, continues in 4 day steps committed in
chunks of two, and terminates adaptively, giving 10 to 28
transport states per evaluation. Campaign 1 used the fixed
14 state default of the evaluator. From Campaign 3 onward the
schedule terminates at the discharge limit inside the loop.

\subsubsection{Radial peaking factor}

The peaking objective of Equation~\eqref{eq:obj_fdh} uses the radial
peaking factor of Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_definition}, formally defined
in Section~\ref{sec:bg-peaking}. That definition leaves two
methodological choices open, and both are fixed here: the numerical
estimator and the evaluation domain.

\paragraph{Numerical estimator.}
The integrals of Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_definition} are replaced by
their Monte Carlo counterparts. A pin-wise fission energy deposition
tally is accumulated over the active fuel region, so each rod
contributes one volume-integrated score.

\begin{equation}
\widehat{F}_{\Delta H}
=
\frac{\displaystyle \max_{i \in \{1,\dots,N\}} \widehat{P}_i}
     {\displaystyle \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} \widehat{P}_j}
\label{eq:fdh_estimator}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\widehat{F}_{\Delta H}$ is the Monte Carlo estimate of the
        radial peaking factor, dimensionless.

  \item $\widehat{P}_i$ is the tallied fission energy deposition rate
        of fuel rod $i$ over the full active height, in W.

  \item $N$ is the number of fuel rods in the evaluation domain,
        dimensionless.

  \item $i$ and $j$ are fuel rod indices, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

Two properties of this estimator govern the selection methodology.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The estimate carries a statistical uncertainty inherited from
        the finite number of simulated histories. Differences between
        candidate designs smaller than that uncertainty cannot be
        resolved by a single evaluation.

  \item The estimate is positively biased. The numerator is a maximum
        over $N$ noisy scores, so fluctuation alone raises the sampled
        maximum above the true maximum, and the bias grows with $N$ and
        with the tally variance.
\end{enumerate}

Both properties are quantified in Section~\ref{sec:meth-stats}, which
also develops the two-stage subset-selection procedure that separates
true optima from noise-driven apparent optima.

\paragraph{Evaluation domain.}
The tally domain of Equation~\eqref{eq:fdh_estimator} determines what
the objective represents. Two levels are used in this work.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Assembly level, in which $N$ covers the 264 fuel rods of a
        single reflected assembly. This level is inexpensive and served
        as the screening objective in Campaigns 1 to 3.

  \item Core level, in which $N$ covers the fuel rods of the full
        32-assembly core at beginning of life. This level supplies both
        the objective and the peaking constraint from Campaign 4
        onward.
\end{enumerate}

The validation programme that motivated the move to core level is
presented in Section~\ref{sec:meth-core-objective}, and its
measurements in Section~\ref{sec:res-corevalidation}.

\subsubsection{Normalisation of the constraint violation}
\label{subsubsec:cv-norm}

The five constraints of the problem are expressed in four different physical
units. The reactivity window acts on the multiplication factor, the enrichment
cap on a weight percentage, the peaking limit on a dimensionless power ratio,
and the vessel-fit condition on a length in centimetres.

The constraint handling of the NSGA-II (Non-dominated Sorting Genetic
Algorithm II) implementation ranks infeasible candidates by the total
constraint violation. When the population contains no feasible member, which
is the situation throughout Campaigns 4 and 5, this ranking alone drives the
search. Summing raw violations across heterogeneous units therefore weights
the constraints by their units rather than by their engineering importance. A
violation of one centimetre in the vessel-fit condition would outweigh a
violation of several thousand pcm in the reactivity window.

From Campaign 6 onward, each constraint is normalised by its own limit before it
enters the optimiser. The normalised violation is defined below.

\begin{equation}
  V = \sum_{j=1}^{n_g} \max\!\left(0, \frac{g_j}{s_j}\right)
  \label{eq:cv-norm}
\end{equation}

The terms of Equation~\eqref{eq:cv-norm} have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $V$ is the total constraint violation used to rank infeasible
    candidates, dimensionless.
  \item $g_j$ is the raw value of constraint $j$ in its native unit, with
    $g_j \leq 0$ denoting feasibility.
  \item $s_j$ is the normalisation scale of constraint $j$, taken equal to the
    limit of that constraint, in the same unit as $g_j$.
  \item $n_g$ is the number of inequality constraints, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

The scales adopted are $s = 1.02$ for the lower reactivity bound, $s = 1.35$
for the upper reactivity bound, $s = 19.75$ for the enrichment cap, $s = 2.0$
for the peaking limit, and $s = 90.0$~cm for the vessel-fit condition. Each
normalised violation is then the fraction of its own limit.

Three properties of this change are noted.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The feasible set is unchanged, because the scales are positive and the
    sign of every $g_j$ is preserved.
  \item The objectives and the hypervolume history are unchanged, because the
    normalisation acts only on the constraint matrix.
  \item Archived evaluations from earlier campaigns remain valid, because the
    physical constraint values are stored unscaled and the normalisation is
    applied when the archive is loaded.
\end{enumerate}

The active scales are written into the checkpoint metadata at launch,
and a resumed campaign refuses to start if its constraint set differs
from the stored one, so the normalisation cannot change silently
between blocks.

\section{Leakage-corrected reactivity target (Route B)}
\label{sec:meth-ktarget}

End of cycle on the reflective assembly is defined at the
leakage-corrected target introduced qualitatively in
Section~\ref{sec:bg-k}.

\begin{equation}
k_\mathrm{target}(p, t_\mathrm{refl})
= \frac{k_\infty^\mathrm{assembly}}{k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}}
\label{eq:ktarget}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $k_\mathrm{target}$ is the leakage-corrected end-of-cycle
        target entering Equation~\eqref{eq:eoc-k}, dimensionless.

  \item $k_\infty^\mathrm{assembly}$ is the infinite multiplication
        factor of the reflective single assembly, dimensionless.

  \item $k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}$ is the effective multiplication
        factor of the 32-assembly core built with the same pitch and
        reflector thickness, dimensionless.

  \item $p$ is the pin pitch, in cm.

  \item $t_\mathrm{refl}$ is the reflector thickness, in cm.
\end{itemize}

The ratio is pre-computed on a $3\times7$ grid over pitch and reflector
thickness and bilinearly interpolated inside the loop.

Two constructions could carry the leakage correction, and they are
mutually exclusive because using both would double-count the reflector
leakage.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Route A, a reflected-assembly sleeve model.

  \item Route B, the pre-computed target of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:ktarget}.
\end{enumerate}

Route B is adopted. The construction is validated a posteriori on the
finalists in Section~\ref{sec:res-corevalidation}.

\section{Surrogate-assisted active-learning loop}
\label{sec:meth-loop}

The campaign follows the dual-stage
pattern~\cite{whyte_parks_2021}, whose four steps
repeat until the stopping rule is met.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A Latin-hypercube design of
        experiments~\cite{mckay_lhs_1979} seeds the archive, and each
        subsequent iteration adds a small infill batch evaluated with
        OpenMC.

  \item A Gaussian-process surrogate with automatic relevance
        determination~\cite{rasmussen_gp_2006} is trained on the whole
        cumulative archive.

  \item NSGA-II~\cite{deb_nsga2_2002} searches the surrogate in place
        of the expensive code.

  \item The most informative proposals form the next infill batch.
\end{enumerate}

Blocks of three iterations are separated by analysis. The empirical
stopping rule has two conditions, both required.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The hypervolume gain stays below 1\% for three consecutive
        iterations.

  \item No censored design remains on the Pareto front, so the front is
        fully resolved.
\end{enumerate}

The stopping rule is a necessary condition and not a sufficient one.
Campaign 6 demonstrates the distinction experimentally. The rule fired
after three iterations whose acquisition was defective and whose
hypervolume gain was exactly zero, and it fired again, this time
correctly, after the acquisition had been repaired and the gains had
decayed monotonically below the threshold. The two firings are
presented side by side in Section~\ref{sec:res-c6-margin}.

Two per-iteration health indicators are therefore recorded alongside
the hypervolume and inspected before the rule is trusted.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The number of candidates that pass the feasibility margin of
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-acq}, which measures whether the
        constraint surrogate is still learning.

  \item The depth of the diversity relaxation cascade, which measures
        whether the surrogate front still contains materially new
        designs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Acquisition function}
\label{sec:meth-acq}

The infill batch is drawn from the non-dominated set of the final
NSGA-II population on the surrogate. The base criterion ranks these
candidates by their aggregated predictive uncertainty.

\begin{equation}
a_i \;=\; \sum_{m=1}^{2} \frac{\sigma_{im}}{\max_{l}\,\sigma_{lm}}
\label{eq:acq-score}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $a_i$ is the acquisition score of candidate $i$,
        dimensionless.

  \item $\sigma_{im}$ is the Gaussian-process posterior standard
        deviation of objective $m$ at candidate $i$, in the unit of
        that objective.

  \item $\max_{l}\,\sigma_{lm}$ is the largest posterior standard
        deviation of objective $m$ over the candidate set, in the same
        unit, so that each objective contributes on a common scale.
\end{itemize}

This is a pure exploration criterion in the sense of
\cite{forrester_book_2008}. Campaign 6 exposed three distinct failure
modes of the bare criterion, and each motivated one revision. The
revisions were introduced one per block, so every behavioural change is
attributable, and the measurements that motivated them are reported in
Section~\ref{sec:res-c6-defects}.

\paragraph{Ceiling-aware surrogate objective.}
The truth evaluator censors every cycle length at the depletion
ceiling, but a Gaussian process trained on the archive extrapolates
through it, and the search then concentrates on predictions that the
evaluator cannot return. The surrogate objective is therefore clipped
at the ceiling before it enters the search.

\begin{equation}
\tilde{f}_1(\mathbf{x}) \;=\;
\max\!\left( f_1(\mathbf{x}),\; -E_\mathrm{cap} \right),
\qquad
E_\mathrm{cap} \;=\; \frac{1000\, B_\mathrm{max}}{P_\mathrm{spec}}
\label{eq:efpd-clip}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\tilde{f}_1$ is the clipped first objective in minimisation
        form, in d, equal to the negative predicted cycle length.

  \item $f_1$ is the raw Gaussian-process prediction of the first
        objective, in d.

  \item $E_\mathrm{cap}$ is the depletion ceiling expressed as a cycle
        length, in d, equal to \num{10016.7} at the Campaign 6 ceiling.

  \item $B_\mathrm{max}$ is the depletion ceiling, in MWd/kgHM.

  \item $P_\mathrm{spec}$ is the specific power of
        Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}, in W/gHM.
\end{itemize}

On the resulting plateau the first objective is constant, so
non-dominated sorting ranks the plateau by peaking alone, which is
exactly how constrained dominance ranks censored truth designs. The cap
is derived at launch from the declared ceiling and the model specific
power, and is recorded in the checkpoint metadata.

\paragraph{Batch diversity.}
Ranking a smooth uncertainty field returns neighbours, because the $k$
highest values of a continuous function are adjacent. A minimum
separation is therefore enforced greedily down the ranking, between
each pick and the archive and between the picks themselves.

\begin{equation}
d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}) \;=\;
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_x}}
\left\lVert \frac{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}}
{\mathbf{x}_u-\mathbf{x}_l} \right\rVert_2
\;\ge\; \delta_\mathrm{min}
\label{eq:batch-div}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$ is the normalised separation of two
        designs, dimensionless.

  \item $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are two design vectors, in the
        native units of Table~\ref{tab:design-space}.

  \item $\mathbf{x}_l$ and $\mathbf{x}_u$ are the lower and upper
        bounds of the design box, in the same units, applied
        componentwise.

  \item $n_x$ is the number of design variables, dimensionless, so
        that $\delta_\mathrm{min}$ reads as a mean per-variable
        fraction of range.

  \item $\delta_\mathrm{min}$ is the minimum separation,
        dimensionless. Campaign 6 used 0.10 in block 3 and block 4 and
        0.14 in block 5.
\end{itemize}

If the candidate set cannot supply the full batch at
$\delta_\mathrm{min}$, the threshold is halved and the scan repeats,
so a small front still fills the batch with the best diversity it can
offer. Only when the candidates are exhausted entirely is the batch
completed with space-filling random designs. The depth of this
relaxation cascade is the second health indicator of the stopping
rule.

\paragraph{Feasibility margin.}
The bare criterion never consults the constraint surrogate, and the
Gaussian-process mean is optimistic exactly where the archive is thin.
Every candidate is therefore scored on the constraint surrogate with an
uncertainty margin before the uncertainty ranking is applied.

\begin{equation}
s_i \;=\; \max_{j \,\in\, \mathcal{G}_\mathrm{GP}}
\left( \mu_{ij} + \kappa\,\sigma_{ij} \right)
\label{eq:feas-margin}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $s_i$ is the margin score of candidate $i$, dimensionless in
        the normalised constraint units of
        Equation~\eqref{eq:cv-norm}, margin-feasible when
        non-positive.

  \item $\mathcal{G}_\mathrm{GP}$ is the set of surrogate-predicted
        constraints. Constraints computed analytically are excluded,
        because the search population satisfies them exactly and their
        surrogate uncertainty is meaningless.

  \item $\mu_{ij}$ is the Gaussian-process mean of constraint $j$ at
        candidate $i$, dimensionless.

  \item $\sigma_{ij}$ is the corresponding posterior standard
        deviation, dimensionless.

  \item $\kappa$ is the margin multiplier, dimensionless, set to 1.5
        in Campaign 6.
\end{itemize}

Margin-feasible candidates rank first, ordered by
Equation~\eqref{eq:acq-score} as before. The remaining candidates are
not discarded. They follow, ordered by ascending $s_i$, so a front with
too few margin-feasible members still fills the batch with the least
risky picks and the batch never degenerates. Where the surrogate is
confident the margin is small, and where it extrapolates the margin is
large, which is the discrimination the bare criterion lacked. The
construction is the deterministic counterpart of the probability of
feasibility weighting of constrained surrogate
optimisation~\cite{forrester_book_2008}, and setting $\kappa = 0$
restores the bare criterion exactly.

  \item The seed-to-seed standard deviation at the historical setting

\section{ATF discharge-burnup limit}
\label{sec:meth-atf}

The discharge limit adopted is $B_\mathrm{ATF} = 75$\,MWd/kgHM.

Cycle length and burnup are linearly related through the constant
specific power, Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd}, with $P_\mathrm{spec}$ given
in Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}. The burnup limit therefore maps
one-to-one onto a cycle-length cap.

Campaign 2 imposed the limit retrospectively, as an exact
post-processing transform of the completed campaign. Campaigns 3 and 4
terminate depletion at $B_\mathrm{ATF}$ inside the loop, so censoring
is eliminated by construction. Hypervolume histories reset whenever the
objective definition changes.

Campaign 6 raises the depletion ceiling from 75 to
\SI{100}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram}. The two numbers play different
roles and the distinction is maintained throughout.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $B_\mathrm{ATF} = \SI{75}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram}$
        remains the discharge-qualification screen, applied at
        candidate selection in
        Section~\ref{sec:res-c6-bscreen}.

  \item $B_\mathrm{max} = \SI{100}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram}$ is a
        numerical depletion horizon whose purpose is to keep the
        Pareto front resolvable. At a ceiling of 75 a substantial part
        of the interesting design space terminates at the ceiling
        rather than at the reactivity target, the censored designs
        distort the objective surrogate, and the front cannot satisfy
        the second condition of the stopping rule.
\end{enumerate}

The surrogate is coupled to the ceiling through the clip of
Equation~\eqref{eq:efpd-clip}, so the search cannot spend evaluations
on predictions beyond the horizon. Under this arrangement exactly one
of the 114 Campaign 6 evaluations censored at the ceiling, and that
design is infeasible on the reactivity window in any case.

The internal consistency of the ceiling constant is checked against
the archives. The censoring value is \num{10016.7} effective full
power days at \SI{100}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram} in Campaigns 2
and 6 and \num{7512.5} at \SI{75}{\mega\watt\day\per\kilo\gram} in
Campaigns 3 to 5, and the two are in the exact ratio of the ceilings,
as required by the fixed heavy-metal inventory.

\section{Statistical treatment of the peaking estimator}
\label{sec:meth-stats}

$F_{\Delta H}$ is the maximum over $N$ noisy mesh cells, with $N = 264$
at assembly level. It is therefore an extreme order statistic, noisy
\emph{and} biased upward, as anticipated in Section~\ref{sec:bg-mc}.
The bias is largest for flat power distributions, which are exactly the
designs a peaking-minimising optimiser prefers.

Final selection therefore uses a two-stage subset selection over a
\emph{noise-margin} candidate set. The candidate set contains every
feasible design within $k\,\sigma_\mathrm{seed}$ of the observed
minimum, where $\sigma_\mathrm{seed}$ is the seed-to-seed standard
deviation of the estimator and $k = 4$--$6$ is the margin multiplier.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Gupta-style screening~\cite{gupta_1965} retains every candidate
        whose confidence interval overlaps the incumbent's.

  \item Welch tests with Bonferroni correction, at confirmation
        fidelity, resolve the retained candidates pairwise.
\end{enumerate}

Values adjusted across fidelities carry the measured inter-fidelity
shift with its spread propagated. They are flagged as adjusted wherever
reported.

\section{Proxy validation and the core-level objective (Campaign 4)}
\label{sec:meth-core-objective}

Optimising a reduced-order proxy requires validating the proxy's
\emph{rank fidelity} to the true objective, not only its statistical
convergence. Three instruments implement this.

\paragraph{Finalist validation.}
Each Pareto finalist is rebuilt as the full 32-assembly core and solved
at BOL with a pin-resolved mesh over the whole footprint,
seed-replicated. The ratio
$F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}/F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}$ is the proxy
gap. The same run yields $k_\mathrm{eff}^\mathrm{core}$, closing
Route~B on the finalists at no additional cost.

\paragraph{Archive rescoring.}
Every feasible archive design is rescored at core level in two stages,
a 3-seed screen followed by an 8-seed resolution of the noise-margin
top. This gives the Spearman rank correlation between proxy and truth
and a direct answer to whether the proxy discarded superior designs.

\paragraph{Bridge model.}
The multiplicative discrepancy is fitted in the Kennedy--O'Hagan
spirit~\cite{kennedy_ohagan_2000,forrester_2007}.

\begin{equation}
\frac{F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}}{F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}}
\approx a + b\,t_\mathrm{refl} + c\,p
\label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{core}$ and $F_{\Delta H}^\mathrm{asm}$
        are the peaking factors of the same design at core and assembly
        level, dimensionless.

  \item $a$ is the fitted intercept, dimensionless.

  \item $b$ is the fitted coefficient of the reflector thickness, in
        cm$^{-1}$.

  \item $c$ is the fitted coefficient of the pin pitch, in cm$^{-1}$.

  \item $t_\mathrm{refl}$ is the reflector thickness, in cm.

  \item $p$ is the pin pitch, in cm.
\end{itemize}

The purpose of the fit is to quantify how much of the gap a
low-dimensional correction could carry. The fitted values on the
Campaign-3 archive are given in Equation~\eqref{eq:bridge-fit}.

The Campaign-4 decision follows from cost. The core BOL solve costs
$\sim$104\,s against a $\sim$110\,min evaluation.

\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta t}{t} \approx \frac{113 - 110}{110} \approx 3\%
\label{eq:corecost}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Delta t$ is the measured wall-time increment of one
        evaluation when the core BOL solve is added, in min.

  \item $t$ is the measured wall time of one evaluation without the
        core solve, in min.

  \item $113$ and $110$ are the measured wall times with and without
        the core solve, in min.
\end{itemize}

At this cost the true quantity is measured \emph{inside the loop}
rather than corrected. $F_{\Delta H}$ and $g_\mathrm{peak}$ are
evaluated on the core, the assembly value is retained as a diagnostic,
and EFPD keeps the validated assembly plus Route-B evaluation. This is
the deletion of the proxy where deletion is affordable, preferred over
co-kriging or trust-region model
management~\cite{alexandrov_2001,poloczek_miso_2017,kandasamy_boca_2017}
on simplicity grounds.

\section{Evaluator-level radial zoning and the enrichment search box}
\label{sec:meth-zoning}

From Campaign 6 the beginning-of-life core map is zoned radially inside
the evaluator, so the peaking objective and the peaking constraint act
on the zoned core rather than on a uniform loading.

The 32-assembly map is partitioned into three concentric rings of 4
central, 12 middle and 16 peripheral assemblies. The enrichment of each
ring is the corresponding design enrichment scaled by a fixed ring
multiplier.

\begin{equation}
e_r \;=\; m_r \, e, \qquad r \in \{C, M, P\}
\label{eq:zoning}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $e_r$ is the as-loaded enrichment of ring $r$, in
        wt\%~$^{235}$U.

  \item $e$ is the design enrichment assigned to that ring, in
        wt\%~$^{235}$U.

  \item $m_r$ is the fixed multiplier of ring $r$, dimensionless, with
        $m_C = 0.720$ for the centre, $m_M = 0.8933$ for the middle
        ring and $m_P = 1.15$ for the periphery.
\end{itemize}

%% e_in and e_out are assigned to the three rings before the
%% multipliers are applied, matching the evaluator implementation.

The centre-suppressed, periphery-enhanced pattern flattens the radial
power distribution, and it is the physically real driver of the
peaking improvement observed in Campaign 6, since neither of the two
bookkeeping changes of that campaign acts on a beginning-of-life
quantity. The attribution argument is completed in
Section~\ref{sec:res-c6-compare}.

The multipliers derive from a dedicated zoned-loading study in which
four independently obtained champion designs selected the same map.
That study fixed the peripheral multiplier at 1.250, and Campaign 6
relaxes it to 1.15. The relaxation trades a fraction of the flattening
for a wider enrichment search box, because the low-enriched-uranium cap
now binds through the zoned periphery rather than through the design
variable directly.

\begin{equation}
g_\mathrm{enr} \;=\; \max_r \left( m_r\, e \right) - 19.75 \;\le\; 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
e \;\le\; \frac{19.75}{m_P} \;=\; 17.174
\label{eq:leu-box}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $g_\mathrm{enr}$ is the enrichment-cap constraint of
        Table~\ref{tab:constraints}, in wt\%~$^{235}$U, redefined on
        the zoned map from Campaign 6.

  \item $19.75$ is the low-enriched-uranium cap, in wt\%~$^{235}$U.

  \item $17.174$ is the resulting upper bound of both enrichment
        design variables in Campaign 6, in wt\%~$^{235}$U.
\end{itemize}

The box bound and the constraint are therefore consistent by
construction, and no design of experiments point can violate the cap
after zoning, which removes the wasted samples observed under the
earlier formulation.

\section{Soluble boron treatment}
\label{sec:meth-boron}

The coolant carries a constant concentration of \SI{1000}{ppm} of
natural boron in every campaign of this work. The value is taken from
the operating conditions of the NuScale-like neutronics benchmark
defined by Fridman, Bilodid and
Valtavirta~\cite{fridman_nuscale_benchmark_2023}, together with the
uniform fuel temperature of \SI{900}{\kelvin} and the uniform coolant
and structure temperature of \SI{600}{\kelvin} adopted in
Table~\ref{tab:model-operating}. The concentration is a
fixed modelling parameter and is not exposed to the optimiser as a
design variable.

The same benchmark specification fixes the assembly geometry inherited
by the present model, namely a $17 \times 17$ lattice with 264 fuel
rods, 24 guide tubes and one central instrument tube, an active height
of \SI{200}{\centi\metre}, and a heavy reflector enclosed by a
stainless steel core barrel. Adopting the benchmark boron concentration
therefore keeps the coolant composition consistent with the geometry
and material specification from which the model derives, rather than
introducing an independent assumption.

The benchmark is a static Beginning of Life (BOL) specification and
does not prescribe a depletion history. The extension of the benchmark
conditions to a depleted core is an assumption of the present work and
is treated as such in Section~\ref{sec:meth-boron-limits}.

\subsection{Basis for the choice}
\label{sec:meth-boron-basis}

Soluble boron is the reactivity-control method of the boron-controlled
Pressurized Water Reactor (PWR) lineage from which the present core
inherits its assembly geometry, and it is retained by the majority of
land-based light-water Small Modular Reactor (SMR) concepts now under
development. Four considerations support retaining it here.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Traceability of the reference conditions.} The
        NuScale-like benchmark provides a published specification, a
        Monte Carlo reference solution obtained with
        Serpent~\cite{fridman_nuscale_benchmark_2023}, and an
        independent reproduction with the same transport code and
        nuclear data library used in this work, namely OpenMC with
        ENDF/B-VII.1~\cite{ez_aldeen_2025}. That
        reproduction reports agreement within 16 to \SI{31}{pcm} in the
        effective multiplication factor and a root mean square relative
        difference below \SI{0.11}{\percent} in assembly radial power.
        Equivalent open documentation does not exist for the
        LABGENE-class naval core that motivates the study, and no
        soluble boron specification for that core is available in the
        public literature.
  \item \textbf{Containment of the design space.} Soluble boron and the
        two absorber-related design variables, the gadolinium oxide
        (\ce{Gd2O3}) weight fraction and the gadolinium-bearing pin
        count, act on the same physical quantity, namely the excess
        reactivity available at beginning of life. Allowing boron to
        vary would make the study partly a study of boron rather than
        of the fuel and lattice variables under investigation.
  \item \textbf{Scope of the safety demonstration.} A
        Soluble-Boron-Free (SBF) core transfers the entire
        excess-reactivity budget to burnable absorbers and control
        rods. A credible SBF design therefore requires a control rod
        bank layout, a Cold Shutdown Margin (CSDM) demonstration under
        the single-failure assumption, and an explicit Moderator
        Temperature Coefficient (MTC) verification, none of which lie
        within the objective and constraint set defined in
        Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}. Treating SBF operation as an
        assumption without that supporting analysis would weaken rather
        than strengthen the design basis.
  \item \textbf{Known direction of the bias.} As shown in
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-boron-bias}, the presence of soluble
        boron relaxes the reactivity constraint and shortens the
        computed cycle length relative to boron-free operation. The
        choice is therefore not a concealed relaxation of the
        optimisation problem in the objective that is being maximised.
\end{enumerate}

The sequence of verifying a model against a documented boron-controlled
specification before addressing boron-free operation is itself
established practice. Ez Aldeen et
al.~\cite{ez_aldeen_2025} adopt the boron-controlled
NuScale-like benchmark as the verification basis for a modelling
programme whose stated target is boron-free SMR cores.

Soluble-boron-free operation is nevertheless a recurring feature of
proposed compact and civil marine light-water designs in the open
literature. The reported motivations are a more negative Moderator
Temperature Coefficient (MTC), reduced primary-circuit corrosion, lower
liquid waste production, simplification of the Chemical and Volume
Control System (CVCS), and, for shipboard application, the concern that
seawater ingress following a hull breach could dilute borated coolant
and introduce a criticality
hazard~\cite{kusunoki_mrx_2000,alam_partI_2019,alzaben_boronfree_2019,ismr_sbf_2024,rr_smr_atf_2025,song_sanchez_2026}.
Reactivity-control practice in operating naval propulsion reactors is
not documented in publicly available sources, and no claim about it is
made here.

\subsection{Direction and magnitude of the bias}
\label{sec:meth-boron-bias}

Removing the soluble boron raises the beginning-of-life multiplication
factor. The reactivity worth of the boron inventory converts to a
change in multiplication factor through

\begin{equation}
  \Delta k = k_{0}\,k_{1}\,\Delta\rho ,
  \label{eq:boron-rho-to-k}
\end{equation}

where
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Delta k$ is the change in effective multiplication factor,
        dimensionless,
  \item $k_{0}$ is the multiplication factor with soluble boron
        present, dimensionless,
  \item $k_{1}$ is the multiplication factor with the boron removed,
        dimensionless,
  \item $\Delta\rho$ is the integral reactivity worth of the removed
        boron, expressed in units of $\Delta k / k$.
\end{itemize}

For the beginning-of-life values encountered in this work the product
$k_{0}k_{1}$ is close to $2$, so a boron worth of one unit of
reactivity appears as approximately two units of multiplication factor.
The integral worth itself depends on the moderator-to-fuel ratio and on
the competition for thermal neutrons with the gadolinium-bearing pins,
both of which vary across the design space, and it is therefore
measured rather than assumed in this work.

\openpoint{Insert the measured integral boron worth, in pcm and in
$\Delta k$, from the \SI{0}{ppm} re-depletion of the Campaign-5
finalists, and state the differential worth in pcm per ppm for
comparison with published PWR values.}

Two consequences follow, and both are recorded as documented biases
rather than corrected within the campaigns.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Reactivity constraint.} Boron-free operation tightens
        the beginning-of-life reactivity constraint $g_{k\max}$ and
        displaces the entire feasibility envelope. Boron-free operation
        is closer to the naval mission but makes the reactivity-control
        problem harder, not easier.
  \item \textbf{Cycle length.} An operating PWR dilutes the boron
        progressively toward zero over the cycle, which releases
        reactivity that is unavailable to the constant-concentration
        model used here. The computed cycle length in Effective Full
        Power Days (EFPD) is therefore a lower bound for designs that
        terminate below the burnup ceiling. The magnitude of the
        shortfall scales with the ratio of the boron worth at end of
        cycle to the reactivity decline rate, and is consequently
        design-dependent. The bias is positive for every design, so the
        ordering of the Pareto front along the cycle-length axis is
        expected to be preserved, but the front is stretched rather
        than translated.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Declared limitations}
\label{sec:meth-boron-limits}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The benchmark from which the concentration is taken is a static
        Beginning of Life (BOL) specification. Its extension to a
        depleted core is an assumption of the present work and is not
        supported by the benchmark itself.
  \item The concentration is held constant throughout depletion. No
        critical boron search is performed at any burnup step, so the
        depletion trajectory represents a reactivity-tracking model and
        not an operating history.
  \item The Moderator Temperature Coefficient (MTC) is not evaluated in
        this work. Soluble boron makes the MTC less negative than
        boron-free operation would, so the present choice is not
        conservative for that parameter, in contrast to the reactivity
        and cycle-length effects described in
        Section~\ref{sec:meth-boron-bias}.
  \item Boron depletion and \isotope[10]{B} burnout are not modelled,
        which is consistent with the constant-concentration assumption.
\end{enumerate}

Under the one-change rule stated in
Section~\ref{sec:meth-campaigns} the treatment is held fixed
through Campaign~4. The Campaign-5 design-basis decision is preceded by
a direct measurement, in which the selected finalists are re-depleted
at \SI{0}{ppm}, so that the choice rests on computed values rather than
on estimates.


\section{Diagnostics, environments, reproducibility}
\label{sec:meth-diagnostics}

Shannon-entropy source-convergence monitoring is attached to every
model. The reflective single assembly is source-converged from
batch~1. The finite core has a real transient, and one core case
converged only at batch~94, hence the generous 60--120 inactive batches
of the core solves, with a per-run sentinel recorded in the archive.

Campaigns 1 and 2 ran on a cloud instance (AWS EC2 c7a.8xlarge,
AMD EPYC 9R14, 32 physical cores without simultaneous
multithreading), inside a purpose-built Docker image that pins the
Conda environment. Campaigns 3 to 5 ran on a 32-core, 64-thread
workstation without administrative privileges, on the same pinned
environment, and Campaign 6 returned to the cloud instance under the
identical image. The reproducibility unit is the environment
definition committed to the repository. OpenMC 0.15.3 and pymoo
0.6.2 are identical across the two hosts, verified by smoke
execution and $k_\infty$ agreement.
