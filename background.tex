\chapter{Theoretical Background}
\label{ch:background}

This chapter assembles the minimum theory the thesis rests on. Standard
derivations are cited rather than
repeated~\cite{duderstadt_hamilton_1976,stacey_2018}, and the emphasis
falls on the points where theory shapes decisions taken later.

The material is ordered as the methodology uses it.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Neutron multiplication and its leakage hierarchy.
  \item Fuel depletion and the role of the burnable absorber.
  \item The power-peaking figure of merit.
  \item The Monte Carlo method and its statistical consequences.
  \item Reduced-order proxies and rank fidelity.
  \item The dual optimization process using:
  \begin{enumerate}
        \item Hypervolume;
        \item NSGA-II search
      \item Gaussian-process surrogates; and 
      \item Active-learning loop.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

This chapter also hosts the formal definitions of the quantities used
throughout the thesis. The choices that turn those definitions into
computable numbers, namely the estimator, the evaluation domain and the
end-of-cycle criterion, are methodological and are fixed in
Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}.

\section{Neutron multiplication, leakage and reactivity}
\label{sec:bg-k}

The multiplication factor is the ratio of neutron populations in
successive fission generations.

In an infinite system with homogeneous boundaries no neutron escapes,
and the neutron balance defines the infinite multiplication factor
$k_\infty$. In a finite system leakage subtracts from the balance, so
the observable quantity is the effective multiplication factor
$k_\mathrm{eff}$, which is always smaller than $k_\infty$ for the same
material composition.

Figure~\ref{fig:bg-k} shows this hierarchy measured on the thesis model
itself. Each geometric step opens an additional leakage path.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Pin cell under reflective boundaries, $k_\infty = 1.296$,
        dimensionless.

  \item Single assembly under reflective boundaries,
        $k_\infty = 1.288$, dimensionless. The difference from the pin
        cell arises only from intra-assembly structure, namely guide
        tubes and poisoned pins.

  \item Full 32-assembly core with reflector and vacuum outer boundary,
        $k_\mathrm{eff} = 1.221$, dimensionless. The reduction measures
        the radial and axial leakage of the finite system.
\end{itemize}

Reactivity expresses the departure from criticality in a form that adds
linearly for small perturbations.

\begin{equation}
\rho = \frac{k - 1}{k}
\label{eq:reactivity}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\rho$ is the reactivity, dimensionless, commonly reported in
        per cent mille (pcm), where $1~\mathrm{pcm} = 10^{-5}$.

  \item $k$ is the multiplication factor of the system under
        consideration, either $k_\infty$ or $k_\mathrm{eff}$,
        dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

Two competing requirements act on the beginning-of-life (BOL)
multiplication factor.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It must exceed unity by enough margin that the core stays
        critical through the intended depletion history.

  \item It must remain bounded above, so that the excess reactivity can
        be held down by the available burnable absorber and control-rod
        worth.
\end{enumerate}

Both requirements enter the optimisation as explicit inequality
constraints on $k_\mathrm{BOL}$. The numerical bounds and their
evaluation level are given in Table~\ref{tab:constraints}.

A further consequence follows from the choice of computational
workhorse. Because the depletion calculation is performed on the
\emph{assembly} under reflective boundaries, end of cycle cannot be
defined at $k_\infty = 1$. The finite core that the assembly represents
loses reactivity to leakage, so the end-of-cycle criterion must be
raised above unity by a leakage correction. That correction is the
target multiplication factor formally defined in
Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget}.

  \caption{The multiplication ladder of the thesis model at reference
  conditions. Each geometric step opens a leakage path. The

\section{Depletion, burnup and cycle length}
\label{sec:bg-depletion}

As fissile material is consumed and absorbing fission products
accumulate, the multiplication factor falls with burnup.

Burnup is the thermal energy released per unit mass of heavy metal
initially present, expressed in MWd/kgHM. It is the natural independent
variable of a depletion calculation because it is independent of the
power level at which the energy was produced.

The isotopic evolution obeys the coupled Bateman equations. They are
solved here by the OpenMC depletion module, with reaction rates updated
by transport solutions on an adaptive burnup schedule.

The assembly specific power is held constant in this model, so burnup
maps linearly onto irradiation time. Cycle length is therefore reported
in effective full-power days (EFPD), obtained from the end-of-cycle
burnup through the conversion below.

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{EFPD} = \frac{10^{3}\,B_\mathrm{EOC}}{P_\mathrm{spec}}
\label{eq:efpd}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathrm{EFPD}$ is the cycle length in effective full-power
        days, in days.

  \item $B_\mathrm{EOC}$ is the end-of-cycle burnup, in MWd/kgHM.

  \item $P_\mathrm{spec}$ is the specific power of the fuel, in
        W\,g$^{-1}$ of heavy metal, numerically equal to
        kW\,kg$^{-1}$ of heavy metal.

  \item $10^{3}$ converts megawatt-days into kilowatt-days, so that the
        ratio closes to days.
\end{itemize}

The criterion that fixes the end-of-cycle burnup $B_\mathrm{EOC}$ is a
methodological choice. The criterion used in this work, together with
the treatment of the discharge-burnup limit, is defined in
Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}.

Figure~\ref{fig:bg-dep} sketches the resulting life curve. Three
horizons structure the campaigns.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The reactivity-defined end of cycle, reached where the
        depleting multiplication factor crosses the leakage-corrected
        target of Section~\ref{sec:meth-ktarget}.

  \item The gadolinia hold-down and its subsequent burnout, which
        suppress the early part of the curve and then release the
        reactivity they were holding.

  \item The accident-tolerant-fuel (ATF) discharge-burnup limit, which
        may terminate irradiation before the reactivity-defined end of
        cycle is reached. This mechanism produced both the censoring
        bookkeeping of Campaign~2 and the objective saturation observed
        in Campaign~3.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Power peaking}
\label{sec:bg-peaking}

Thermal margins are governed not by the average power of the core but
by the power of its hottest fuel rod. The radial peaking factor
$F_{\Delta H}$ is the figure of merit used in this work for that
purpose.

The radial peaking factor is defined as the ratio between the axially
integrated linear power of the most heavily loaded fuel rod and the
average axially integrated linear power of all fuel rods in the
evaluation domain.

\begin{equation}
F_{\Delta H}
=
\frac{\displaystyle \max_{i \in \{1,\dots,N\}} \int_{0}^{H} q'_i(z)\,\mathrm{d}z}
     {\displaystyle \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} \int_{0}^{H} q'_j(z)\,\mathrm{d}z}
\label{eq:fdh_definition}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $F_{\Delta H}$ is the radial peaking factor, dimensionless,
        equal to unity for a perfectly flat radial power distribution.

  \item $q'_i(z)$ is the linear power density of fuel rod $i$ at axial
        elevation $z$, in W\,cm$^{-1}$.

  \item $z$ is the axial coordinate measured from the bottom of the
        active fuel region, in cm.

  \item $H$ is the active fuel height, in cm.

  \item $N$ is the number of fuel rods in the evaluation domain,
        dimensionless.

  \item $i$ and $j$ are fuel rod indices, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

The name of the factor follows from the coolant enthalpy rise along the
hot channel, which is proportional to the axially integrated power of
the adjacent rod. The factor is therefore a thermal-hydraulic margin
indicator expressed in purely neutronic terms.

The evaluation domain over which the maximum is taken, and the Monte
Carlo estimator that replaces the integrals in practice, are
methodological choices. Both are fixed in
Section~\ref{sec:objective_functions}.

Two structural features of the lattice shape the radial power
distribution and are visible in Figure~\ref{fig:bg-pin}.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Guide tubes displace fuel and add moderator locally, which
        raises the power of their immediate neighbours.

  \item Gadolinia-bearing pins depress their own power strongly and
        transfer that power to the pins around them.
\end{enumerate}

Two properties of the figure of merit matter for the chapters that
follow.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It is evaluated at beginning of life on fresh fuel, where
        enrichment zoning and absorber effects on the power
        distribution are strongest.

  \item It is a \emph{maximum over many estimated quantities}. Under
        Monte Carlo evaluation this makes it an extreme order
        statistic, with the statistical consequences developed in
        Section~\ref{sec:bg-mc}.
\end{enumerate}

  \caption{Synthetic pin-power map on the thesis lattice, illustrating

\section{Gadolinia burnable absorbers}
\label{sec:bg-gd}

Excess beginning-of-life reactivity must be held down without spending
control-rod worth.

Gadolinia (Gd$_2$O$_3$) mixed into selected fuel pins achieves this
through the extreme thermal absorption of $^{155}$Gd and $^{157}$Gd,
shown in Figure~\ref{fig:bg-xs}. Poisoned pellets are optically black
to thermal neutrons, so absorption proceeds from the pellet surface
inward. The \emph{duration} of the hold-down is therefore set by
geometry as much as by absorber content. Industrial practice exploits
this self-shielding by concentrating the poison in a limited number of
rods~\cite{gd_enriched_2021}.

Three consequences recur in the results of this thesis.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Reactivity authority scales with the total absorber inventory,
        that is, with the product of concentration and poisoned-pin
        count. A design space that fixes the pin pattern therefore
        bounds the achievable hold-down.

  \item The residual end-of-life reactivity penalty of natural
        gadolinia is small~\cite{ba_snf_2001}.

  \item The local power depression couples the absorber to the peaking
        objective in a way that a maximum-based estimator cannot
        register, because the absorber lowers the power of its own pin
        rather than that of the hottest pin.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{images/bg_thermal_xs.pdf}
  \caption{Thermal absorption cross sections at 2200\,m/s. The two odd
  gadolinium isotopes absorb two to five orders of magnitude more

\section{The Monte Carlo method and its statistics}
\label{sec:bg-mc}

OpenMC solves the transport eigenvalue problem by power iteration over
fission generations. A batch of neutron histories is tracked, the
fission sites it produces seed the next batch, and tallies accumulate
over the \emph{active} batches only, the earlier \emph{inactive}
batches being discarded while the fission source is still settling.

Source convergence is monitored through the Shannon entropy of the
fission-site distribution, which becomes stationary once the spatial
source has settled. This diagnostic is attached to every evaluation
from Campaign~3 onward, and its behaviour on the finite core is
reported in Section~\ref{sec:res-corevalidation}.

Every tallied quantity is a statistical estimate. Two properties of
those estimates drive the methodology of this thesis, and both are
illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:bg-mc}.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The standard deviation of a tally contracts with the inverse
        square root of the number of active histories. Panel~(a) shows
        this law anchored to the measured fidelity ladder of this work
        for $F_{\Delta H}$.

  \item An objective defined as the \emph{maximum} over many noisy
        cells is an extreme order statistic and is biased
        \emph{upward}, because the cell that wins is preferentially one
        that fluctuated high.
\end{enumerate}

The second property is the less familiar of the two and has three
practical consequences.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item At the per-cell noise level of the Campaign-2 fidelity, the
        estimated maximum over a nearly flat field of 264 cells sits
        far above the true maximum. Panel~(b) makes the mechanism
        visible.

  \item Averaging over independent random seeds narrows the
        distribution of the estimate but does not shift its centre. The
        bias is removed only by increasing the number of histories per
        evaluation.

  \item The bias grows as the true power field flattens, because more
        near-ties give more opportunities for an upward fluctuation to
        win. The estimator therefore penalises most heavily exactly the
        designs that a peaking-minimising optimiser should prefer.
\end{enumerate}

This mechanism is quantified on the real campaign in
Section~\ref{sec:res-c1c2} and is the origin of the ranking inversion
that motivates the two-stage selection methodology of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-stats}.

\section{Reduced-order proxies and rank fidelity}
\label{sec:bg-proxy}

An optimiser driven by a cheap proxy inherits the \emph{ordering} that
the proxy imposes on candidate designs, not its absolute accuracy. The
operative quantity is therefore a rank correlation rather than an error
norm.

Spearman's rank correlation coefficient is used for that purpose.

\begin{equation}
\rho_S = 1 - \frac{6 \sum_{i=1}^{n} d_i^{\,2}}{n\,(n^{2}-1)}
\label{eq:spearman}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\rho_S$ is Spearman's rank correlation coefficient,
        dimensionless, bounded between $-1$ and $+1$, equal to $+1$ for
        identical orderings. The subscript distinguishes it from
        reactivity, Equation~\eqref{eq:reactivity}.

  \item $d_i$ is the difference between the rank assigned to design $i$
        by the proxy and the rank assigned to the same design by the
        reference quantity, dimensionless.

  \item $n$ is the number of paired designs, dimensionless.

  \item $i$ is the design index, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

The weakness of a rank-based proxy is systematic rather than random.
Proxy-to-truth correlation degrades near the optimum, where candidate
designs crowd together in objective space and small structural errors
are enough to reorder them.

Two responses to this problem exist in the literature.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Fuse the fidelities statistically, through a learned
        discrepancy between the cheap and the expensive
        model~\cite{kennedy_ohagan_2000,forrester_2007}.

  \item Measure the expensive quantity directly, wherever its cost
        permits.
\end{enumerate}

Both are exercised in this thesis, in
Section~\ref{sec:meth-core-objective} and in
Section~\ref{sec:res-corevalidation}.

\section{Multi-objective optimisation}
\label{sec:bg-moo}

With two competing objectives no single optimum exists, and the outcome
of the optimisation is a set of mutually incomparable designs rather
than one design.

Design $a$ \emph{dominates} design $b$ if it is at least as good as $b$
in both objectives and strictly better in at least one of them. The
non-dominated subset of a design archive is its Pareto set, and the
corresponding attainment staircase in objective space is the Pareto
front, illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:bg-pareto}.

Progress of a front is summarised by a single scalar, the
\emph{hypervolume}. This is the measure of the region of objective
space that the front dominates with respect to a fixed reference point.
Its stagnation forms half of the empirical stopping rule of
Section~\ref{sec:meth-loop}.

The search engine used throughout is
NSGA-II~\cite{deb_nsga2_2002}, which evolves a population under
non-dominated sorting with crowding-distance selection. The first
mechanism drives the population towards the front and the second
spreads it along the front.

   \caption{Dominance, the Pareto front and hypervolume in the objective

\section{Gaussian-process surrogates}
\label{sec:bg-gp}

A Gaussian process (GP) places a prior over functions, specified
completely by a mean function and a covariance kernel. Conditioning on
the evaluated designs yields a posterior whose mean interpolates the
data and whose variance quantifies ignorance away from
it~\cite{rasmussen_gp_2006}. Figure~\ref{fig:bg-gp} illustrates both.

Two kernel features carry weight in this work.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item An \emph{automatic relevance determination} kernel learns one
        length-scale per design variable. A variable along which the
        response is flat is assigned a large length-scale, which is the
        property exploited by the variable-importance analysis.

  \item A \emph{white-noise} component absorbs the evaluation noise.
        Two properties of this component matter:
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item its assumption of independent and identically
                distributed noise across designs is violated by a
                shared random seed, which motivates the per-design
                seeding policy adopted in Campaign~3,
          \item its fitted magnitude is an upper bound on the true
                Monte Carlo noise, so the excess over the measured
                noise quantifies residual model misfit.
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Design of experiments and active learning}
\label{sec:bg-al}

The surrogate is only as good as its training set.

The initial design of experiments uses Latin hypercube
sampling~\cite{mckay_lhs_1979}, which stratifies every variable
individually and therefore fills a high-dimensional box far more evenly
than uniform random sampling at equal evaluation budget.

From there the campaign follows the dual-stage active-learning loop of
Figure~\ref{fig:bg-al}, whose four steps repeat until the stopping rule
is met.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The expensive code evaluates a batch of designs.
  \item The Gaussian process is retrained on the \emph{whole}
        cumulative archive rather than on the newest batch alone.
  \item NSGA-II searches the surrogate in place of the expensive code.
  \item A small infill batch of the resulting proposals is returned to
        the expensive code.
\end{enumerate}

The stopping rule is applied between blocks of iterations, and the
selected finalists are confirmed at higher fidelity. This separation
between a cheap search stage and an expensive confirmation stage is the
two-tier principle that recurs throughout the thesis.
