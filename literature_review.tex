\chapter{Literature Review}

\section{Small modular PWRs and naval-heritage cores}
\label{sec:smr-naval}

\subsection{The contemporary small modular reactor landscape}
\label{subsec:smr-landscape}

Small modular reactors have moved from concept to early deployment over the
period 2024 to 2026. The International Atomic Energy Agency lists 68 active
designs in the 2024 edition of its reference booklet, drawing on the September
2024 update of the Advanced Reactors Information System~\cite{iaea_smr_2024}.
The Nuclear Energy Agency identified 127 technologies worldwide in the third
edition of its dashboard and assessed 74 of them across six readiness
dimensions~\cite{nea_dashboard_2025}. Pressurised water reactors dominate the
most advanced cohort, and most are integral pressurised water reactors in which
the steam generators, core and pressuriser are enclosed within a single
pressure vessel.

The leading integral and compact PWR-type designs, with their design and
licensing status, are the following.

\begin{itemize}
  \item NuScale US460, for which the United States Nuclear Regulatory
    Commission issued a Standard Design Approval on 29 May 2025 for a
    six-module plant of approximately 462~MWe, each module rated
    250~MWth~\cite{nrc_us460_2025,fedreg_us460_2025}.
  \item Westinghouse AP300, a 300~MWe single-loop PWR derived from the AP1000,
    in pre-application review with the Nuclear Regulatory Commission and in the
    United Kingdom Generic Design Assessment~\cite{nrc_ap300}.
  \item Holtec SMR-300, a 300~MWe PWR whose construction permit application was
    accepted by the Nuclear Regulatory Commission on 13 February
    2026~\cite{holtec_smr300_2026}.
  \item Rolls-Royce SMR, a 470~MWe PWR selected in the United Kingdom
    small modular reactor competition in June 2025~\cite{rr_smr_2025}.
  \item The Korean innovative small modular reactor, which targets
    soluble-boron-free operation using gadolinia burnable absorbers, including
    isotopically enriched gadolinium~\cite{ismr_epjn_2026}.
  \item The Chinese ACP100, also called Linglong One, under construction at
    Changjiang, which completed primary-circuit cold functional tests on
    16 October 2025 and targets commercial operation in the first half of
    2026~\cite{acp100_wnn_2025}.
  \item The Argentine CAREM-25, an indigenous integral PWR under construction
    since 2014~\cite{carem_wnn}.
  \item The Russian KLT-40S, operating in the Akademik Lomonosov floating plant
    since 2020, and the RITM-200 family operating in the LK-60 nuclear
    icebreakers~\cite{wna_ships,ritm200n_nei_2024}.
  \item The French NUWARD programme, which reset its design toward proven
    technology in 2024~\cite{nuward_wnn_2019}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why small modular reactors matter now}
\label{subsec:why-now}

The macro-scale rationale for nuclear expansion is quantified by the International 
Atomic Energy Agency's annual capacity projections. Relative to a global operational 
capacity of 377~GW(e) at the end of 2024, the high-case projection reaches 992~GW(e) 
by 2050, while the low-case projection reaches 561~GW(e)~\cite{iaea_rds1_2025}. 
Across these scenarios, Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) are anticipated to play an 
increasingly prominent role, accounting for 24\% of the 676~GW(e) in gross new capacity 
additions under the high case and 5\% of the 320~GW(e) additions in the low 
case~\cite{iaea_rds1_2025}. The net capacity growth between reference years is expressed as:
%
\begin{equation}
  \Delta C = C_{2050} - C_{2024},
  \label{eq:capacity-growth}
\end{equation}
%
where $\Delta C$ represents the net change in installed electrical capacity in GW(e), 
$C_{2050}$ is the projected capacity in 2050, and $C_{2024} = 377\text{~GW(e)}$ denotes 
the operational baseline at year-end 2024.

This projected growth is underpinned by strong multilateral political and industrial 
commitments. At COP28 on 2 December 2023, 25 nations launched the Declaration to Triple 
Nuclear Energy by 2050 relative to 2020 levels~\cite{cop28_doe_2023}. Endorsements expanded 
to 31 nations at COP29 and reached 33 countries at COP30 in Bel{\'e}m with the accession of 
Rwanda and Senegal~\cite{wnn_triple_2025}. Crucially, this initiative has extended beyond 
intergovernmental pledges to include commitments from over 140 nuclear supply chain 
companies, major commercial energy consumers (such as data center operators), and 16 
international financial institutions~\cite{iaea_rds1_2025,wnn_triple_2025}.

Despite extensive global research and development, and more than 80 distinct SMR and 
microreactor designs currently documented by the IAEA, commercial deployment remains 
in its nascent First-of-a-Kind (FOAK) phase. To date, only two operational SMR designs 
have entered commercial service: Russia's floating KLT-40S pressurized water reactors and 
China's high-temperature gas-cooled pebble-bed HTR-PM demonstrator~\cite{worldnuclearreport_smr}. 
Early operational experience has highlighted the challenges of FOAK cost escalation and 
schedule delays, reinforcing that realizing the projected SMR market share depends heavily 
on achieving true factory modularization, licensing harmonization, and leveraging 
mature technological baselines, such as evolutionary integral PWR concepts derived 
from marine propulsion~\cite{ritm200n_nei_2024}.

\subsection{Naval-heritage cores in the open literature}
\label{subsec:naval-heritage}

Detailed core data for military naval reactors are classified in every state
that operates them. This review therefore restricts itself to open, official,
and peer-reviewed sources.

The naval-heritage argument is built not on classified military cores but on
the documented transition of naval reactor technology into civilian marine and
land-based applications. Four national lineages are treated in turn, followed
by the open academic literature on naval core design principles.

\paragraph{The Russian lineage.}
The line developed by JSC ``Afrikantov OKBM'' proceeds from the OK-150 and
OK-900 icebreaker reactors to the KLT-40 series, and then to the integral
RITM-200 family~\cite{wna_ships}.

Two members of this line are documented in sufficient detail to be useful here.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The KLT-40S, deployed in the floating nuclear power plant (FNPP)
    \emph{Akademik Lomonosov}, uses uranium dioxide (UO$_2$) dispersed in a
    silumin matrix at 18.6~wt\% uranium-235, a core of 121 fuel assemblies, and
    a refuelling cycle of 30 to 36 months~\cite{worldnuclearreport_smr}.
  \item The RITM-200N, the land-based member of the family, is rated 190~MWth
    and 55~MWe gross and uses uranium enriched below
    20~wt\%~\cite{ritm200n_nei_2024}.
\end{itemize}

The relevance of the RITM-200N to this work is that its fuel cassettes are
described by its designers as analogues of those used in the cores of the
RITM-200 and KLT-40S icebreakers~\cite{ritm200n_nei_2024}. The transfer of a
marine core to a land-based prototype is therefore an established practice and
not a device peculiar to the Brazilian programme.

\paragraph{The French lineage.}
The K15 reactor is rated at approximately 150~MWth with a core life of about ten
years. It powers French submarines and the aircraft carrier
\emph{Charles de Gaulle}, and it operates on low-enriched uranium
(LEU)~\cite{wna_ships}.

The K15 is the closest Western analogue to the present work in the sense that it
demonstrates a naval propulsion core operating below the 20~wt\% threshold that
separates low-enriched uranium (LEU) from high-enriched uranium (HEU).

\paragraph{The United States and United Kingdom lineage.}
Naval reactors of the United States and the United Kingdom use weapon-grade
highly enriched uranium (HEU) at approximately 93.5~wt\%
uranium-235~\cite{wna_ships}.

The feasibility of converting these cores to low-enriched uranium (LEU) at
19.75~wt\% has been examined in two open assessments.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The report of the JASON advisory panel, which assessed the technical
    obstacles to conversion~\cite{jason_2016}.
  \item The report of the Office of Naval Reactors to Congress, which assessed
    the same question from a programme perspective~\cite{onr_leu_2016}.
\end{itemize}

These two assessments establish 19.75~wt\% as the recognised ceiling for a
low-enriched naval core, which is the value adopted as the enrichment constraint
in this work.

\paragraph{The Brazilian lineage.}
The LABGENE prototype, developed by the Centro Tecnol\'ogico da Marinha em
S\~ao Paulo (CTMSP) at the Aramar Experimental Center in Iper\'o, S\~ao Paulo,
is a 48~MWth pressurised water reactor (PWR). It serves as the land-based
prototype for the reactor of the \'Alvaro Alberto
submarine~\cite{fapesp_labgene}.

The detailed core neutronics of LABGENE are not published in the open
literature. No enrichment, lattice pitch, assembly count, burnable poison
loading, or peaking factor for that core can be cited from any open source.

This absence of open core data is the reason the present work is framed as a
LABGENE-class study rather than as a study of LABGENE itself. The rated thermal
power of 48~MWth and the naval pressurised water reactor (PWR) architecture are
taken from the open record. Every remaining core parameter, namely enrichment,
lattice pitch, assembly count, gadolinia (Gd$_2$O$_3$) loading, poisoned-pin
count, and reflector thickness, is an independent modelling choice of this
thesis and carries no claim of correspondence with the actual prototype.

\paragraph{Open academic analyses.}
Two bodies of open academic work inform the design principles applied in this
thesis.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The thesis of Ippolito, which compared naval core designs at 7~wt\%,
    20~wt\%, and 97.3~wt\% enrichment and established the penalty in core

  \item The Cambridge series on civil marine small modular reactors (SMRs),

\end{itemize}

The differences collected in Table~\ref{tab:naval-vs-commercial} follow from a
small set of naval operational requirements. Their implications for core and
fuel design are the following.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Higher enrichment permits a compact core with a large energy reserve.
    The naval rationale is energy autonomy without frequent
    refuelling~\cite{ippolito_1990}.
  \item A long refuelling interval demands a large beginning-of-life excess
    reactivity and therefore a heavy burnable poison inventory. The naval
    rationale is operational availability at sea~\cite{alam_2019_hotchannel}.
  \item Reduced-boron or boron-free operation shifts the reactivity hold-down
    burden to gadolinia and to the control rods. The naval rationale is fast
    manoeuvring and simplified coolant chemistry~\cite{ismr_epjn_2026}.
  \item A heavy and self-shielded gadolinia loading flattens the reactivity
    swing over the long cycle. The naval rationale is criticality control over
    the whole core life~\cite{ismr_epjn_2026}.
  \item A high volumetric power density tightens the margin to the hot channel
    limit expressed by $F_{\Delta H}$. The naval rationale is compactness
    within the hull~\cite{alam_2019_hotchannel}.
  \item Qualification for inclination, motion and shock increases structural
    and thermal-hydraulic margins. The naval rationale is safe operation under
    sea states~\cite{ritm200n_nei_2024}.
  \item Fine reactivity control by control rods alone supports rapid load
    following, which is the central naval manoeuvring
    requirement~\cite{ismr_epjn_2026}.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Positioning of the LABGENE-class 48\texorpdfstring{\,}{ }MWth core}
\label{subsec:positioning}

The core studied in this thesis is a LABGENE-class 48~MWth pressurised water
reactor of naval heritage. Among designs with open documentation, the closest
operating analogues are the KLT-40S and the RITM-200 family, which share the
naval logic of a compact low-enriched-uranium core with a long refuelling
interval~\cite{worldnuclearreport_smr,ritm200n_nei_2024}. The optimisation
objectives of cycle length and the radial enthalpy-rise hot channel factor
$F_{\Delta H}$ map directly onto the naval requirements of long core life and
compact high-power-density operation. The design variables of enrichment,
gadolinia concentration and poisoned-pin count, pin pitch, and reflector
thickness, together with the constraint on beginning-of-life $k_{\mathrm{eff}}$
at 1000~ppm soluble boron, express the reduced-boron and heavy-burnable-poison
strategy characteristic of long-life naval-heritage cores.

\section{Optimisation of nuclear reactor cores: development and state
of the art}
\label{sec:lit-opt}

The heritage designs of Section~\ref{sec:smr-naval} fix the physical
frame of the present work. This section reviews how such cores are
optimised. In-core fuel management and core design optimisation select
enrichments, burnable absorber loadings, geometric parameters and fuel
placement so that energy extraction is maximised while safety limits
are respected. The centrality of this activity to safe and economic
operation is reflected in dedicated validation programmes of the
International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) for the associated code
packages~\cite{iaea_tecdoc_815}.

The field has six decades of history and its methods track the wider
history of engineering optimisation. The review proceeds in three
parts.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The chronological development of the methods, from mathematical
        programming to machine learning
        (Sections~\ref{sec:lit-opt-early} to
        \ref{sec:lit-opt-surrogate}).

  \item The structural difficulties of the field, above all the
        difficulty introduced by burnup
        (Sections~\ref{sec:lit-opt-difficulties}
        and \ref{sec:lit-opt-burnup}).

  \item The current state of the art, with the strengths and
        limitations of each family of methods and the positioning of
        the present work (Section~\ref{sec:lit-opt-sota}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mathematical programming and the Haling principle}
\label{sec:lit-opt-early}

The first formal optimisation of nuclear fuel management is generally
credited to Wall and Fenech, who cast the multi-cycle refuelling of a
PWR as a dynamic programming problem~\cite{wall_fenech_1965}.
Mathematical programming formulations followed within a few years.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Linear and quadratic programming for fuel recycle
        scheduling~\cite{tabak_1968}.

  \item Linear programming for the refuelling of light water
        reactors~\cite{suzuki_kiyose_1971}.

  \item Accelerated exhaustive search for boiling water reactor fuel
        management~\cite{stover_sesonske_1969}.

  \item Optimal control formulations of the depletion problem
        itself~\cite{wade_terney_1971}.
\end{enumerate}

The most influential idea of the era is the Haling
principle~\cite{haling_1964}. Haling observed that if the power
distribution keeps a constant shape throughout the cycle, that shape
minimises the peak power over the cycle, and the end-of-cycle state can
be computed in a single depletion pass. The principle became the
standard shortcut of the period precisely because it removed the need
to march every candidate design through time. Its limitation is equally
well documented. Constant-shape operation is not consistent with
realistic control strategies and the principle can misjudge the thermal
margin~\cite{sun_1993}.

The deterministic era carried a structural weakness. Linearisation and
convexity assumptions sit uneasily with a design space that is
discrete, nonconvex and rich in local optima, a mismatch the field
recognised over the following decades.

\subsection{Heuristics, knowledge-based systems and perturbation
theory}
\label{sec:lit-opt-heuristic}

The 1980s produced two complementary responses to that mismatch. The
first was direct search steered by engineering rules, exemplified by
the automated generation of loading patterns that maximise
beginning-of-cycle reactivity under power peaking and burnup
constraints~\cite{hobson_turinsky_1986}. Knowledge-based and expert
systems later formalised the same designer heuristics symbolically, a
line of work reviewed by its own authors in~\cite{nissan_1997}.

The second response was generalised perturbation theory (GPT). GPT
estimates how the core eigenvalue and the power distribution respond to
a fuel shuffle without a full re-solution of the transport problem, and
it was demonstrated for three-dimensional design optimisation of Canada
Deuterium Uranium (CANDU) reactors~\cite{rozon_beaudet_1992}. GPT then
became the neutronics engine of the FORMOSA-P code, where a nodal GPT
model evaluates objectives and constraints cheaply enough to sit inside
a stochastic search, extended even to the feed enrichment as a decision
variable~\cite{maldonado_1995}.

\subsection{Stochastic global search}
\label{sec:lit-opt-stochastic}

The decisive methodological shift arrived at the turn of the 1990s,
when the field accepted nonconvexity and moved to stochastic global
search. Kropaczek and Turinsky introduced simulated annealing for PWR
in-core fuel management through FORMOSA-P (Fuel Optimization for
Reloads Multiple Objectives by Simulated Annealing,
PWR)~\cite{kropaczek_turinsky_1991}. The code minimises power peaking,
maximises end-of-cycle reactivity or maximises discharge burnup, with
design limits enforced by penalty functions or by rejection of
violating patterns~\cite{maldonado_1995}.

Genetic algorithms entered the field shortly after, building on the
general framework of~\cite{goldberg_1989}. The Cambridge school applied
them to PWR reload design~\cite{poon_parks_1992,poon_parks_1993}, and
parallel work in the United States combined genetic search with expert
knowledge~\cite{dechaine_feltus_1995,dechaine_feltus_1996}. Tabu search
followed~\cite{lin_1998}, and a quantitative comparison of the
competing heuristics closed the decade~\cite{yamamoto_1997}.

The landmark result of the era for the present work is the first
nondominated genetic search for PWR reload design~\cite{parks_1996}.
Instead of collapsing competing objectives into one weighted scalar,
the algorithm returns an entire Pareto front in a single run. It
anticipates the Pareto-based formulation that the Non-dominated Sorting
Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) later standardised~\cite{deb_nsga2_2002}
and that this thesis adopts.

\subsection{Population-based metaheuristics and multi-objective
evolutionary algorithms}
\label{sec:lit-opt-population}

The 2000s diversified the metaheuristic toolbox, with a sustained
Brazilian contribution from the COPPE/UFRJ school. Representative
results include the following.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Systematic investigations of genetic algorithms in core
        design~\cite{pereira_1999}.

  \item Coarse-grained parallel genetic algorithms for core design
        optimisation~\cite{pereira_lapa_2003}.

  \item The Ant-Q ant colony algorithm applied to the reload
        problem~\cite{machado_schirru_2002} and its extension to
        artificial ant colony connective networks~\cite{delima_2008}.
\end{enumerate}

Particle swarm optimisation, differential evolution, harmony search and
further population-based variants followed through the 2010s. A
comprehensive survey of multi-objective optimisation methods and their
nuclear applications covers this diversification~\cite{stewart_2021}.

On the multi-objective side, NSGA-II~\cite{deb_nsga2_2002} became the
workhorse of the field, supported by the broader theory of
multi-objective evolutionary algorithms
(MOEAs)~\cite{deb_2001,coello_2007}. The family remains active at the
frontier. A recent application optimises the core of a small modular
dual fluid reactor with NSGA-III under reactor physics
constraints~\cite{chen_2022}.

\subsection{Surrogate-assisted optimisation and machine learning}
\label{sec:lit-opt-surrogate}

The binding constraint of every method above is the number of calls to
the expensive simulator. Surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation
addresses it directly. A cheap statistical model is trained on
evaluated designs, the optimiser searches the model, and the simulator
is reserved for the most informative candidates~\cite{jin_2011}.
Gaussian process regression is the canonical surrogate because it
supplies a calibrated predictive uncertainty~\cite{rasmussen_gp_2006}.
The loop is initialised by space-filling designs such as Latin
hypercube sampling~\cite{mckay_lhs_1979} and driven by infill criteria
that balance exploration against
exploitation~\cite{forrester_book_2008,garnett_2023}. For
multi-objective problems the hypervolume indicator supplies both a
convergence measure and an acquisition
target~\cite{zitzler_thiele_1999}.

Nuclear applications of this pattern are recent and growing.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Neural network surrogates coupled to NSGA-II for PWR reload
        design~\cite{whyte_parks_2021}.

  \item A convolutional neural network surrogate combined with a
        genetic algorithm for reload pattern
        optimisation~\cite{wan_2022}.

  \item Neural network acceleration of a genetic algorithm for a
        coupled fast and thermal experiment whose design space exceeds
        $3\times10^{71}$ configurations~\cite{pevey_2022}.
\end{enumerate}

Deep reinforcement learning is the second frontier. Physics-informed
reinforcement learning for assembly design is reported by its authors
to find several times more feasible patterns than stochastic
optimisation at a markedly lower computational
cost~\cite{radaideh_2021_rl}, and neuroevolution has been applied to
large-scale boiling water reactor bundle
design~\cite{radaideh_2021_bwr}. The NEORL framework packages these
algorithms for energy applications~\cite{radaideh_2023_neorl}.
Systematic assessments followed~\cite{seurin_2024_rl}, and the Pareto
Envelope Augmented with Reinforcement Learning (PEARL) approach is
reported to reach a larger hypervolume than NSGA-III, multi-objective
simulated annealing and multi-objective tabu search on a full-size PWR
reload problem~\cite{seurin_2024_pearl}. These are results reported by
the developing groups and independent replication remains limited.

The open-source ecosystem completes the picture. OpenMC provides
community-developed Monte Carlo transport~\cite{romano_openmc_2015}
with an in-memory Python-coupled depletion
capability~\cite{romano_2021_depletion}, and pymoo provides NSGA-II and
its relatives in the same language~\cite{blank_deb_2020}. Together they
make reproducible surrogate-assisted core optimisation feasible on
modest computing budgets, the route taken by this thesis.

\subsection{Specific difficulties of the field}
\label{sec:lit-opt-difficulties}

Six structural features distinguish core optimisation from generic
engineering optimisation. Each one shaped the history above and each
one acts on the pipeline of this thesis.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Combinatorial size.} The number of candidate
        configurations grows factorially with the number of positions,
        and discrete choices coexist with continuous variables such as
        enrichment and dimensions. A recent design study reports more
        than $3\times10^{71}$ configurations for 150 positions and
        three candidate materials~\cite{pevey_2022}, and the survey
        literature treats the loading pattern problem as a canonical
        hard combinatorial problem~\cite{stewart_2021}.

  \item \emph{Expensive black-box evaluations.} Each candidate is
        scored by a reactor physics code, and a Monte Carlo depletion
        evaluation takes of the order of hours
        (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}). The affordable number of
        evaluations is therefore capped, which is the motivating
        premise of surrogate-assisted search~\cite{jin_2011}.

  \item \emph{Nonconvex and multimodal landscapes.} Objectives respond
        discontinuously to discrete changes and the landscape is rich
        in local optima, the mismatch that ended the deterministic era
        and justified stochastic
        search~\cite{kropaczek_turinsky_1991,parks_1996}.

  \item \emph{Competing objectives under licensing constraints.} Cycle
        length, peaking factors, discharge burnup and cost compete, and
        safety limits enter as hard constraints rather than as
        preferences~\cite{parks_1996,stewart_2021}.

  \item \emph{Stochastic objectives.} Monte Carlo simulators return
        objectives with statistical uncertainty. The noise perturbs the
        ranking of nearby designs and propagates through depletion
        steps, as developed in Section~\ref{sec:lit-opt-burnup}.

  \item \emph{The fidelity gap.} Cheap screening models rank designs
        differently from high-fidelity evaluation. The canonical
        statistical fusion of two fidelities is the autoregressive
        model of~\cite{kennedy_ohagan_2000}, with engineering practice
        in~\cite{forrester_2007} and provably convergent model
        management in~\cite{alexandrov_2001}. A recurring caution is
        that the cross-fidelity correlation weakens near the optimum,
        exactly where an optimiser converges. This thesis measures the
        corresponding rank inversion between assembly-level and
        core-level evaluation in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The burnup difficulty}
\label{sec:lit-opt-burnup}

Among these features, depletion is the one that defines the cost
structure of the problem, and it deserves a dedicated treatment because
it is the central methodological concern of this work.

A static figure of merit, such as the beginning-of-life (BOL) power
peaking, requires one transport solution per candidate. Cycle length,
end-of-cycle reactivity and discharge burnup are different in kind.
They depend on the whole time history of the core, so each candidate
requires a full depletion simulation and the cost of one evaluation is
multiplied by the number of burnup steps.

The object solved inside each such evaluation is the coupled transport
and depletion problem. The transport eigenvalue problem yields the
neutron flux for the current composition. The composition then evolves
according to the Bateman equations, written below in their standard
one-group form~\cite{duderstadt_hamilton_1976,stacey_2018}.

\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}N_i}{\mathrm{d}t} =
\sum_{j \neq i} \left( b_{j \rightarrow i}\,\lambda_j
  + \sigma_{j \rightarrow i}\,\phi \right) N_j
- \left( \lambda_i + \sigma_i^{\mathrm{a}}\,\phi \right) N_i
\label{eq:bateman}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $N_i$ and $N_j$ are the atom densities of nuclides $i$ and
        $j$, in atoms\,cm$^{-3}$.

  \item $t$ is the time, in s.

  \item $\lambda_i$ and $\lambda_j$ are the radioactive decay constants
        of nuclides $i$ and $j$, in s$^{-1}$.

  \item $b_{j \rightarrow i}$ is the branching fraction of the decay of
        nuclide $j$ into nuclide $i$, dimensionless.

  \item $\phi$ is the one-group scalar neutron flux, in
        neutrons\,cm$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$.

  \item $\sigma_{j \rightarrow i}$ is the effective one-group cross
        section for neutron-induced transmutation of nuclide $j$ into
        nuclide $i$, in cm$^{2}$, customarily quoted in barns.

  \item $\sigma_i^{\mathrm{a}}$ is the effective one-group removal
        cross section of nuclide $i$, covering absorption and the other
        loss reactions, in cm$^{2}$, customarily quoted in barns.
\end{itemize}

Collecting all nuclides into a vector gives the matrix form used by
modern depletion solvers~\cite{romano_2021_depletion}.

\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{n}}{\mathrm{d}t} =
\mathbf{A}(\phi)\,\mathbf{n}
\label{eq:bateman-matrix}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathbf{n}$ is the vector of nuclide atom densities, with
        components in atoms\,cm$^{-3}$.

  \item $\mathbf{A}(\phi)$ is the burnup matrix, assembled from the
        decay constants and the flux-weighted reaction rates, in
        s$^{-1}$.
\end{itemize}

The matrix depends on the flux and the flux depends on the composition
through the transport solution, so the coupled system is nonlinear even
though each substep is linear. Within a step the solution is a matrix
exponential, evaluated in practice by the Chebyshev Rational
Approximation Method (CRAM)~\cite{pusa_2011,pusa_2016}, the solver
implemented in the OpenMC depletion module used in this
work~\cite{romano_2021_depletion}.

Three consequences make burnup-aware optimisation qualitatively harder
than static optimisation.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Path dependence.} Two designs indistinguishable at BOL
        diverge along the cycle through spectral history, power history
        and the burn-off of the burnable absorber. Ranking designs on a
        BOL proxy therefore carries an intrinsic risk of misordering
        the end-of-cycle outcome.

  \item \emph{Time-dependent controls.} Soluble boron letdown and
        control rod sequencing change during the cycle, so realistic
        in-cycle operation is an optimal control problem in time rather
        than a static placement problem, as recognised from the
        earliest formulations~\cite{wade_terney_1971}.

  \item \emph{Cost multiplication.} Every burnup step of every
        candidate carries the full cost of a transport solution, so the
        evaluation time grows linearly with the number of burnup steps.
\end{enumerate}

The literature offers four strategies to keep this cost tractable.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Haling single pass~\cite{haling_1964}, cheap but
        approximate, with the thermal margin caveat noted
        above~\cite{sun_1993}.

  \item The linear reactivity model~\cite{driscoll_1990}, which
        exploits the near-linear decline of assembly reactivity with
        burnup to estimate cycle length and reload enrichment at
        negligible cost, without resolving the power distribution.

  \item BOL proxies and reduced-order depletion, which score designs on
        beginning-of-life quantities or on coarse burnup ladders. They
        are cheap, but the design ranking made at BOL need not survive
        to the end of cycle, the risk this thesis measures directly in
        Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

  \item Direct depletion inside the loop, with the full simulation per
        candidate~\cite{romano_2021_depletion}. This is the
        highest-fidelity route and the most expensive one, and it is
        the route that surrogate assistance makes affordable in the
        present work.
\end{enumerate}

Monte Carlo transport adds two problems of its own to the depletion
coupling. The statistical noise of the transport solution enters the
reaction rates and propagates from step to step. And the coupling
scheme itself can be numerically unstable. Explicit coupling schemes
develop spatial oscillations of the flux in cycle
calculations~\cite{dufek_hoogenboom_2009}, the predictor-corrector
schemes implemented in established codes can be unstable in large
systems~\cite{dufek_2013_pc}, and stable alternatives such as the
stochastic implicit Euler scheme~\cite{dufek_2013_sie} and higher-order
integrators~\cite{josey_2017} were developed in response. These results
ground the fixed transport fidelity and the burnup schedule adopted in
Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}.

\emph{Metaheuristics.} Genetic algorithms, NSGA-II, simulated annealing
and tabu search remain the reference engines because they search
discrete nonconvex spaces globally, handle constraints naturally and
return whole Pareto
fronts~\cite{deb_nsga2_2002,kropaczek_turinsky_1991}. Their cost is the
evaluation count. Hundreds to thousands of full simulations are
typical, which is prohibitive when every evaluation is a Monte Carlo
depletion, and no convergence guarantee exists~\cite{stewart_2021}.

\emph{Surrogate-assisted and Bayesian optimisation.} Surrogates can cut
the number of simulator calls by an order of magnitude, and Gaussian
processes add a calibrated uncertainty that supports active
learning~\cite{jin_2011,rasmussen_gp_2006,garnett_2023}. The price is
model risk. Accuracy degrades with dimension, the model must be managed
and retrained as the search advances, and noisy Monte Carlo objectives
complicate the fitting~\cite{jin_2011}. Published nuclear applications
rely mostly on neural network
surrogates~\cite{whyte_parks_2021,wan_2022,pevey_2022}, and a Gaussian
process pipeline for a core design problem of the present kind remains
comparatively unexplored, which is part of the opportunity this thesis
takes.

\emph{Deep reinforcement learning.} Reported results are strong,
including several times more feasible patterns than stochastic
optimisation~\cite{radaideh_2021_rl} and a larger hypervolume than
NSGA-III and multi-objective simulated annealing and tabu search on a
full-size PWR problem~\cite{seurin_2024_pearl}. The approach is
sample-hungry in training, complex to implement and tune, and its
advantages still rest on the reports of the developing
groups~\cite{seurin_2024_rl}.

\emph{Multi-fidelity frameworks.} Combining cheap screening with
selective high-fidelity confirmation is attractive whenever two model
levels exist~\cite{kennedy_ohagan_2000,forrester_2007,alexandrov_2001}.
The gains hinge on the correlation between the levels, and that
correlation weakens near the optimum. The present work therefore
measures the high-fidelity quantity in the loop where its cost permits,
rather than correcting a proxy.

The pipeline of this thesis sits in the surrogate-assisted family and
is assembled from the components above.
NSGA-II~\cite{deb_nsga2_2002}, implemented in
pymoo~\cite{blank_deb_2020}, searches a Gaussian process surrogate
ensemble~\cite{rasmussen_gp_2006} initialised by Latin hypercube
sampling~\cite{mckay_lhs_1979} and refined by hypervolume-guided active
learning~\cite{zitzler_thiele_1999}, following the dual-stage
active-learning architecture formulated in a Karlsruhe Institute of
Technology work proposal~\cite{kit_inr_proposal}. Every retained design
is scored by full OpenMC
depletion~\cite{romano_openmc_2015,romano_2021_depletion}, so the cycle
length objective is measured in effective full-power days rather than
proxied, and candidate designs are re-scored at core level to confront
the fidelity gap of Section~\ref{sec:lit-opt-difficulties} directly,
with the measured rank inversion reported in
Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

\section{Gadolinia burnable absorbers}
\label{sec:lit-gd}

Gadolinia owes its role to the extreme thermal absorption of $^{155}$Gd
and $^{157}$Gd, which renders poisoned pellets optically black and
makes the absorber strongly self-shielding. Suppression duration is
therefore governed by surface burn-off rather than bulk content, and
industrial practice concentrates the poison in a limited number of rods
precisely to exploit this effect~\cite{gd_enriched_2021}.

Operating experience is extensive, of the order of 45\,000 gadolinia
rods across 29 light-water reactors by the late
1980s~\cite{anf_gd_1987}, and includes concentrations up to 10\,wt\% in
commercial reloads with reduced uranium enrichment in the poisoned
rods~\cite{robinson_gd10}.

Two systematic effects recur across the literature and matter to the
present work.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Gadolinia rods tend to \emph{raise} the local peaking factor by
        depressing their own power and loading their
        neighbours~\cite{ap1000_gd_2022}.

  \item The end-of-life residual penalty of natural gadolinia is small,
        below $0.2\%\,\Delta k$ near 45\,GWd/MTU~\cite{ba_snf_2001}.
\end{enumerate}

Parametric studies quantify the count--concentration equivalence. In an
APR1400-type lattice, four rods at $\sim$3.3\,wt\% match eight rods at
$\sim$7.2\,wt\% for the same BOL
reactivity~\cite{apr1400_gd_2020}. Enriched-gadolinium variants extend
the design space further~\cite{gd_enriched_2021,ap1000_gd_2022}. A
parametric study of enriched gadolinium exists specifically for the
Angra-2 assembly~\cite{angra2_gd_2018}, the domestic heritage plant of
the present design basis.
