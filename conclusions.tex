\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

\section{What the study set out to do and what it found}
\label{sec:concl-answer}

The study asked which core designs a LABGENE-class pressurised water
reactor of \SI{48}{MW} thermal could adopt, when the only information
available is the open record, and it answered by building a
surrogate-assisted multi-objective optimisation around a Monte Carlo
model and running it through eight campaigns. Each campaign changed one
thing and measured its effect. The final campaign produced a Pareto
front of eight designs and a second set of four designs that answer a
different question, and the distinction between the two sets is the
main conclusion of the work.

Three findings carry the weight.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A derived bound is never a substitute for a measured
        constraint. The reactivity ceiling of Campaigns 1 to 6 was
        replaced in Campaign 7 by a directly measured controllability
        screen, and the comparison showed the derived ceiling
        rejecting fourteen designs whose own measured bank worth made
        them controllable (Section~\ref{sec:res-c7-screens}). Campaign
        8 reversed the nesting so that the measurement does the work
        and the bound is inert. The retrospective derivation also gave
        the historic ceiling of 1.35 its provenance, as the all-bank
        controllability bound of this core to within two per cent.

  \item The finite-height correction is a constant of the geometry.
        The axial leakage factor varies by \SI{160}{pcm} over a factor
        of two in excess reactivity, so a single value corrects the
        whole design space and every Campaign 8 cycle length refers to
        a finite-height core (Section~\ref{sec:meth-lax}).

  \item Cycle length is not the binding requirement of this core.
        Section~\ref{sec:concl-reform} states this finding and its
        consequence for the formulation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Cycle length is a requirement, not an objective}
\label{sec:concl-reform}

The optimisation was posed, from the first campaign, as the
maximisation of cycle length against the minimisation of the radial
peaking factor. That is the natural formulation for a naval core,
whose defining advantage is time between refuellings, and it was kept
through eight campaigns so that the campaigns remain comparable.
Campaign 8 shows that it is the wrong formulation for the final design
choice, for a reason that only became visible once controllability was
measured rather than assumed.

The open record gives the prototype a refuelling interval of four to
five years. Through Equation~\eqref{eq:mission-efpd} that interval
corresponds to a cycle length between about 900 and 1500 effective
full power days, depending on a capacity factor that no open source
states. Every design on the Campaign 8 front exceeds the upper end of
that range, the shortest by a third and the longest by a factor of
four (Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-mission}). The objective kept pushing
cycle length long after the requirement was met, and it could only do
so by raising the beginning-of-life excess reactivity, because a
longer cycle is bought with reactivity to be burned later.

That excess has to be held by something at the start of life. In this
model the soluble boron and the gadolinia are already present in every
eigenvalue, so what remains is held by the control rods. Every design
on the front needs the four regulating banks to hold between
\num{8930} and \SI{13627}{pcm} at power, which means banks inserted
deep into the core throughout early life. A pressurised water reactor
does not operate that way. Its regulating banks sit near the withdrawn
position at power and carry only the power defect and the manoeuvring
band, while the excess is held by boron adjustment and by burnable
absorber whose burnout tracks the fuel. The four Campaign 8 designs
that behave that way, controllable by two banks and holding \num{2564}
to \SI{4068}{pcm}, still deliver 1142 to 3381 effective full power
days, and the shortest of them meets five years at a capacity factor
of 0.8 with commercial-grade enrichment
(Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-split}).

The explanation is simple once the numbers are side by side. The
requirement on cycle length is a floor, not a summit. Anything above
the floor buys nothing that the mission needs and costs excess
reactivity that the control system must absorb, and absorbing it
raises the rodded peaking to the screening bound. The quantities that
do not have floors, that keep getting better the further one goes, are
the controllability margin and the peaking. Those are the objectives.
The cycle length belongs among the constraints, at the value the
mission requires.

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf{x}}\ &\big(F_{\Delta H}(\mathbf{x}),\ \ k_\mathrm{RE12}(\mathbf{x})\big) \\
\text{subject to}\ &\mathrm{EFPD}(\mathbf{x}) \ge \mathrm{EFPD}_\mathrm{req},\quad
g_j(\mathbf{x}) \le 0
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:concl-reform}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $F_{\Delta H}$ is the radial enthalpy-rise hot channel factor
        with all rods out, dimensionless, the first objective.

  \item $k_\mathrm{RE12}$ is the core eigenvalue with the first two
        regulating banks inserted, dimensionless, the second objective.
        Minimising it maximises the operating margin that the smallest
        practical rod insertion provides.

  \item $\mathrm{EFPD}_\mathrm{req}$ is the cycle length that meets the
        refuelling interval at the stated capacity factor, in effective
        full power days, entering as a constraint.

  \item $g_j$ are the remaining constraints of the final campaign, the
        reactivity floor, the enrichment cap, the vessel fit, the
        peaking screen and the four-bank control screen.
\end{itemize}

Two things follow from the reformulation that the present study can
already say. The Campaign 8 archive contains the designs this
formulation would select, since the two-bank subset is exactly the
region where $k_\mathrm{RE12}$ is smallest among feasible designs, and
the surrogate machinery needs no change because the two-bank eigenvalue
is already recorded on every evaluation. And the reformulation makes
the enrichment question answerable in the terms the open record poses
it. At the required cycle length, the enrichment is whatever the
controllability and peaking objectives leave, which on the Campaign 8
evidence is between about 3 and \SI{7}{wt\%} as a uniform assembly
value, the range the programme was reported to be considering in 2014.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:concl-limits}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The model is a two-dimensional beginning-of-life core in the
        optimisation loop, corrected for axial leakage by a measured
        constant. Axial power shape, axial rod worth curves and the
        three-dimensional peaking are confirmed on the candidates
        only.

  \item The control screen tests hold-down at power with the operating
        boron present and no xenon. It is not a shutdown-margin
        demonstration, which needs the cold state, all assemblies
        minus the highest-worth one, and a stated margin. The boron
        worth of the candidates is measured separately.

  \item The reactivity swing over the cycle is not penalised. The
        optimiser removed burnable absorber wherever it could, and the
        gadolinium burnout hump of up to \SI{6000}{pcm} seen in the
        depletion histories is absorbed by nothing in the objective set.
        In a borated core the boron letdown absorbs it. In a boron-free
        core it would have to be absorbed by rods.

  \item The statistical error on the peaking objective was not
        measured during the campaigns, only bounded, and the ordering of
        neighbouring front designs on that axis is not resolved.

  \item Every design below a pitch of \SI{1.2242}{cm} in Campaigns 1
        to 7 carried clipped guide tubes. The final campaign is free of
        the defect and the earlier results are caveated.

  \item Thermal-hydraulic feedback is absent. Temperatures are fixed at
        representative hot full power values, so no coefficient of
        reactivity, no departure from nucleate boiling ratio and no fuel
        temperature limit is evaluated.
\end{enumerate}

\label{sec:concl-future}

candidates in progress. Table~\ref{tab:concl-future} lists the

Three of these are already prepared. The boron worth measurement has
its script and its methodology block. The three-dimensional
confirmation runs on the water-slab axial model that corrected the
campaign target, with the hardware model as the next increment. And
the reformulated campaign needs no new evaluator, because every
quantity it optimises was recorded on every Campaign 8 evaluation.
