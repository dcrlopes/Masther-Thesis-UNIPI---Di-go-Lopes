\section{Purpose and reading key}
\label{app:inputs-purpose}

This annex lists every quantity required to rebuild the reactor model of
this thesis, together with the origin of that quantity and the place in
the text where it is used or checked.

The intent is that no number in the model is unaccounted for. A reader
who wants to reproduce the geometry, the materials, the operating point
or the numerical settings should need nothing beyond this annex and the
simulation repository.

Each entry carries an origin class. The classes are the following.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{OFF}. Stated in a document of the Marinha do Brasil, of
        the Brazilian government or of an international organisation.

  \item \textbf{LIT}. Stated in a peer-reviewed article, a laboratory
        report, a code manual or an institutional publication.

  \item \textbf{REP}. Reproduced from the model of another reactor,
        namely the NuScale-class benchmark adopted as the design analog.

  \item \textbf{INF}. Inferred from open information that does not state
        the quantity directly, for example a dimension read from a
        published drawing.

  \item \textbf{EST}. A physical or engineering estimate made in this
        work, with the reasoning given in the note attached to the row.

  \item \textbf{NUM}. A numerical or modelling device with no physical
        counterpart, for example a mesh size or a surface pad.

  \item \textbf{DER}. Derived by calculation from other entries of this
        annex.

  \item \textbf{VAR}. A design variable searched by the optimiser rather
        than a fixed input. Table~\ref{tab:in-freefixed} lists these.
\end{itemize}

The column \textbf{V} records whether the value was validated inside
this thesis. Its three states are the following.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Y}. The value was confirmed by an independent
        calculation or transport solve reported in the thesis.

  \item \textbf{P}. The value was exercised by a check that constrains
        it without confirming it on its own, for example a
        three-dimensional confirmation that varies it as a flag.

  \item \textbf{N}. The value was adopted as given and was not checked
        independently.
\end{itemize}

Two things are deliberately outside this annex.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The intervals swept in the sensitivity studies. Those intervals
        belong to the methodology and to the results of each study, not
        to the traceability of the base model, and they are reported
        with their own justification where each study is presented.

  \item The design-variable bounds of each campaign. These define the
        search space rather than the model, and they are given in the
        design-space table of Section~\ref{sec:meth-c8-space}.
        Table~\ref{tab:in-freefixed} of this annex states only which
        quantities are fixed and which are searched.
\end{enumerate}

All the values below were read from the simulation repository on the
branch \texttt{main}, which is the single branch carried forward after
every campaign branch was merged. The files are
\texttt{reactor\_model.py} for the geometry and the materials,
\texttt{core\_geometry.py} for the core envelope,
\texttt{zoning.py} and \texttt{leu\_policy.py} for the loading map and
the enrichment policy, \texttt{openmc\_evaluator.py} for the depletion
schedule and the transport settings, \texttt{run\_optimization.py} for
the campaign limits passed at launch, and \texttt{hardware3d.py} for the
three-dimensional confirmation model.

\section{Vessel, core envelope and reflector}
\label{app:inputs-vessel}

The radial budget of the design is set by the vessel inner radius. The
fuel footprint is bounded by the circumscribed envelope radius, which is
the smallest cylinder that contains every assembly intact.

\begin{equation}
R_\mathrm{env}(p) = \sqrt{13}\; N_\mathrm{lat}\, p
\label{eq:in-renv}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $R_\mathrm{env}$ is the circumscribed radius of the intact
        32-assembly footprint, in cm.
  \item $N_\mathrm{lat}$ is the lattice order of one assembly, equal to
        17, dimensionless.
  \item $p$ is the fuel rod pitch, in cm.
  \item $\sqrt{13}$ is the distance in assembly pitches from the core
        centre to the farthest fuel corner of the six-by-six map with
        the four corners removed, dimensionless.
\end{itemize}

The vessel-fit constraint follows directly.

\begin{equation}
g_\mathrm{geom} = R_\mathrm{env}(p) + \delta_\mathrm{pad}
+ t_\mathrm{refl} - \left( R_\mathrm{ves} - c_\mathrm{ves} \right)
\label{eq:in-ggeom}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $g_\mathrm{geom}$ is the vessel-fit constraint value, feasible
        when non-positive, in cm.
  \item $\delta_\mathrm{pad}$ is the lattice-corner pad, in cm.
  \item $t_\mathrm{refl}$ is the minimum radial reflector thickness, in
        cm.
  \item $R_\mathrm{ves}$ is the vessel inner radius, in cm.
  \item $c_\mathrm{ves}$ is the radial clearance reserved for the core
        barrel and the downcomer, in cm.
\end{itemize}

        future change of layout cannot invalidate it silently.

  \item The height is an estimate. It is read from the proportions of
        the vessel drawing and cross-checked against the heavy-metal
        mass of Table~\ref{tab:in-operating}. In the two-dimensional
        transport model the height enters only the heavy-metal and
        specific-power bookkeeping, so it changes the specific power and
        the cycle length in days but no multiplication factor.

  \item No open source specifies the reflector or the core barrel of the
        prototype. A steel reflector rather than a water pond is adopted
        because it is the practice of compact naval and integral cores
        and because it is what the benchmark uses.

  \item The benchmark value is 0.956. The value adopted here is
        deliberately more moderating, which is the conservative
        direction for reflector return. The three-dimensional
        confirmation exposes it as a flag so the difference can be
        quantified rather than assumed away.
\end{enumerate}

        placement, namely the optimised 16, 20 and 24 pin maps of
        \cite{bae_gd_2023}, the central-zone placement of
        \cite{epr_fsar_ch4} and the NuFuel-HTP2 patterns of
        \cite{nuscale_htp2_2017}.

  \item The grid model is used only in the three-dimensional
        confirmation. The benchmark places five grids over a 200\,cm
        active height. This work places four over 120\,cm at the same
        band height, with one of them inside the plenum, where it is
        smeared at its exact area fraction because the plenum has no pin
        structure.

  \item See note~(d). The same number sets the pitch below which the
        guide tube is clipped.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Material compositions and densities}
\label{app:inputs-materials}

The uranium isotopics are set explicitly rather than through the OpenMC
enrichment shortcut, because that shortcut assumes a fixed uranium-234 to
uranium-235 mass ratio that is documented only below 5\,wt\%. The
correlation adopted is the one used by the SCALE sequences.

\begin{equation}
w_{234} = 0.007731 \, \left( w_{235} \right)^{1.0837}
\label{eq:in-u234}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $w_{234}$ is the uranium-234 content of the uranium, in weight
        per cent.
  \item $w_{235}$ is the uranium-235 enrichment of the uranium, in
        weight per cent.
\end{itemize}

Uranium-236 is set to zero, because the fuel is modelled as fresh
non-recycled commercial uranium, and uranium-238 takes the balance. The
range over which Equation~\eqref{eq:in-u234} is applied is discussed in
Section~\ref{app:inputs-decisions}.

        plain pellet, which reflects the lower sintered density of the

        heavy-metal bookkeeping only through the plain pellet, because
        the specific power is computed on the unpoisoned pellet density.

        sequences. Its published validity range must be read together
        with the enrichment explored here, see
        Section~\ref{app:inputs-decisions}.

  \item The gadolinia is modelled with natural gadolinium. No isotopic
        tailoring is applied.

  \item The enrichment of a gadolinia-bearing rod is the enrichment of
        the zone it sits in, reduced by five per cent of its own value
        for every weight per cent of Gd$_2$O$_3$. The rule is the
        standard practice reported for the low-concentration gadolinia
        design of the Spanish three-loop 17 by 17 fuelled units, where
        the reduction offsets the degraded thermal conductivity of the
        urania-gadolinia mixture.

  \item The compositions are those coded in the model. They were not
        checked against a compendium of material compositions for
        radiation transport. See the open point in
        Section~\ref{app:inputs-openpoints}.

  \item The value corresponds to compressed light water at the operating
        pressure and moderator temperature of
        Table~\ref{tab:in-operating}. The pressure itself is not an
        input of the neutronic model. It enters only through this
        density, which is the honest statement of how the operating
        pressure acts on the results.

  \item The boron is added as natural boron at the stated mass fraction
        of the water. The boron worth of the candidate designs was
        measured directly in Section~\ref{sec:res-c8-boron}, which
        validates the implementation.

  \item The absorber, cladding and helium number densities are
        reproduced from the benchmark scripts and each material density
        is set by their sum. They were not re-derived here.

  \item The library and the chain are the ones distributed for OpenMC
        and are the same pair used by the benchmark, which is what makes
        the comparison with the benchmark meaningful.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Operating conditions and power bookkeeping}
\label{app:inputs-operating}

The specific power fed to the depletion follows from the thermal power
and the fresh heavy-metal mass of the core.

\begin{equation}
q = \frac{P_\mathrm{th}}{m_\mathrm{HM}}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
m_\mathrm{HM} = \pi r_\mathrm{f}^{2} H \rho_\mathrm{UO_2} f_\mathrm{HM}
\, n_\mathrm{rod} \, n_\mathrm{asm}
\label{eq:in-hm}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the symbols have the following meaning.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $q$ is the core specific power, in W per gram of heavy metal.
  \item $P_\mathrm{th}$ is the core thermal power, in W.
  \item $m_\mathrm{HM}$ is the fresh heavy-metal mass of the core, in g.
  \item $r_\mathrm{f}$ is the fuel pellet outer radius, in cm.

\section{Known departures from the reference benchmark}
\label{app:inputs-departures}

The design analog is a published benchmark of a NuScale-class integral
reactor. The model of this thesis departs from it in the following
respects, each of which is stated rather than absorbed silently.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The active height is 120\,cm against 200\,cm in the benchmark,
        which follows from the vessel envelope of the prototype.

  \item The reflector steel volume fraction is 0.90 against 0.956 in the
        benchmark. The three-dimensional confirmation exposes this as a
        flag.

  \item The two-dimensional model uses a single absorber radius of
        0.4331\,cm for both absorbers, where the benchmark uses
        0.4229\,cm for boron carbide and 0.4267\,cm for the
        silver-indium-cadmium alloy. The three-dimensional confirmation
        exposes this as a flag.

  \item The coolant density is 0.72\,g/cm$^3$ against the 0.752\,g/cm$^3$
        implied by the benchmark number densities, and the moderator
        temperature is 580\,K against 600\,K. The two choices are paired
        and are discussed in Section~\ref{app:inputs-decisions}.

  \item The spacer grid model has four grids over the shorter active
        height against five over the benchmark height, and one of the
        four sits in the plenum.

  \item The instrumentation tube is modelled with guide tube dimensions
        and carries no absorber rod.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Decisions recorded against these inputs}
\label{app:inputs-decisions}

Four inputs of the preceding tables raise a question that a reader will
ask. Each was examined and closed with an explicit decision rather than
left as an open point. The decisions and their reasoning are recorded
here so that they are not reopened.

\subsection*{Use of the uranium-234 correlation above its documented
range}

Equation~\eqref{eq:in-u234} is documented for uranium-235 enrichments
below 10\,wt\%. The enrichment search bound of the final campaign is
13.913\,wt\% at the design variable and the as-built peripheral ring
reaches 16\,wt\%, so the correlation is applied beyond its stated range.

The correlation is retained deliberately. Three arguments support the
decision.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The candidate designs selected from the final campaign carry
        enrichments below 10\,wt\%, so the correlation is used inside
        its documented range for every design that reaches the
        post-optimisation analysis.

  \item Uranium-234 is a strong thermal absorber, so an extrapolation
        that overstates its content understates the multiplication
        factor. The direction of the error is known and it is
        conservative for a design that must be shown to reach
        criticality.

  \item A design driven above 10\,wt\% is already penalised by the
        controllability screen, which is the binding constraint of this
        core. Correcting the isotopic vector at high enrichment would
        not move such a design into the candidate set.
\end{enumerate}

Two caveats are attached to the decision.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The peripheral multiplier of 1.150 means the as-built enrichment
        of the outer ring is higher than the design variable, so the
        check on whether a candidate stays below 10\,wt\% must be made
        on the as-built peripheral enrichment and not on the variable.

  \item The docstring of \texttt{make\_uo2} claims validity over the
        whole 2 to 19.75\,wt\% range. The cited source does not support
        that claim, and the text of this thesis states the limitation
        rather than repeating the docstring.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Moderator temperature of 580\,K against 600\,K in the
benchmark}

The moderator temperature is a hardcoded field of the
\texttt{Operating} dataclass, in the file
\texttt{reactor\_\allowbreak model.py}. It is not an OpenMC default.

The value is paired consistently with the coolant density of
0.72\,g/cm$^3$ of Table~\ref{tab:in-materials}, so the model is
internally coherent even though it sits 20\,K below the benchmark.

The choice does not threaten the Pareto front. The temperature is
identical for every design of every campaign and the rod pitch is fixed,
so the effect on the multiplication factor is a common-mode bias. A bias
that shifts every design by the same amount does not reorder a Pareto
front, and the front of this thesis is built by ranking designs against
one another rather than against an absolute reactivity target.

\subsection*{Origin of the single absorber radius of 0.4331\,cm}

The value was adopted from the benchmark notebook at the initialisation
of the model, before the two absorber materials were separated in the
three-dimensional hardware description. It has carried an unresolved
verification marker in the source code since then, and it is listed as a
departure in Section~\ref{app:inputs-departures} rather than presented
as a verified input.

The consequence is bounded. The single radius sits between the two
benchmark radii, and the three-dimensional confirmation exposes the
choice as a flag, so the effect on the bank worths can be measured
rather than argued.

\subsection*{Attribution of the gadolinia rules}

The enrichment derate of a gadolinia-bearing rod is attributed to the
ENUSA low-concentration gadolinia design report, which states the five
per cent relative reduction per weight per cent of Gd$_2$O$_3$ and
attributes it to the degraded thermal conductivity of the mixture.

The placement rules of the nested ladder are attributed to the optimised
gadolinia pin maps of the Oak Ridge study and to the central-zone
placement practice of the U.S. EPR safety analysis report. A further
Idaho National Laboratory uprate assessment cited in an early source
comment is redundant with these two and is not carried into the thesis.
